\title{A semi-implicit finite volume scheme for the six-equation two-phase model with mechanical, thermal and chemical relaxation at all Mach numbers}
\begin{abstract}
We present a second-order semi-implicit IMEX finite volume scheme for the single-velocity six-equation two-phase model
with total energy and mechanical, thermal and chemical relaxation, for stiffened-gas, Noble--Abel stiffened-gas and
Mie--Gr\"uneisen equations of state. The two stiffnesses of the model, the acoustic waves at low Mach numbers and the
relaxation processes up to infinite rates, are treated in a single implicit step: the pressure work and the three
relaxation processes, each at a finite or infinite rate, a
slower process being active only when the faster ones are instantaneous, enter one local closure per cell that reduces
to a scalar equation for the cell pressure, so that each stage requires the solution of a mildly nonlinear system for
the cell pressures, solved by Newton's method. The time step is restricted by the flow velocity only, independently of
the Mach number and of the relaxation rates, and the scheme converges to the corresponding equilibrium schemes as the
relaxation rates tend to infinity and is formally asymptotic preserving in the low Mach number limit. At shocks, the
defect between the conservative and the non-conservative mixture internal energy is distributed between the phases
according to the shock relations of the model, with a share that is unique for weak shocks and computed by shooting
for strong ones, without calibrated heat exchange. The fluxes are split in the manner of Zha and Bilgen: all
quantities, including the volume fraction, are transported explicitly by the path-conservative Rusanov scheme with the
material speed as dissipation speed, and the implicit step carries the pressure, its work on each phase and an
incremental flux of the conservative transported quantities, with implicit Laplace dissipation of the pressure and the
velocity that vanishes in the low Mach number regime. Second order in time is obtained with a stiffly accurate,
L-stable IMEX Runge--Kutta scheme. We prove that the scheme preserves moving contacts. Numerical results in one and two
space dimensions, from the incompressible limit to strong shocks, cavitation and phase transition, confirm the accuracy
and robustness of the method.
\end{abstract}
