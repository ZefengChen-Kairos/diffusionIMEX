\title{A semi-implicit finite volume scheme for the six-equation two-phase model with mechanical, thermal and chemical relaxation at all Mach numbers}
\begin{abstract}
We present a second-order semi-implicit IMEX finite volume scheme for the single-velocity six-equation two-phase model
with total energy and mechanical, thermal and chemical relaxation, for stiffened-gas, Noble--Abel stiffened-gas and
Mie--Gr\"uneisen equations of state. Following Toro and V\'azquez-Cend\'on, the fluxes are split into an advective part,
in which all phase quantities are transported explicitly with a Rusanov flux whose dissipation speed is the material
speed and the volume fraction is discretised by the path-conservative Rusanov scheme, and a pressure part, which carries
the pressure, its work on each phase and an incremental flux of the transported quantities and is treated implicitly
together with the relaxation terms. The time step is restricted by the flow velocity only, independently of the Mach
number and of the relaxation rates. The implicit pressure work and the three relaxation processes, at finite or infinite
rates, enter a single local closure that reduces to one scalar equation for the cell pressure, so that each stage
requires the solution of a mildly nonlinear system for the cell pressures, solved by Newton's method. The defect between
the conservative and the non-conservative mixture internal energy is distributed between the phases according to the
shock relations of the model, without calibrated heat exchange, and the acoustic dissipation missing from the central
implicit operator is supplied at the faces from the acoustic Riemann solution. Second order in time is obtained with a
stiffly accurate, L-stable IMEX Runge--Kutta scheme. We prove that the scheme preserves moving contacts, that the transported
phase states remain admissible under a face condition independent of the phase contents, and that it converges to the
corresponding equilibrium schemes as the relaxation rates tend to infinity; formally, it is asymptotic preserving in the
low Mach number limit. Numerical results in one and two space dimensions, from the incompressible limit to strong
shocks, cavitation and phase transition, confirm the accuracy and robustness of the method.
\end{abstract}
