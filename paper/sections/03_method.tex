\section{Semi-implicit numerical scheme}
\label{sec:method}

We now describe the semi-implicit scheme. After introducing the notation, we present the explicit update of the
advective sub-system (Sec.~\ref{sec:advective}), the implicit update of the pressure--relaxation sub-system with its
local closure and relaxation closures (Sec.~\ref{sec:implicit}), and the face velocity and face pressure that carry the
acoustic dissipation (Sec.~\ref{sec:dissipation}). The properties of the scheme are proved in
Secs.~\ref{sec:positivity}--\ref{sec:ap}; second order in time is obtained in Sec.~\ref{sec:time}, and
Sec.~\ref{sec:fully} collects the fully discrete scheme in two space dimensions.

The computational domain $\Omega=[x_{\min},x_{\max}]\times[y_{\min},y_{\max}]$ is covered by a uniform Cartesian grid of
$N_x\times N_y$ control volumes with spacings $\Delta x=(x_{\max}-x_{\min})/N_x$ and $\Delta y=(y_{\max}-y_{\min})/N_y$. The cell $(i,j)$ has
centre $\mathbf x_{i,j}=(x_i,y_j)$, its faces are denoted by $(i\pm\tfrac12,j)$ and $(i,j\pm\tfrac12)$ and its corners by
$(i\pm\tfrac12,j\pm\tfrac12)$. All variables are cell averages, defined at the cell centres for both sub-systems; time levels
are denoted by superscripts and the face differences by $\delta_x[F]_{i,j}=F_{i+1/2,j}-F_{i-1/2,j}$,
$\delta_y[G]_{i,j}=G_{i,j+1/2}-G_{i,j-1/2}$. Where the $y$-direction is analogous it is omitted.

\subsection{Advective sub-system}
\label{sec:advective}
The advective sub-system \eqref{eq:advective} is discretised by an explicit conservative finite volume update of all
transported quantities,
\begin{equation}
X^*_{i,j}=X^n_{i,j}-\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x}\,\delta_x\big[F^e[X]\big]_{i,j}-\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta y}\,\delta_y\big[G^e[X]\big]_{i,j},\qquad
X\in\{\alpha_1,\alpha_2,m_1,m_2,\varepsilon_1,\varepsilon_2,\rho u,\rho v,E\},
\label{eq:explicit}
\end{equation}
with the Rusanov flux \cite{Rusanov1961}
\begin{equation}
F^e_{i+1/2,j}[X]=\tfrac12\big(X^Lu^L+X^Ru^R\big)-\tfrac12a_{i+1/2,j}\big(X^R-X^L\big),\qquad a_{i+1/2,j}=\max\big(|u^L|,|u^R|\big),
\label{eq:rusanov}
\end{equation}
or $a$ equal to the maximum of $|u^L|$, $|u^R|$ over all faces of the same direction. The dissipation uses only the eigenvalue $u$ of the advective
sub-system, so that it is proportional to the flow velocity. The face values $X^{L,R}$ are MUSCL reconstructions
\cite{vanLeer1979} with minmod. The volume-transported quantities $\mathcal X_V=(\alpha_1,m_1,m_2,\varepsilon_1,\varepsilon_2)$ share
one limiter,
\begin{equation}
X^L_{i+1/2}=X_i+\tfrac12\sigma_i,\quad X^R_{i+1/2}=X_{i+1}-\tfrac12\sigma_{i+1},\qquad \sigma_i=\phi_i\,\tfrac12(X_{i+1}-X_{i-1}),\qquad
\phi_i=\min_{X\in\mathcal X_V}\frac{\mathrm{minmod}(X_i-X_{i-1},X_{i+1}-X_i)}{\tfrac12(X_{i+1}-X_{i-1})}\in[0,1],
\label{eq:shared_limiter}
\end{equation}
so that linear relations between them, in particular $\varepsilon_k=c_k(p)\alpha_k+\big(q_k-b_kc_k(p)\big)m_k$, $c_k(p)=(p+\gamma_k\pi_k)/(\gamma_k-1)$, at
uniform pressure, hold on the faces; quantities that are constant on the stencil to round-off are excluded from the minimum. The velocity is
reconstructed component-wise with its own minmod slope, the face momentum and energy are assembled from the face
values, and the face value of $\alpha_2$ is $1-\alpha_1^{\rm face}$. Both the central part and the dissipation of
\eqref{eq:rusanov} use the reconstructed values.

The time step is given by the CFL condition of the advective sub-system,
\begin{equation}
\Delta t=C\left(\frac{\max_\Omega|u|}{\Delta x}+\frac{\max_\Omega|v|}{\Delta y}\right)^{-1},
\label{eq:cfl}
\end{equation}
which depends on the flow velocity only; $C=0.2$ unless stated otherwise, and the first steps are limited by the
start-up rule of Sec.~\ref{sec:time}. A proof of stability under \eqref{eq:cfl} is not available, as for the
other semi-implicit schemes of this class; all computations of Sec.~\ref{sec:results} are advanced with it.

\subsection{Pressure--relaxation sub-system}
\label{sec:implicit}
Having evolved the advective sub-system, we now consider the pressure--relaxation sub-system, in which all remaining
contributions are treated implicitly. They are solved together, within a single step, in the following sequence,
repeated at every iteration of the pressure solver:
\begin{enumerate}
\item for given cell pressures, the face velocities give the incremental fluxes, the transported phase states, the
volume strain and the conservative updates of momentum and total energy;
\item a local closure in every cell, containing the pressure work and the three relaxation processes, returns the cell
pressure and the phase states consistent with them;
\item the pressure system, which requires the closure pressure to equal the cell pressure, is solved by Newton's method.
\end{enumerate}
This section derives the implicit step in semi-discrete form; the fully discrete counterpart is given in
Sec.~\ref{sec:fully}.

\paragraph{Semi-discrete implicit step}
The pressure--relaxation sub-system \eqref{eq:pressure_sub} is discretised implicitly. Following the splitting of
Sec.~\ref{sec:split}, the flux of every transported quantity at the new time level is written as
$Xu^{n+1}=Xu^n+X(u^{n+1}-u^n)$: the first part is the explicit flux of Sec.~\ref{sec:advective}, the second, the incremental
flux, is implicit. The volumes, partial densities and phase energies carry the same incremental flux, so that
transport does not change the phase densities and specific energies; the momentum and kinetic-energy increments follow
from the incremental mass flux. The non-conservative transport of the volume
fraction is written in volume-transport form, $u\,\partial_x\alpha_k=\partial_x(\alpha_ku)-\alpha_k\partial_xu$: with the linear path, the
path-conservative fluctuation is a conservative volume flux, treated like the fluxes of $m_k$ and $\varepsilon_k$, plus the volume
strain $-\alpha_k\partial_xu$, treated implicitly together with the pressure work. In semi-discrete form,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
X^{\rm tr}&=X^*-\Delta t\,\partial_x\big(X^{\rm face}(u^{n+1}-u^n)\big),\qquad X\in\{\alpha_k,m_k,\varepsilon_k\},\\
(\rho u)^{n+1}&=(\rho u)^*-\Delta t\,\partial_x\bar p^{\,n+1}-\Delta t\,\partial_x\big(u^{n+1}\hat M\big),\\
E^{n+1}&=E^*-\Delta t\,\partial_x\Big(\textstyle\sum_k\varepsilon^{\rm face}_k(u^{n+1}-u^n)+\tfrac12(u^{n+1})^2\hat M+\bar p^{\,n+1}u^{n+1}\Big),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:semidiscrete}
\end{equation}
where $\hat M=\sum_km^{\rm face}_k(u^{n+1}-u^n)$ is the incremental mass flux and the face values are taken from the explicit
state. The phase states at $t^{n+1}$ follow from the transported states, the volume
strain $s=\Delta t\,\partial_xu^{n+1}=1-\sum_k\alpha^{\rm tr}_k$ and the conservative mixture internal energy
$(\rho e)^C=E^{n+1}-\tfrac12(\rho u)^{n+1\,2}/\rho^{n+1}$ through a local closure (Sec.~\ref{sec:closure}). The momentum and energy updates depend on $\bar p^{\,n+1}$, and the closure returns $\bar p^{\,n+1}$ as a function of
$s$ and $(\rho e)^C$, so the implicit step reduces to one equation per cell for the pressure,
\begin{equation}
G(\bar p)\equiv\bar p^{\rm cl}\big(s(\bar p),X^{\rm tr}(\bar p),(\rho e)^C(\bar p);\ \mu,\theta,\nu\big)-\bar p=0 ,
\label{eq:global_semi}
\end{equation}
a nonlinear and relaxing generalisation of the pressure wave equation $p^{n+1}=p^*-\Delta t\,\rho c^2\partial_xu^{n+1}$.

\paragraph{Derivation of the pressure wave equation}
The structure of \eqref{eq:global_semi} is seen by linearising it. To first order in $\Delta t$ the closure gives the cell
pressure as the pressure $P^*$ of the explicit state minus the response of the mixture to the volume strain,
\begin{equation}
\bar p^{\,n+1}=P^*-K^W s,\qquad s=\Delta t\,\partial_xu^{n+1},\qquad K^W=\rho c_p^2 ,
\label{eq:p_linear}
\end{equation}
with the Wood bulk modulus of the mixture at mechanical equilibrium ($K^W$ is replaced by the stiffness of the activated
closure). The momentum update of \eqref{eq:semidiscrete}, without the incremental term, provides the missing link,
\begin{equation}
u^{n+1}=u^*-\frac{\Delta t}{\rho}\,\partial_x\bar p^{\,n+1},
\label{eq:u_update}
\end{equation}
and inserting it into \eqref{eq:p_linear} eliminates the velocity, giving an elliptic equation for the pressure,
\begin{equation}
\bar p^{\,n+1}-\Delta t^2K^W\,\partial_x\Big(\frac1\rho\,\partial_x\bar p^{\,n+1}\Big)=P^*-\Delta t\,K^W\,\partial_xu^* .
\label{eq:wave}
\end{equation}
This is the pressure equation of semi-implicit schemes for single-phase flows \cite{DumbserCasulli2016,BoscheriPareschi2021} and of
the seven-equation scheme of \cite{BattistiBoscheri2025}, with the stiffness of the two-phase mixture. The scheme does not
use the linearisation: the closure evaluates the pressure response exactly for the equations of state and the
relaxation rates, and also contains the incremental fluxes and the distribution of the energy defect, so that the
discrete system \eqref{eq:global_semi} keeps the structure of \eqref{eq:wave}, a nonlinearity local to each cell together
with a linear coupling through the face velocities, without its linearisation errors.

\begin{remark}[Mixture-energy consistency]
The closure distributes the difference between the non-conservative and the conservative mixture internal
energy to the phases (Sec.~\ref{sec:closure}), so that $\sum_k\varepsilon_k^{n+1}=E^{n+1}-\tfrac12\rho^{n+1}(u^{n+1})^2$ holds exactly and the
pressure is given by the phase equations of state; no linearisation of the kinetic energy is involved.
\end{remark}

\subsubsection{Local closure}
\label{sec:closure}
Backward Euler of the implicit part of \eqref{eq:model}, with the strain shared between the phases by the frozen
fractions $\omega_k=\alpha^{\rm tr}_k/\sum_j\alpha^{\rm tr}_j$ in both the volume change and the pressure work, gives
($\chi_1=+1$, $\chi_2=-1$, $\tau$ the implicit step)
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\alpha_k&=\alpha^{\rm tr}_k+\omega_ks+\chi_kR,\qquad R=\tau\big(\mu(p_1-p_2)+\mathcal M/\rho_I\big),\\
m_k&=m^{\rm tr}_k+\chi_k\tau\mathcal M,\\
\varepsilon_k&=\varepsilon^{\rm tr}_k-\omega_kp_k\,s-\chi_k\,\tau p_I\mu(p_1-p_2)+\chi_k\tau\big(\mathcal Q+e_I\mathcal M\big)+\Lambda_k(\delta),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:gibbs}
\end{equation}
so $\sum_k\alpha_k=1$ for every pressure. The last term distributes the defect between the non-conservative and the
conservative mixture internal energy,
\begin{equation}
\delta=(\rho e)^C-\sum_k\varepsilon^{\rm tr}_k+s\sum_k\omega_kp_k,\qquad \sum_k\Lambda_k(\delta)=\delta .
\label{eq:defect}
\end{equation}
At a shock $\delta$ is the dissipation of the scheme, and its partition fixes the limit shock relations. We distribute
it so that each phase follows its own Hugoniot \cite{SaurelGavrilyukRenaud2003,SPB2009},
\begin{equation}
e_k-e_{k0}+\tfrac12(p_0+p_1)(v_k-v_{k0})=0 ,
\label{eq:II5}
\end{equation}
the shock relations also used for the exact solutions of Sec.~\ref{sec:results}. The physical part is the
Hugoniot--isentrope energy gap of each phase between the extrema $p_{\rm lo}<p_{\rm hi}$ of the mixture-equilibrium pressure
of $X^*$ on the compact stencil ($\pm1$; the five-point cross in 2D) of a compressing cell, i.e.\ a cell where the central
difference of $u^*$ (and $v^*$) has negative sum,
\begin{equation}
Q_k=m_k\,(v^{\rm pre}_k-b_k)\,\frac{(p_{\rm hi}+\gamma_k\pi_k)(\tilde v_H-\tilde v_S)}{\gamma_k-1},\qquad
\tilde v_H=\frac{(\gamma_k-1)x_{\rm hi}+(\gamma_k+1)x_{\rm lo}}{(\gamma_k+1)x_{\rm hi}+(\gamma_k-1)x_{\rm lo}},\quad
\tilde v_S=\Big(\frac{x_{\rm lo}}{x_{\rm hi}}\Big)^{1/\gamma_k},
\label{eq:Qk}
\end{equation}
$x=p+\pi_k$, with $m_k$ of the cell itself, $v^{\rm pre}_k=\alpha_k/m_k$ from the lowest-pressure cell of the stencil, and $Q_k=0$ in
other cells; $Q_k$ is computed once per stage on $X^*$ and frozen. Its
share is consistent with \eqref{eq:II5} for weak shocks: under \eqref{eq:II5} the heat received by phase $k$ on a shock
profile is its compression work minus its Hugoniot work, $Y_kq_k=\int(p-\bar p)\,\mathrm dv_k$, $\bar p=p_0+\tfrac12\Delta p$; for
$\Delta p\to0$ each phase is isentropic along the profile to the required order,
$v_k(p)=v_{k0}[1-x/K_k+\mathcal G_kx^2/K_k^2+O(x^3)]$, $x=p-p_0$, $K_k=\rho_kc_k^2$, $\mathcal G_k=\tfrac12(1+\partial K_k/\partial p|_s)$, and
\[
Y_k\rho\,q_k=\frac{\alpha_k\mathcal G_k}{6K_k^2}\,\Delta p^3+O(\Delta p^4),
\]
the entropy production of a single-phase shock; $Q_k/\sum_jQ_j$ reduces to this share as $p_{\rm hi}\to p_{\rm lo}$. The
remainder of $\delta$ is a discretisation error, distributed in proportion to the energy of each phase above its
admissibility bound,
\begin{equation}
\lambda^E_k=\frac{E_k}{\sum_jE_j},\qquad E_k=\frac{(p_k+\pi_k)(\alpha_k-m_kb_k)}{\gamma_k-1}\Big|_{\rm tr} .
\label{eq:lambdaE}
\end{equation}
It is the only linear split that keeps both phases admissible for every admissible $\delta$: with $\Lambda_k=\lambda_k\delta$,
$\sum_k\lambda_k=1$, the requirement $E_k+\lambda_k\delta>0$ for $\delta\to+\infty$ gives $\lambda_k\ge0$, and for $\delta\to-\sum_jE_j$
gives $\lambda_k\le E_k/\sum_jE_j$; summing over $k$, both sides equal one, so $\lambda_k=E_k/\sum_jE_j$. Altogether
\begin{equation}
\Lambda_k(\delta)=\begin{cases}\min(\delta,Q)\,\dfrac{Q_k}{Q}+\big(\delta-\min(\delta,Q)\big)\lambda^E_k,&\delta\ge0,\\[4pt]
\delta\,\lambda^E_k,&\delta<0,\end{cases}\qquad Q=\sum_kQ_k ,
\label{eq:Lambda}
\end{equation}
a continuous, piecewise affine function of $\delta$; with a common pressure $\delta=D_0+ps$, $D_0=(\rho e)^C-\sum_k\varepsilon^{\rm tr}_k$.
The NASG $p$ closure selects the active piece at every trial pressure of its root solve; the other closures that use
$\Lambda_k$ (finite $\mu$ and $\theta$, Mie--Gr\"uneisen phases) select it at the current Newton iterate of the cell pressure. For Mie--Gr\"uneisen phases $Q_k$ and $E_k$ follow from the Hugoniot, the isentrope
$(p-p_r)v^{\Gamma+1}={\rm const}$ and the thermal energy above the cold curve.

\subsubsection{Relaxation closures}
\label{sec:relaxation}
The relaxation terms are not split from the transport: they enter the closure \eqref{eq:gibbs}, which for each set of
rates reduces to one scalar equation in the cell pressure. We follow the ordering of Pelanti \cite{Pelanti2022}
($\mu=\infty$ if $\theta>0$ or $\nu>0$; $\theta=\infty$ if $\nu>0$); on the active piece $\Lambda_k=\lambda_k\delta+\xi_k$.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{$p$ closure} ($\mu=\infty$, $\theta=\nu=0$). With $p_1=p_2=p$, \eqref{eq:gibbs} becomes the discrete Gibbs relation
$\varepsilon_k=\varepsilon^{\rm tr}_k-p(\alpha_k-\alpha^{\rm tr}_k)+\Lambda_k$, and by \eqref{eq:eps_nasg}
\[
\alpha_k(p)=\frac{(\gamma_k-1)\big[A_k+B_kp\big]+(p+\gamma_k\pi_k)m_kb_k}{\gamma_k(p+\pi_k)},\quad
A_k=\varepsilon^{\rm tr}_k-m_kq_k+\lambda_kD_0+\xi_k,\quad B_k=\alpha^{\rm tr}_k+\lambda_ks .
\]
$\Phi(p)=\sum_k\alpha_k(p)-1$ tends to $+\infty$ as $p\downarrow\max_k(-\pi_k)$ and is negative for $p\to\infty$; it is decreasing, and the root
unique, when $N_k=A_k-(B_k-m_kb_k)\pi_k>0$ for both phases. In general the root is bracketed on $(\max_k(-\pi_k),\infty)$ and
found by a safeguarded Newton iteration, and admissibility is checked at the solution. For Mie--Gr\"uneisen phases $\alpha_k(p)$ is the unique root of a monotone scalar equation; the outer
equation $\Phi(p)=0$ is then solved by a bracketed Newton iteration, without a global monotonicity result.
\item \emph{Finite $\mu$}. The impedances in $p_I$ are frozen on $X^*$. For given $R$, \eqref{eq:gibbs} is a $2\times2$ linear
system for $(p_1,p_2)$, with $\delta=D_0+s\sum_k\omega_kp_k$; the scalar equation $R-\tau\mu\big(p_1(R)-p_2(R)\big)=0$ is increasing in $R$
and its root lies between $0$ and $\tau\mu(p_1-p_2)|_{R=0}$; $\bar p^{\rm cl}=\alpha_1p_1+\alpha_2p_2$.
\item \emph{$pT$ closure} ($\mu=\theta=\infty$). $T(p)$ follows explicitly from $\sum_km_ke_k(p,T)=(\rho e)^C$, and
$\Phi(p)=\sum_km_kv_k(p,T(p))-1$.
\item \emph{Finite $\theta$}. With $\alpha^0_k(p)$, $T^0_k(p)$ the volumes and temperatures of the $p$ closure at $p$, the heat
$\Delta Q$ added to phase 1 changes the volumes by $\alpha_k=\alpha^0_k+\chi_k(\gamma_k-1)\Delta Q/\big(\gamma_k(p+\pi_k)\big)$ and the temperatures by
$\chi_k\Delta Q/C_{pk}$, $C_{pk}=\gamma_kc_{vk}m_k$, so that
$\Delta Q(p)=\tau\theta\big(T^0_2(p)-T^0_1(p)\big)/\big(1+\tau\theta(C_{p1}^{-1}+C_{p2}^{-1})\big)$ in closed form, and $\Phi(p)=\sum_k\alpha_k(p)-1$.
\item \emph{Finite $\nu$} ($\mu=\theta=\infty$). For given $p$ the vapour fraction solves
$\rho Y-m^{\rm tr}_2+\tau\nu\,(g_2-g_1)\big(p,T(p,Y)\big)=0$, increasing in $Y$ and clamped to its admissible interval;
then $\Phi(p)=\rho\sum_kY_kv_k(p,T)-1$. A state-dependent rate (Sec.~\ref{sec:res_bartak}) is evaluated in each cell from its
$pT$-equilibrium state at the beginning of the step and frozen over the stages.
\item \emph{$pTg$ closure} ($\mu=\theta=\nu=\infty$). $T=T_{\rm sat}(p)$, $Y$ from the energy, $\Phi(p)=\rho\sum_kY_kv_k-1$; $Y$ is clamped to
$[Y_{\min},1-Y_{\min}]$, $Y_{\min}=10^{-10}$, with $T$ then from the energy, and for $p\ge p_c$ the cell is single-phase.
\end{itemize}
The interfacial quantities $\rho_I$, $e_I$ do not appear. Thermal and chemical relaxation are active in interface cells,
$\epsilon_I\le\alpha_1\le1-\epsilon_I$ with $\epsilon_I=10^{-4}$. Mass transfer is activated where the temperature of the $pT$-equilibrium
state exceeds $T_{\rm sat}(p)$, or, when thermal and chemical relaxation act together, where a phase temperature of the
mechanical-equilibrium state exceeds $T_{\rm sat}(p)$ (the levels of Sec.~\ref{sec:results}). Activation is decided once per
time step on $U^n$; for the instantaneous $pT$ and $pTg$ closures $U^n$ is projected onto their equilibrium manifold (the
same closure with $s=0$), and a cell whose projection has no admissible solution uses the mechanical closure for that
step. The closure type is frozen over the stages.

\subsubsection{Solution of the pressure system}
\label{sec:pressure_system}
With the face velocity and face pressure of Sec.~\ref{sec:dissipation}, the fully discrete counterpart of
\eqref{eq:global_semi} is the system
\begin{equation}
G_{i,j}(P)\equiv\bar p^{\rm cl}_{i,j}\big(s_{i,j},X^{\rm tr}_{i,j},(\rho e)^C_{i,j};\ \mu,\theta,\nu\big)-P_{i,j}=0
\label{eq:global}
\end{equation}
for the cell pressures $P_{i,j}$. Its residual in cell $(i,j)$ depends only on the pressures of the cell and its four
neighbours, and the nonlinearity, which carries the equations of state and the relaxation, is local to the cell, as in
the mildly nonlinear systems of \cite{DumbserCasulli2016,CasulliZanolli2012}. The Jacobian is a three-point (five-point in 2D) matrix with negative diagonal and non-negative off-diagonal entries.
The system is solved by Newton's method with a finite-difference Jacobian and a backtracking line search that also
requires every local closure to be admissible. The unknown is the pressure perturbation $P-P_{\rm ref}$, with $P_{\rm ref}$ the
minimum of the initial mixture pressure. The residual of cell $(i,j)$ is scaled by $N_{i,j}=|P_{i,j}|+K_{i,j}+P_{E,i,j}+1$, where
$K$ is the Wood bulk modulus $\rho c_p^2$ of $X^*$ and $P_{E}=\sum_k\big(|\varepsilon^*_k|+|m^*_kq_k|\big)/\sum_k\partial\varepsilon_k/\partial p|_{\alpha_k,m_k}$ the
pressure scale of the energy round-off; the natural scale of $P$ is $\sigma_{i,j}=N_{i,j}/D_{i,j}$ with
$D_{i,j}=1+(K_{i,j}+P_{E,i,j})\sum_f(\tau/h_f)\kappa_f$, the scale of $\partial G_{i,j}/\partial P_{i,j}$ ($\kappa_f$ the pressure coefficient of $u_f$, Sec.~\ref{sec:fully}),
and the finite-difference increments are $10^{-7}\sigma$. The linear systems are solved by the Thomas algorithm for
non-periodic one-dimensional problems and otherwise by BiCGStab with a Jacobi plus constant-coarse-space
preconditioner (relative tolerance $10^{-13}$), which makes the iteration independent of the Mach number. The
iteration stops when $\max|G_{i,j}|/N_{i,j}<10^{-13}$ or when the Newton step satisfies $\max|\delta P_{i,j}|/(|P_{i,j}-P_{\rm ref}|+\sigma_{i,j})<10^{-13}$;
if the line search cannot reduce the residual, the solution is accepted when every $|G_{i,j}|$ lies within $10^{-13}N_{i,j}$ plus the
round-off floor of the closure, at most 60 iterations being allowed.

\subsection{Face velocity, face pressure and acoustic dissipation}
\label{sec:dissipation}
The implicit terms are discretised by central differences on the faces, supplemented by the acoustic dissipation
that the central operator lacks. On the face $(i+\tfrac12,j)$, with $\rho^*_{i,j}=\sum_km^*_{k,i,j}$, $u^*_{i,j}=(\rho u)^*_{i,j}/\rho^*_{i,j}$ and
$Z_{i,j}=\rho^*_{i,j}c_{f,i,j}$ (frozen sound speed on $X^*$), the face velocity is the average of the cell velocities plus
the pressure acceleration of the two adjacent half cells, the face pressure is the density-weighted average that
conserves momentum, and both carry the upwind terms of the acoustic Riemann solution:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
u_f&=\bar u^*_f-\frac{\tau(P_{i+1,j}-P_{i,j})}{\bar\rho_f\Delta x}-\varphi_f\,\psi^P_f\,\frac{P_{i+1,j}-P_{i,j}}{Z_{i,j}+Z_{i+1,j}},\\
p_f&=\frac{\rho^*_{i+1,j}P_{i,j}+\rho^*_{i,j}P_{i+1,j}}{\rho^*_{i,j}+\rho^*_{i+1,j}}-\varphi_f\,\psi^u_f\,\frac{Z_{i,j}Z_{i+1,j}}{Z_{i,j}+Z_{i+1,j}}\,D^*_f,
\end{aligned}\qquad \bar\rho_f=\tfrac12(\rho^*_{i,j}+\rho^*_{i+1,j}),
\label{eq:faces}
\end{equation}
with $\bar u^*_f=\tfrac12(u^*_{i,j}+u^*_{i+1,j})$ and the velocity jump $D^*_f$ given below. The face sweeps $u_f\tau$ during the
step, of which the explicit flux has already swept $\bar u^e_f\tau$, $\bar u^e_f=\tfrac12(u^L+u^R)$; the incremental flux
supplies the difference from the upwind side of $u_f$, with the reconstructed face values of \eqref{eq:rusanov} as
donors,
\begin{equation}
I_f[X]=X^L_f\big[(u_f)^+-(\bar u^e_f)^+\big]+X^R_f\big[(u_f)^--(\bar u^e_f)^-\big],\qquad X\in\{\alpha_k,m_k,\varepsilon_k\}.
\label{eq:increment}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Acoustic complement}
The central implicit acoustic operator does not damp the odd--even velocity mode, and the material-speed
dissipation of \eqref{eq:rusanov} does not act on pressure jumps; the terms with $\varphi_f$ in \eqref{eq:faces} supply the
acoustic Godunov dissipation that is missing. For linear acoustics, one backward-Euler step with \eqref{eq:faces} has,
at low wave numbers, the effective viscosity $\tfrac12c^2\tau+\varphi\,\tfrac12c\Delta x=\tfrac12c\Delta x(\nu+\varphi)$, $\nu=c\tau/\Delta x$.
Requiring that each step carries at least the upwind viscosity $\tfrac12c\Delta x$ on resolved acoustic waves, and no
more, gives
\begin{equation}
\varphi_f=\Big(1-\frac{\tau\min(c_{f,i,j},c_{f,i+1,j})}{\Delta x}\Big)^+ :
\label{eq:weight}
\end{equation}
for $\tau\to0$ \eqref{eq:faces} gives the acoustic Godunov face states, for $c\tau\ge\Delta x$ the complement vanishes. The frozen sound speed is used since
$\rho c_f\ge\rho c_{\rm eq}$ for every relaxed closure \cite{FlattenLund2011}, so that the dissipation is never smaller than that of
the relaxed waves. Where $X^*$ is not admissible, $c_f=\infty$ and the face carries no acoustic terms.

\paragraph{Second-order factors}
The factors $\psi\in[0,1]$ evaluate the complement on the reconstructed face states, $\psi D=X^R-X^L$ for a face jump $D$.
The pressure is a function of the volume-transported quantities and the velocity, whose reconstruction is scaled by
$\phi_s=\min(\hat\phi_{i,j},\hat\phi_{i+1,j})$, with $\hat\phi$ the minimum of the shared factor \eqref{eq:shared_limiter} and of the smoothness ratios
(minmod over central slope) of the velocity components; with the minmod slope of $P$ this gives
\begin{equation}
\psi^P_f=1-\phi_s\big(1-\psi^{\rm mm}_f[P]\big),\qquad \psi^u_f=\psi^{\rm mm}_f[u],\qquad
\psi^{\rm mm}_f[X]=1-\frac{\sigma^{\rm mm}_{i,j}[X]+\sigma^{\rm mm}_{i+1,j}[X]}{2D},
\label{eq:psi}
\end{equation}
the velocity-jump term acting on the separately reconstructed velocity. The slopes of $P$ are those of the pressure field
of the previous Newton initial guess (the pressure of the previous stage), and those of $u$ are taken on $u^*$, so that
the factors are frozen during the solution of the stage; with unlimited central slopes $\sigma$ are the central slopes,
clipped to $\psi\in[0,1]$. Both terms are first order at a local
extremum of any quantity whose flux they modify, in particular at contacts, and second order where the solution is
smooth (the LED criterion of Jameson \cite{Jameson1995}).

\paragraph{Velocity jump}
In one dimension $D^*_f=u^*_{i+1}-u^*_i$. In two dimensions the normal jump is replaced by the vertex divergence,
\begin{equation}
D^*_{i+1/2,j}=\Delta x\,\tfrac12\big(\mathrm{div}_{i+1/2,j+1/2}+\mathrm{div}_{i+1/2,j-1/2}\big),\quad
\mathrm{div}_{i+1/2,j+1/2}=\frac{u_{i+1,j}+u_{i+1,j+1}-u_{i,j}-u_{i,j+1}}{2\Delta x}+\frac{v_{i,j+1}+v_{i+1,j+1}-v_{i,j}-v_{i+1,j}}{2\Delta y},
\label{eq:vertex_div}
\end{equation}
so that the term acts as a bulk viscosity on the divergence and leaves divergence-free motion untouched; for
$y$-independent data it reduces to the one-dimensional jump.

\paragraph{Boundaries}
Ghost cells are filled by copying (transmissive), reflecting (walls) or wrapping (periodic) the interior cells, and the
pressure of a ghost cell equals that of the adjacent interior cell. On a wall face the face velocity is the wall velocity
($u_f=0$ for a fixed wall, with no incremental flux; the piston of Sec.~\ref{sec:res_bubble} moves the wall face). At a
prescribed-pressure boundary the ghost cells keep the volume fractions, partial densities and velocity of the adjacent
cell with both phase pressures equal to the prescribed value, which enters the pressure system as a given value.

\subsection{Positivity}
\label{sec:positivity}
The admissible set of a stiffened-gas or Noble--Abel stiffened-gas phase is the cone of states with $\alpha_k>0$, $m_k>0$
and $L_k(X)>0$ for the two linear functionals $L^{(1)}_k=\alpha_k-m_kb_k$ and $L^{(2)}_k=\varepsilon_k-m_kq_k-(\alpha_k-m_kb_k)\pi_k$. Positivity
of the transported states requires first that the reconstructed face states belong to this cone.

\begin{lemma}[Face states]\label{lem:faces}
Let the cell states on the stencil $\{i-1,i,i+1\}$ be admissible. Then the face values \eqref{eq:shared_limiter} of $\alpha_k$ and
$m_k$ are positive, and for $L\in\{L^{(1)}_k,L^{(2)}_k\}$
\begin{equation}
L\big(X^L_{i+1/2}\big)\ge L_i-\tfrac{\phi_i}4L_{i-1},\qquad L\big(X^R_{i-1/2}\big)\ge L_i-\tfrac{\phi_i}4L_{i+1},
\label{eq:face_bound}
\end{equation}
with $L_j=L(X_j)$. The face states of cell $i$ are therefore admissible if $\phi_i\le4L_i/\max(L_{i-1},L_{i+1})$ for both functionals.
This holds in particular when $L$ is proportional on the stencil to one of the volume-transported quantities, e.g.\ for a
stiffened gas at locally uniform pressure, where $L^{(2)}_k=\alpha_k(p+\pi_k)/(\gamma_k-1)$, which covers interfaces and trace phases.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For each $X\in\mathcal X_V$ the slope $\sigma_i$ has the sign of both one-sided differences and $|\sigma_i|\le\min(|X_{i+1}-X_i|,|X_i-X_{i-1}|)$, so
$X^L_{i+1/2}=X_i+\tfrac12\sigma_i$ lies between $X_i$ and $\tfrac12(X_i+X_{i+1})$, and $X^R_{i-1/2}$ between $X_i$ and $\tfrac12(X_i+X_{i-1})$; hence the
face values of $\alpha_k$ and $m_k$ are positive. All quantities share $\phi_i$, so $X^L_{i+1/2}=X_i+\tfrac{\phi_i}4(X_{i+1}-X_{i-1})$ for every
component and, $L$ being linear, $L(X^L_{i+1/2})=L_i+\tfrac{\phi_i}4(L_{i+1}-L_{i-1})\ge L_i-\tfrac{\phi_i}4L_{i-1}$ since $L_{i+1}>0$; likewise for
$X^R_{i-1/2}$. If $L=\kappa X$ on the stencil for some $X\in\mathcal X_V$ and $\kappa>0$, then $L(X^{L,R})=\kappa X^{L,R}$, which has the sign of the face
values of $X$, positive by the first part.
\end{proof}
The bound \eqref{eq:face_bound} is sharp for a linear reconstruction with a common slope factor: a linear functional of
the face state is not a convex combination of the cell values, and the minmod factors of the individual components do
not bound it. Admissibility of the face states is therefore not guaranteed in general, e.g.\ at a strong pressure jump
coinciding with a jump of the phase content.

\begin{proposition}[Positivity]\label{prop:positivity}
Let the phases be stiffened or Noble--Abel stiffened gases, and consider a stage with $Q^e=Q^s$ (a backward-Euler step
or the first stage of \eqref{eq:lsdirk}) whose reconstructed face states are admissible (Lemma~\ref{lem:faces}). If $r(a_f+|u_f|)\le\tfrac12$ on every
face, $r=\tau/\Delta x$ ($\tfrac14$ per direction in 2D), the transported state $(\alpha_k,m_k,\varepsilon_k)^{\rm tr}$ of every phase is a
non-negative combination of reconstructed face values and hence admissible, independently of the content ratio of
the phases.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The explicit flux plus the increment is $X^L_f[\tfrac12(u^L+a)+w^L]+X^R_f[\tfrac12(u^R-a)+w^R]$, $w^{L,R}$ the brackets of
\eqref{eq:increment}; with $a\ge\max|u^{L,R}|$ the neighbour coefficients are $\ge(u_f)^+$ and $\ge-(u_f)^-$, and the
own-cell coefficients are $\tfrac12-r(\cdot)$ with $(\cdot)\le a+|u_f|$, using $X_i=\tfrac12(X^R_{i-1/2}+X^L_{i+1/2})$, which holds for $Q^e=Q^s$.
The admissible set of SG and NASG phases is a convex cone.
\end{proof}
The condition involves the implicit face velocity and is not implied by \eqref{eq:cfl}; with $C=0.2$ the explicit part
satisfies $a_{ii}ra\approx0.06$, and the condition fails only where $u_f$ departs strongly from the material velocity.

\subsection{Well-balanced property}
\label{sec:wb}
\begin{proposition}[Contacts and temperature consistency]\label{prop:wb}
(i) A state with uniform $u$ and $p$ and arbitrary $\alpha_k$, $\rho_k$ is preserved exactly by the scheme with mechanical
relaxation; with thermal or chemical relaxation active, this holds if in addition $T_1=T_2$.
(ii) If each phase has uniform $p$ and $T_k$, transport does not change $\rho_k$, $e_k$, $T_k$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
(i) By \eqref{eq:eps_nasg} $\varepsilon_k=c_k(p)\alpha_k+(q_k-b_kc_k(p))m_k$ in every cell; the shared limiter keeps this relation on the faces
and \eqref{eq:rusanov} is linear, so it holds for $X^*$. With $P\equiv p$ all terms of \eqref{eq:faces} beyond the averages
vanish, $u_f=u$, $p_f=p$, the increments vanish, $s=\delta=0$, and $\alpha_k=\alpha^*_k$ solves the closure at $p$, so $P\equiv p$ solves
\eqref{eq:global} (with $T_1=T_2$ the projection onto the $pT$ manifold leaves the state unchanged). (ii) $m_k$ and $\varepsilon_k$ are proportional to $\alpha_k$ and all three are transported by the same
linear operator.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Asymptotic preserving property}
\label{sec:ap}
\paragraph{Relaxation limits}
\begin{theorem}[Relaxation limits]\label{thm:limits}
Assume that the local closures have non-singular Jacobians at their solutions and that the Jacobian of
\eqref{eq:global} is non-singular. (i) As $\mu$, $\theta$ or $\nu\to\infty$ the stage solution converges to that of the corresponding
equilibrium closure with an error $O(1/(\tau\mu))$, $O(1/(\tau\theta))$, $O(1/(\tau\nu))$ (for $\nu$, where $Y$ is not clamped).
(ii) For a vanishing rate the closure reduces exactly to the scheme with that relaxation switched off: $\mu=0$ gives
the frozen two-pressure scheme, $\theta=0$ the $p$ closure and $\nu=0$ the $pT$ closure.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
(i) For $\mu$, with $\kappa=1/(\tau\mu)$ the closure reads $p_1-p_2=\kappa R$; at $\kappa=0$ it is the $p$ closure, and the
implicit function theorem gives an $O(\kappa)$ difference for each cell; the non-singular global Jacobian carries it to the
discrete solution. The $\theta$ and $\nu$ limits follow in the same way from $T_1-T_2=O(1/(\tau\theta))$ and
$|g_2-g_1|=O(1/(\tau\nu))$. The tableau of Sec.~\ref{sec:time} is stiffly accurate and L-stable, so every stage lies on
the equilibrium manifold. (ii) $\mu=0$, $\theta=0$, $\nu=0$ give $R=0$, $\Delta Q=0$, $Y=Y^{\rm tr}$.
\end{proof}
Formally, the limit schemes are consistent discretisations of the relaxed models: linearising the $p$ closure, each
phase is isentropic and $\mathrm d\alpha_k=s\,\frac{\alpha_k/(\rho_kc_k^2)}{\sum_j\alpha_j/(\rho_jc_j^2)}$, which with $s=\Delta t\,\partial_xu$ is the volume equation of
the Kapila model.

\paragraph{Low-Mach limit of the mixture}
Let $u_0$, $c_0$ and $\rho_0$ be a reference velocity, sound speed and density and $M=u_0/c_0$. With impermeable
boundaries and well-prepared data, pressure variations are of the size of the inertial terms, $\rho_0u_0^2=M^2\rho_0c_0^2$,
so that $\bar p=\bar p_{(0)}+M^2\bar p_{(2)}+\dots$ with $\bar p_{(2)}=O(\rho_0c_0^2)$. In the momentum equation the gradient of
$\bar p_{(0)}$ would exceed the inertia by $M^{-2}$, hence $\partial_x\bar p_{(0)}=0$; integrating the pressure equation of the
mixture, $\partial_t\bar p+u\,\partial_x\bar p+\rho c_p^2\,\partial_xu=0$, over the domain then gives $\bar p_{(0)}={\rm const}$ and
$\partial_xu_{(0)}=0$: the incompressible limit of the mixture.
\begin{proposition}[Formal low-Mach limit]\label{prop:lowmach}
For fixed $\Delta t$, $\Delta x$ and well-prepared data $P=P_0+M^2P_2$, formally as $M\to0$: the face velocities are
discretely divergence-free to $O(M^2)$, $P_2$ solves the discrete Poisson equation
$\delta\cdot\big(\bar u^*_f-\tau\,\delta P_2/(\bar\rho_f\Delta x)\big)=O(M^2)$, the acoustic complement vanishes ($\varphi=0$ once
$M<a_{ii}C$), and the limit scheme is explicit convection plus a projection, with a time step independent of $M$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For pressure variations of order $\rho_0c_0^2$ the pressure gradient in $u_f$ is $O(M^{-2})$ relative to the inertia; inserting
$P=P_0+M^2P_2$ into the linearised closure $P_i-P^*_i=-K^W_i\,s_i$, $s_i=(\tau/\Delta x)\,\delta[u_f]_i$, with $P^*$ the mixture pressure of
$X^*$ and $K^W=\rho c_p^2$ the Wood bulk modulus, and collecting the orders $M^{-2}$ and $1$
gives the first two statements; $c\tau/\Delta x=O(1/M)$ gives the third.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Second-order time discretisation}
\label{sec:time}
The scheme of Secs.~\ref{sec:advective}--\ref{sec:dissipation} is first order in time. Second order is obtained with
the semi-implicit IMEX Runge--Kutta schemes of Boscarino, Filbet and Russo \cite{BoscarinoFilbetRusso2016}, for which the
time linearisation introduced by the splitting, explicit velocity in the advective fluxes and implicit velocity
increment, gives the semi-implicit structure directly. The semi-discrete system is written in autonomous form as
\begin{equation}
\partial_tU=\mathcal H\big(U_E(t),U_I(t)\big),
\label{eq:autonomous}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal H$ is the spatial discretisation of the split system, $U_E$ the state on which the advective sub-system
is evaluated and $U_I$ the state of the pressure--relaxation sub-system. An IMEX Runge--Kutta scheme
\cite{Ascher1997,PareschiRusso2005} is defined by a pair of Butcher tableaux $(\tilde A,\tilde{\mathbf b},\tilde{\mathbf c})$ and
$(A,\mathbf b,\mathbf c)$, $\tilde A$ strictly lower triangular and $A$ lower triangular. We use the stiffly accurate, L-stable
LSDIRK2(2,2,2) scheme \cite{BoscarinoFilbetRusso2016,Avgerinos2019},
\begin{equation}
\begin{array}{c|cc}0&0&0\\ \hat c&\hat c&0\\\hline &1-\beta&\beta\end{array}\qquad
\begin{array}{c|cc}\beta&\beta&0\\ 1&1-\beta&\beta\\\hline &1-\beta&\beta\end{array},\qquad \beta=1-\tfrac1{\sqrt2},\quad \hat c=\tfrac1{2\beta}.
\label{eq:lsdirk}
\end{equation}
With $U^n_E=U^n_I=U^n$, the stages $i=1,2$ are
\begin{equation}
U_{E,i}=U^n+\Delta t\sum_{j<i}\tilde a_{ij}K_j,\qquad \tilde U_{I,i}=U^n+\Delta t\sum_{j<i}a_{ij}K_j,\qquad
K_i=\mathcal H\big(U_{E,i},\ \tilde U_{I,i}+\Delta t\,a_{ii}K_i\big).
\label{eq:stages}
\end{equation}
Each stage is one application of the first-order scheme: the explicit fluxes \eqref{eq:explicit} are evaluated on
$U_{E,i}$, the update starts from $\tilde U_{I,i}$ instead of $U^n$, and the implicit step is $\tau=a_{ii}\Delta t$, giving the stage
value $U_i$ and $K_i=(U_i-\tilde U_{I,i})/\tau$. Since $\mathbf b$ is the last row of $A$, $U^{n+1}=U_2$. The first step uses the
acoustic Courant number $0.4$, and no step may exceed $1.2$ times the previous one; a step limited by this rule (in
practice the start-up and the steps after a shortened step) uses one backward-Euler stage. With a prescribed fixed time
step the rule is not applied.

\subsection{Fully discrete scheme in two dimensions}
\label{sec:fully}
One stage of the scheme, with the explicit state $Q^e=U_{E,i}$, the update base $Q^s=\tilde U_{I,i}$ and the implicit step
$\tau=a_{ii}\Delta t$ ($Q^e=Q^s=U^n$, $\tau=\Delta t$ for a first-order step), consists of the following steps; $r_x=\tau/\Delta x$,
$r_y=\tau/\Delta y$, and $u_n$, $u_t$ denote the normal and tangential velocity on a face. When thermal or chemical relaxation is
used, before the first stage of a step the relaxation closure of every cell is activated and $U^n$ is projected as in
Sec.~\ref{sec:relaxation}.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Explicit advective sub-system.} The face values are reconstructed on $Q^e$ by \eqref{eq:shared_limiter}, and every
transported quantity is advanced with the Rusanov fluxes \eqref{eq:rusanov},
\[
X^*_{i,j}=X^s_{i,j}-r_x\,\delta_x\big[F^e[X]\big]_{i,j}-r_y\,\delta_y\big[G^e[X]\big]_{i,j},\qquad
X\in\{\alpha_1,\alpha_2,m_1,m_2,\varepsilon_1,\varepsilon_2,\rho u,\rho v,E\},
\]
with $G^e$ the $y$-flux of \eqref{eq:rusanov} with $v$ in place of $u$.
\item \emph{Face coefficients.} On every face the quantities of Sec.~\ref{sec:dissipation} are computed from $X^*$, the
limiter factors of the reconstruction of $Q^e$ and the pressures of the previous stage (the Newton initial guess): the averages $\bar u^*_f$, $\bar\rho_f$, the impedances $Z$, the weight \eqref{eq:weight},
the factors \eqref{eq:psi} and the velocity jump \eqref{eq:vertex_div}. They are frozen during the solution of the stage,
so that the face velocity and face pressure \eqref{eq:faces} are affine in the two adjacent cell pressures,
\[
u_f=\bar u^*_f-\kappa_f\,(P_R-P_L),\qquad p_f=w_fP_L+(1-w_f)P_R-\varpi_f ,
\]
with $\kappa_f=\tau/(\bar\rho_f h)+\varphi_f\psi^P_f/(Z_L+Z_R)$, $w_f=\rho^*_R/(\rho^*_L+\rho^*_R)$ and
$\varpi_f=\varphi_f\psi^u_fZ_LZ_RD^*_f/(Z_L+Z_R)$, $h=\Delta x$ or $\Delta y$; wall faces and prescribed-pressure
boundaries are treated as in Sec.~\ref{sec:dissipation}.
\item \emph{Incremental fluxes and conservative updates.} For given cell pressures, the incremental fluxes
\eqref{eq:increment} are split into their donor parts $I_f=I^L_f+I^R_f$; the incremental mass fluxes are
$\hat M^{L,R}_f=\sum_kI^{L,R}_f[m_k]$, $\hat M_f=\hat M^L_f+\hat M^R_f$. Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
&X^{\rm tr}_{i,j}=X^*_{i,j}-r_x\,\delta_x\big[I[X]\big]_{i,j}-r_y\,\delta_y\big[I[X]\big]_{i,j},\qquad X\in\{\alpha_k,m_k,\varepsilon_k\},\\
&s_{i,j}=1-\textstyle\sum_k\alpha^{\rm tr}_{k,i,j}=r_x\,\delta_x[u_f]_{i,j}+r_y\,\delta_y[v_f]_{i,j},\qquad \rho^{n+1}_{i,j}=\textstyle\sum_km^{\rm tr}_{k,i,j},
\end{aligned}
\]
the momentum is advanced with the face pressure and the incremental momentum flux, normal and tangential,
\[
\begin{aligned}
(\rho u)^{\rm new}_{i,j}&=(\rho u)^*_{i,j}-r_x\,\delta_x\big[p_f+u_f\hat M_f\big]_{i,j}-r_y\,\delta_y\big[u^L_t\hat M^L_f+u^R_t\hat M^R_f\big]_{i,j},\\
(\rho v)^{\rm new}_{i,j}&=(\rho v)^*_{i,j}-r_x\,\delta_x\big[v^L_t\hat M^L_f+v^R_t\hat M^R_f\big]_{i,j}-r_y\,\delta_y\big[p_f+v_f\hat M_f\big]_{i,j},
\end{aligned}
\]
with the tangential velocities $u_t$, $v_t$ of the donor cells on $X^*$, and the total energy with the energy flux
\[
F^I_f[E]=\textstyle\sum_kI_f[\varepsilon_k]+\tfrac12u_n^2\hat M_f+\tfrac12\big((u^L_t)^2\hat M^L_f+(u^R_t)^2\hat M^R_f\big)+p_fu_n ,\qquad
E^{\rm new}_{i,j}=E^*_{i,j}-r_x\,\delta_x\big[F^I[E]\big]_{i,j}-r_y\,\delta_y\big[F^I[E]\big]_{i,j},
\]
$u_n=u_f$ on $x$-faces and $u_n=v_f$ on $y$-faces. The conservative mixture internal energy is
$(\rho e)^C_{i,j}=E^{\rm new}_{i,j}-|(\rho\mathbf u)^{\rm new}_{i,j}|^2/(2\rho^{n+1}_{i,j})$. All these quantities depend only on the pressures of
the cell and its four neighbours.
\item \emph{Local closure.} In every cell the defect \eqref{eq:defect} is partitioned by \eqref{eq:Lambda}, with the
physical budget \eqref{eq:Qk} computed on $X^*$ after step 1 and frozen during the stage, and the relaxation closure of
Sec.~\ref{sec:relaxation} returns $\bar p^{\rm cl}_{i,j}$ and the phase states.
\item \emph{Pressure system.} The system \eqref{eq:global} is solved for the cell pressures by the Newton method of
Sec.~\ref{sec:pressure_system}; every residual evaluation repeats steps 3 and 4.
\item \emph{Update.} At the converged pressures, the stage value consists of the momentum and the total energy of
step 3 and the volume fractions, partial densities and phase energies of the closure of step 4.
\end{enumerate}
The operator $\mathcal H$ in \eqref{eq:stages} is the one-stage update given by steps 1--6: step 1 is the explicit part,
evaluated on $U_{E,i}$, and steps 2--6 the implicit part over $\tau=a_{ii}\Delta t$. The construction is the same in one
and two space dimensions.

In summary, the scheme is stabilised by three sources of numerical dissipation. The first is the Rusanov
dissipation of the explicit advective fluxes, which scales with the flow velocity. The second and third are the two
terms of the acoustic complement in \eqref{eq:faces}: a pressure-jump term in the face velocity and a velocity-jump
term in the face pressure, which act on the acoustic waves with the weight \eqref{eq:weight} and vanish as $c\tau/\Delta x$
grows, i.e.\ in the low Mach number limit. Since all three are either explicit with the material speed or implicit,
none of them requires a time step smaller than \eqref{eq:cfl}.
