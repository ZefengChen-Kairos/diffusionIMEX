\section{Semi-implicit numerical scheme}
\label{sec:method}

We now describe the semi-implicit scheme. After introducing the notation, we present the explicit update of the
advective sub-system (Sec.~\ref{sec:advective}), the implicit update of the pressure--relaxation sub-system with its
local closure and relaxation closures (Sec.~\ref{sec:implicit}), and the face velocity and face pressure that carry the
acoustic dissipation (Sec.~\ref{sec:dissipation}). The properties of the scheme are proved in
Secs.~\ref{sec:wb}--\ref{sec:ap}; second order in time is obtained in Sec.~\ref{sec:time}, and
Sec.~\ref{sec:fully} collects the fully discrete scheme in two space dimensions.

The computational domain $\Omega=[x_{\min},x_{\max}]\times[y_{\min},y_{\max}]$ is covered by a uniform Cartesian grid of
$N_x\times N_y$ control volumes with spacings $\Delta x=(x_{\max}-x_{\min})/N_x$ and $\Delta y=(y_{\max}-y_{\min})/N_y$. The cell $(i,j)$ has
centre $\mathbf x_{i,j}=(x_i,y_j)$, its faces are denoted by $(i\pm\tfrac12,j)$ and $(i,j\pm\tfrac12)$ and its corners by
$(i\pm\tfrac12,j\pm\tfrac12)$. All variables are cell averages, defined at the cell centres for both sub-systems; time levels
are denoted by superscripts and the face differences by $\delta_x[F]_{i,j}=F_{i+1/2,j}-F_{i-1/2,j}$,
$\delta_y[G]_{i,j}=G_{i,j+1/2}-G_{i,j-1/2}$. Where the $y$-direction is analogous it is omitted.

\subsection{Advective sub-system}
\label{sec:advective}
The advective sub-system \eqref{eq:advective} transports every quantity with the flow velocity. Since the volume fraction
is advected by the non-conservative product $u\,\partial_x\alpha_1$, it is a non-conservative hyperbolic system
$\partial_t\mathbf Q+\mathbf A^c(\mathbf Q)\,\partial_x\mathbf Q=\mathbf 0$, with $\mathbf A^c$ given in \eqref{eq:Ac}. The coefficient matrix is the Jacobian of the transport flux
$Xu$ in every row except the first, $u\,\partial_x\alpha_1$, which is not the derivative of any flux. The sub-system is discretised by
the path-conservative Rusanov scheme \cite{DalMaso1995,Pares2006,Castro2006},
\begin{equation}
\mathbf Q^*_i=\mathbf Q^n_i-\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x}\Big(\mathbf D^-_{i+1/2}+\mathbf D^+_{i-1/2}\Big),\qquad
\mathbf D^-_{i+1/2}+\mathbf D^+_{i+1/2}=\int_0^1\mathbf A^c(\boldsymbol\Psi)\,\partial_s\boldsymbol\Psi\,\mathrm ds ,
\label{eq:pc_update}
\end{equation}
where $\boldsymbol\Psi(s)$, $s\in[0,1]$, is the path joining the two states of the face, and the Rusanov dissipation
$\pm\tfrac12a_{i+1/2}\Delta\mathbf Q_{i+1/2}$ enters $\mathbf D^\mp_{i+1/2}$ with $\Delta(\cdot)=(\cdot)^R-(\cdot)^L$ and
\begin{equation}
a_{i+1/2}=\max\big(|u^L_{i+1/2}|,|u^R_{i+1/2}|\big)
\label{eq:rusanov_speed}
\end{equation}
(or its maximum over all faces of the same direction), the eigenvalue $u$ of the advective sub-system on the two face
states, so that the dissipation is proportional to the flow velocity.

For the rows of $m_k$, $\rho u$, $\varepsilon_k$ and $E$ the row of $\mathbf A^c$ is the gradient of the flux $Xu$, the path integral equals
the jump of the flux whatever the path, and \eqref{eq:pc_update} is the conservative update with the Rusanov flux
\cite{Rusanov1961},
\begin{equation}
X^*_{i,j}=X^n_{i,j}-\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x}\,\delta_x\big[F^e[X]\big]_{i,j}-\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta y}\,\delta_y\big[G^e[X]\big]_{i,j},\qquad
X\in\{m_1,m_2,\varepsilon_1,\varepsilon_2,\rho u,\rho v,E\},
\label{eq:explicit}
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
F^e_{i+1/2,j}[X]=\tfrac12\big(X^Lu^L+X^Ru^R\big)-\tfrac12a_{i+1/2,j}\big(X^R-X^L\big).
\label{eq:rusanov}
\end{equation}
For the row of $\alpha_1$ the path integral is taken along the linear path with the face velocity frozen at the mean
$\bar u_{i+1/2}=\tfrac12(u^L+u^R)$, $\int_0^1u\,\partial_s\alpha_1\,\mathrm ds=\bar u_{i+1/2}\,(\alpha_{1,i+1}-\alpha_{1,i})$, and the fluctuation is split
between the two cells as in Saurel and Abgrall \cite{SaurelAbgrall1999a}, with the Rusanov flux $F^e[\alpha_1]$ of the volume
$\alpha_1u$,
\begin{equation}
D^-_{i+1/2}[\alpha_1]=F^e_{i+1/2}[\alpha_1]-\alpha_{1,i}\,\bar u_{i+1/2},\qquad
D^+_{i+1/2}[\alpha_1]=\alpha_{1,i+1}\,\bar u_{i+1/2}-F^e_{i+1/2}[\alpha_1].
\label{eq:pc_rusanov}
\end{equation}
Their sum is the path integral, and the update of the cell reads
\begin{equation}
\alpha^*_{1,i,j}=\alpha^n_{1,i,j}-\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x}\Big(\delta_x\big[F^e[\alpha_1]\big]_{i,j}-\alpha^n_{1,i,j}\,\delta_x[\bar u]_{i,j}\Big)
-\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta y}\Big(\delta_y\big[G^e[\alpha_1]\big]_{i,j}-\alpha^n_{1,i,j}\,\delta_y[\bar v]_{i,j}\Big),\qquad
\alpha^*_2=1-\alpha^*_1 :
\label{eq:alpha_explicit}
\end{equation}
the Rusanov difference of the volume flux minus the cell's own volume fraction times the divergence of the face
velocities. The advection of the volume fraction is completed in this step; the implicit step changes $\alpha_k$ only
through the relaxation terms. The volume strain is assigned with the cell's own $\alpha_{1,i}$, which is the volume fraction of the
mass and energy that the conservative fluxes of the cell compress, so that the volume, the mass and the energy of a
trace phase stay consistent where the velocity jumps across an interface. With uniform velocity $\delta_x[\bar u]=0$, and
$\alpha_k$, $m_k$ and $\varepsilon_k$ are advanced by the same linear Rusanov operator, so that a state with uniform velocity and pressure is
transported without changing the phase densities and energies (Sec.~\ref{sec:wb}).

The face values are MUSCL reconstructions \cite{vanLeer1979} with minmod. The volume-transported quantities
$\mathcal X_V=(\alpha_1,m_1,m_2,\varepsilon_1,\varepsilon_2)$ share one limiter,
\begin{equation}
X^L_{i+1/2}=X_i+\tfrac12\sigma_i,\quad X^R_{i+1/2}=X_{i+1}-\tfrac12\sigma_{i+1},\qquad \sigma_i=\phi_i\,\tfrac12(X_{i+1}-X_{i-1}),\qquad
\phi_i=\min_{X\in\mathcal X_V}\frac{\mathrm{minmod}(X_i-X_{i-1},X_{i+1}-X_i)}{\tfrac12(X_{i+1}-X_{i-1})}\in[0,1],
\label{eq:shared_limiter}
\end{equation}
so that linear relations between them, in particular $\varepsilon_k=c_k(p)\alpha_k+\big(q_k-b_kc_k(p)\big)m_k$, $c_k(p)=(p+\gamma_k\pi_k)/(\gamma_k-1)$, at
uniform pressure, hold on the faces; quantities that are constant on the stencil to round-off are excluded from the minimum.
For stiffened and Noble--Abel stiffened gases the admissible states of a phase form the cone $\alpha_k>0$, $m_k>0$, $L_k>0$ for the
two linear functionals $L^{(1)}_k=\alpha_k-m_kb_k$ and $L^{(2)}_k=\varepsilon_k-m_kq_k-(\alpha_k-m_kb_k)\pi_k$. Since all components share $\phi_i$, such a
functional of a face state is $L(X^{L,R})=L_i\pm\tfrac{\phi_i}4(L_{i+1}-L_{i-1})$, which is not a convex combination of the cell values and is not
bounded by the minmod factors of the individual components, e.g.\ at a strong pressure jump coinciding with a jump of
the phase content. The shared factor is therefore limited further, for every phase and both functionals,
\begin{equation}
\phi_i\le\frac{2L_i}{|L_{i+1}-L_{i-1}|},\qquad L\in\{L^{(1)}_k,L^{(2)}_k\},\ k=1,2 ,
\label{eq:phi_cap}
\end{equation}
which keeps $L$ on both faces above $\tfrac12L_i$, the bound that minmod gives for a single non-negative monotone component. The velocity is reconstructed component-wise with its own minmod slope, and the face
momentum and energy are assembled from the face values.

The time step is given by the CFL condition of the advective sub-system,
\begin{equation}
\Delta t=C\left(\frac{\max_\Omega|u|}{\Delta x}+\frac{\max_\Omega|v|}{\Delta y}\right)^{-1},
\label{eq:cfl}
\end{equation}
which depends on the flow velocity only; $C=0.2$ unless stated otherwise, and the first steps are limited by the
start-up rule of Sec.~\ref{sec:time}. A proof of stability under \eqref{eq:cfl} is not available, as for the
other semi-implicit schemes of this class; all computations of Sec.~\ref{sec:results} are advanced with it.

\subsection{Pressure--relaxation sub-system}
\label{sec:implicit}
Having evolved the advective sub-system, we now consider the pressure--relaxation sub-system, in which all remaining
contributions are treated implicitly. They are solved together, within a single step, in the following sequence,
repeated at every iteration of the pressure solver:
\begin{enumerate}
\item for given cell pressures, the face velocities give the incremental fluxes, the transported phase states, the
strain of the pressure work and the conservative updates of momentum and total energy;
\item a local closure in every cell, containing the pressure work and the three relaxation processes, returns the cell
pressure and the phase states consistent with them;
\item the pressure system, which requires the closure pressure to equal the cell pressure, is solved by Newton's method.
\end{enumerate}
This section derives the implicit step in semi-discrete form; the fully discrete counterpart is given in
Sec.~\ref{sec:fully}.

\paragraph{Semi-discrete implicit step}
The pressure--relaxation sub-system \eqref{eq:pressure_sub} is discretised implicitly. Following the splitting of
Sec.~\ref{sec:split}, the flux of every conservative transported quantity at the new time level is written as
$Xu^{n+1}=Xu^n+X(u^{n+1}-u^n)$: the first part is the explicit flux of Sec.~\ref{sec:advective}, the second, the incremental
flux, is implicit. The partial densities and phase energies carry the same incremental flux; the momentum and
kinetic-energy increments follow from the incremental mass flux. The volume fraction has no incremental flux: its
advection is completed by the path-conservative update \eqref{eq:alpha_explicit}, and in the implicit step it changes only
through the relaxation terms, $\alpha^{\rm tr}_k=\alpha^*_k$. In semi-discrete form,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
X^{\rm tr}&=X^*-\Delta t\,\partial_x\big(X^{\rm face}(u^{n+1}-u^n)\big),\qquad X\in\{m_k,\varepsilon_k\},\\
(\rho u)^{n+1}&=(\rho u)^*-\Delta t\,\partial_x\bar p^{\,n+1}-\Delta t\,\partial_x\big(u^{n+1}\hat M\big),\\
E^{n+1}&=E^*-\Delta t\,\partial_x\Big(\textstyle\sum_k\varepsilon^{\rm face}_k(u^{n+1}-u^n)+\tfrac12(u^{n+1})^2\hat M+\bar p^{\,n+1}u^{n+1}\Big),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:semidiscrete}
\end{equation}
where $\hat M=\sum_km^{\rm face}_k(u^{n+1}-u^n)$ is the incremental mass flux and the face values are taken from the explicit
state. The phase states at $t^{n+1}$ follow from the transported states, the strain $s=\Delta t\,\partial_xu^{n+1}$ of the
pressure work and the conservative mixture internal energy
$(\rho e)^C=E^{n+1}-\tfrac12(\rho u)^{n+1\,2}/\rho^{n+1}$ through a local closure (Sec.~\ref{sec:closure}). The momentum and energy updates depend on $\bar p^{\,n+1}$, and the closure returns $\bar p^{\,n+1}$ as a function of
$s$ and $(\rho e)^C$, so the implicit step reduces to one equation per cell for the pressure,
\begin{equation}
G(\bar p)\equiv\bar p^{\rm cl}\big(s(\bar p),X^{\rm tr}(\bar p),(\rho e)^C(\bar p);\ \mu,\theta,\nu\big)-\bar p=0 ,
\label{eq:global_semi}
\end{equation}
a nonlinear and relaxing generalisation of the pressure wave equation $p^{n+1}=p^*-\Delta t\,\rho c^2\partial_xu^{n+1}$.

\paragraph{Derivation of the pressure wave equation}
The structure of \eqref{eq:global_semi} is seen by linearising it. To first order in $\Delta t$ the closure gives the cell
pressure as the pressure $P^*$ of the explicit state minus the response of the mixture to the compression,
\begin{equation}
\bar p^{\,n+1}=P^*-K^W s,\qquad s=\Delta t\,\partial_xu^{n+1},\qquad K^W=\rho c_p^2 ,
\label{eq:p_linear}
\end{equation}
with the Wood bulk modulus of the mixture at mechanical equilibrium ($K^W$ is replaced by the stiffness of the activated
closure). The momentum update of \eqref{eq:semidiscrete}, without the incremental term, provides the missing link,
\begin{equation}
u^{n+1}=u^*-\frac{\Delta t}{\rho}\,\partial_x\bar p^{\,n+1},
\label{eq:u_update}
\end{equation}
and inserting it into \eqref{eq:p_linear} eliminates the velocity, giving an elliptic equation for the pressure,
\begin{equation}
\bar p^{\,n+1}-\Delta t^2K^W\,\partial_x\Big(\frac1\rho\,\partial_x\bar p^{\,n+1}\Big)=P^*-\Delta t\,K^W\,\partial_xu^* .
\label{eq:wave}
\end{equation}
This is the pressure equation of semi-implicit schemes for single-phase flows \cite{DumbserCasulli2016,BoscheriPareschi2021} and of
the seven-equation scheme of \cite{BattistiBoscheri2025}, with the stiffness of the two-phase mixture. The scheme does not
use the linearisation: the closure evaluates the pressure response exactly for the equations of state and the
relaxation rates, and also contains the incremental fluxes and the distribution of the energy defect, so that the
discrete system \eqref{eq:global_semi} keeps the structure of \eqref{eq:wave}, a nonlinearity local to each cell together
with a linear coupling through the face velocities, without its linearisation errors.

The closure distributes the difference between the non-conservative and the conservative mixture internal energy to
the phases (Sec.~\ref{sec:closure}), so that $\sum_k\varepsilon_k^{n+1}=E^{n+1}-\tfrac12\rho^{n+1}(u^{n+1})^2$ holds exactly and the pressure is given
by the phase equations of state; no linearisation of the kinetic energy is involved.

\subsubsection{Local closure}
\label{sec:closure}
Backward Euler of the implicit part of \eqref{eq:model}, in which the volume fractions change only by relaxation and
phase $k$ does the pressure work $\alpha^*_kp_k\,s$, is written with the closure input $\alpha^{\rm tr}_k:=\alpha^*_k(1-s)$ and the fractions
$\omega_k=\alpha^{\rm tr}_k/\sum_j\alpha^{\rm tr}_j=\alpha^*_k$, so that $\alpha^{\rm tr}_k+\omega_ks=\alpha^*_k$ and $\omega_kp_ks$ is the pressure work. It gives
($\chi_1=+1$, $\chi_2=-1$, $\tau$ the implicit step)
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\alpha_k&=\alpha^{\rm tr}_k+\omega_ks+\chi_kR,\qquad R=\tau\big(\mu(p_1-p_2)+\mathcal M/\rho_I\big),\\
m_k&=m^{\rm tr}_k+\chi_k\tau\mathcal M,\\
\varepsilon_k&=\varepsilon^{\rm tr}_k-\omega_kp_k\,s-\chi_k\,\tau p_I\mu(p_1-p_2)+\chi_k\tau\big(\mathcal Q+e_I\mathcal M\big)+\Lambda_k(\delta),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:gibbs}
\end{equation}
so $\sum_k\alpha_k=1$ for every pressure. The last term distributes the defect between the non-conservative and the
conservative mixture internal energy,
\begin{equation}
\delta=(\rho e)^C-\sum_k\varepsilon^{\rm tr}_k+s\sum_k\omega_kp_k,\qquad \sum_k\Lambda_k(\delta)=\delta .
\label{eq:defect}
\end{equation}
By \eqref{eq:gibbs} $\sum_k\varepsilon_k=(\rho e)^C$ for every $\Lambda_k$ with $\sum_k\Lambda_k=\delta$, so the closed state is
mixture-energy consistent without a final projection, and $\delta$ does not depend on the relaxation terms, whose
contributions cancel between the phases. In smooth flow $\delta$ is a discretisation error; at a shock it is the dissipation
of the scheme, the kinetic energy that its numerical viscosity converts into internal energy. Its partition between the
phases is not fixed by the inviscid model and determines the limit shock relations of the scheme.

\paragraph{Shock relations and the weak-shock share}
We require each phase to follow its own Hugoniot \cite{SaurelGavrilyukRenaud2003,SPB2009},
\begin{equation}
e_k-e_{k0}+\bar p\,(v_k-v_{k0})=0,\qquad \bar p=\tfrac12(p_0+p_1),\qquad k=1,2,
\label{eq:II5}
\end{equation}
the shock relations also used for the exact solutions of Sec.~\ref{sec:results}. Consider a stationary shock profile of the
relaxed model with mass flux $j$, total normal stress $P=p-\sigma$ ($\sigma$ the viscous stress), the Rayleigh line
$P(v)=p_0+j^2(v_0-v)$ and the mixture energy balance $\mathrm de=-P\,\mathrm dv$. Each phase receives the heat $\mathrm dq_k$,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm de_k=-p\,\mathrm dv_k+\mathrm dq_k,\qquad Y_k\,\mathrm dq_k=\lambda_k\,\mathrm dq,\qquad \mathrm dq=(p-P)\,\mathrm dv\ge0,\qquad \textstyle\sum_k\lambda_k=1,
\label{eq:profile}
\end{equation}
with $\lambda_k=\partial\Lambda_k/\partial\delta$ the local share. Summing over $k$ recovers $\mathrm de=-P\,\mathrm dv$: whatever the share, the
mixture ends on its Hugoniot, and the share only decides the end volumes of the phases. Integrating
$\mathrm de_k=-p\,\mathrm dv_k+\mathrm dq_k$ over the profile and comparing with \eqref{eq:II5}, the end state satisfies \eqref{eq:II5} if and only if
\begin{equation}
Y_kq_k=\int_{\rm profile}(p-\bar p)\,\mathrm dv_k ,\qquad k=1,2,
\label{eq:heat}
\end{equation}
the heat received by phase $k$ is its compression work minus its Hugoniot work.

\begin{theorem}[Weak-shock share]\label{thm:weak}
Let $\Delta p=p_1-p_0\to0$, $K_k=\rho_kc_k^2$ and $\mathcal G_k=\tfrac12\big(1+\partial K_k/\partial p|_s\big)$ the fundamental derivative of phase $k$. Under
\eqref{eq:II5} the heat received by phase $k$ per unit mixture volume is
\begin{equation}
Y_k\rho\,q_k=\frac{\alpha_k\mathcal G_k}{6K_k^2}\,\Delta p^3+O(\Delta p^4),
\label{eq:weak_heat}
\end{equation}
the entropy production $T_k\Delta s_k$ of a single-phase shock of the same strength in phase $k$. Hence a share $\lambda_k$ that is
consistent with \eqref{eq:II5} for weak shocks is unique and equal to
$w_k=\big(\alpha_k\mathcal G_k/K_k^2\big)/\sum_j\alpha_j\mathcal G_j/K_j^2$; for stiffened and Noble--Abel stiffened gases $\mathcal G_k=(\gamma_k+1)/2$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
To the order required each phase is isentropic along a weak profile, and with $x=p-p_0$
\[
v_k(p)=v_{k0}\Big[1-\frac{x}{K_k}+\mathcal G_k\frac{x^2}{K_k^2}+O(x^3)\Big],\qquad
\mathrm dv_k=v_{k0}\Big[-\frac1{K_k}+2\mathcal G_k\frac{x}{K_k^2}+O(x^2)\Big]\mathrm dx .
\]
Inserting this in \eqref{eq:heat} with $p-\bar p=x-\tfrac12\Delta p$,
\[
Y_kq_k=v_{k0}Y_k\int_0^{\Delta p}\Big(x-\frac{\Delta p}2\Big)\Big[-\frac1{K_k}+2\mathcal G_k\frac{x}{K_k^2}\Big]\mathrm dx+O(\Delta p^4)
=v_{k0}Y_k\,\frac{2\mathcal G_k}{K_k^2}\,\frac{\Delta p^3}{12}+O(\Delta p^4),
\]
since the first term integrates to zero; with $\rho v_{k0}Y_k=\alpha_k$ this is \eqref{eq:weak_heat}. It equals $T_k\Delta s_k$ of the single-phase
Hugoniot of phase $k$ at the same $\Delta p$ \cite{LandauLifshitz}, which does not depend on the path. If $\lambda_k$ is a continuous function
of the state, then on a weak profile $\lambda_k=\lambda_k(U_0)+O(\Delta p)$, and \eqref{eq:heat} with \eqref{eq:weak_heat} requires $\lambda_k(U_0)=w_k(U_0)$.
\end{proof}

A share by mass, by $\alpha_k/(\gamma_k-1)$ or by the volume-change fraction $\alpha_k/K_k$ is therefore not consistent with
\eqref{eq:II5}. For strong shocks the heat \eqref{eq:heat} depends on the profile, so that no closed-form gap between the end states
reproduces it. Since the scheme distributes $\delta$ with a share that is constant while the shock crosses a cell, the
share it needs is the constant share for which the profile \eqref{eq:profile} ends at the state \eqref{eq:II5}. This share is
computed directly. The end volume of the mixture, $v_{\rm end}=\sum_kY_kv_{kH}$, with $v_{kH}$ the end volume of phase $k$ on its
Hugoniot \eqref{eq:II5}, does not depend on the share, so that $j^2=(p_1-p_0)/(v_0-v_{\rm end})$ and the profile is integrated over
the fixed interval $[v_0,v_{\rm end}]$. With $\lambda_1=c$, $\lambda_2=1-c$, \eqref{eq:profile} and $\sum_kY_kv_k=v$ give
\begin{equation}
\frac{\mathrm dp}{\mathrm dv}=\frac{\sum_kY_kr_k/b_k-1}{\sum_kY_ka_k/b_k},\qquad
\frac{\mathrm dv_1}{\mathrm dv}=\frac{r_1-a_1\,\mathrm dp/\mathrm dv}{b_1},\qquad
\frac{\mathrm dq}{\mathrm dv}=p-P(v),
\label{eq:profile_ode}
\end{equation}
with $a_k=\partial e_k/\partial p|_{v}$, $b_k=\partial e_k/\partial v|_{p}+p$ and $r_k=(\lambda_k/Y_k)(p-P)$; for SG and NASG phases
$a_k=(v_k-b_k)/(\gamma_k-1)$, $b_k=\gamma_k(p+\pi_k)/(\gamma_k-1)$, and for Mie--Gr\"uneisen phases $a_k=v_k/\Gamma_k$,
$b_k=(p-p_r)(1+1/\Gamma_k)+\rho_kp_r'/\Gamma_k$. The share $c$ is the root of $v_1(v_{\rm end};c)=v_{1H}$, which is increasing in $c$; it is
found by the Illinois method in ${\rm logit}(c)$, and \eqref{eq:profile_ode} is integrated by an adaptive Runge--Kutta--Fehlberg method
(Cash--Karp coefficients, relative tolerance $10^{-8}$). The same integration gives the mixture dissipation $q=\int(p-P)\,\mathrm dv$ per
unit mass, and the physical part of the dissipation of phase $k$ per unit volume is
\begin{equation}
Q_k=\lambda_k\,\rho\,q,\qquad Q=\sum_kQ_k=\rho\,q .
\label{eq:Qk}
\end{equation}
For weak shocks, $p_1-p_0<10^{-2}\min_kK_k$, the residual of the shooting is $O(\Delta p^3)$ and the limit of
Theorem~\ref{thm:weak} is used instead, $\lambda_k=w_k$, $q=\sum_kY_kv_k\mathcal G_k\Delta p^3/(6K_k^2)$. By construction the share
makes the profile \eqref{eq:profile} end on \eqref{eq:II5} for every shock strength and reduces to $w_k$ for weak shocks. In the scheme,
the numerical dissipation of the phase energies in the explicit fluxes also heats each phase directly, without passing
through $\delta$; the limit phase states behind a shock therefore deviate slightly from \eqref{eq:II5}, by a few tenths of a percent
in the phase densities for the strongest mixture shock of Sec.~\ref{sec:results}, a deviation that does not vanish under mesh
refinement.

The pre- and post-shock states are not local. The scheme takes them on its compact stencil ($\pm1$, the five-point
cross in 2D): $p_0=p_{\rm lo}<p_1=p_{\rm hi}$ are the extrema of the mixture-equilibrium pressure of $X^*$ on the stencil, the
pre-shock phase volumes $v_{k0}=\alpha_k/m_k$ are taken from the lowest-pressure cell of the stencil, and $Y_k$ and $\rho$ are those of
the cell itself. $Q_k$ is computed once per stage on $X^*$ and frozen. Since a shock passing through a cell deposits in
total the mixture dissipation $Q$, the part of $\delta$ above $Q$ is an error. The
physical part is restricted to compressing cells: for $\mathcal G_k>0$ every shock satisfying the entropy condition is
compressive (Bethe--Weyl \cite{MenikoffPlohr1989}), which holds for SG and NASG phases, $\mathcal G_k=(\gamma_k+1)/2$, and for the
Mie--Gr\"uneisen phases of Sec.~\ref{sec:results} over the range of states of the tests. Accordingly $Q_k\ne0$ only in cells where
the central difference of $u^*$ (and $v^*$) has negative sum.

\paragraph{Partition of the discretisation error}
The remainder of $\delta$ is a discretisation error. Let $E_k$ be the internal energy of phase $k$ above its admissibility
bound, for SG and NASG phases
\begin{equation}
E_k=\varepsilon^{\rm tr}_k-m^{\rm tr}_kq_k-(\alpha^{\rm tr}_k-m^{\rm tr}_kb_k)\pi_k=\frac{(p_k+\pi_k)(\alpha_k-m_kb_k)}{\gamma_k-1}\Big|_{\rm tr},
\label{eq:Ek}
\end{equation}
and for Mie--Gr\"uneisen phases the thermal energy above the cold curve, $\varepsilon_k-m_k\varepsilon_r$.

\begin{theorem}[Partition of the error]\label{thm:lambdaE}
Let both phases be admissible, $E_k>0$, and let the error be distributed at fixed phase volumes linearly,
$\Lambda_k=\lambda_k\delta$ with $\sum_k\lambda_k=1$. Both phases remain admissible, $E_k+\lambda_k\delta>0$, for every $\delta\in(-\sum_jE_j,0)$ if and only if
\begin{equation}
\lambda_k=\lambda^E_k=\frac{E_k}{\sum_jE_j} ,
\label{eq:lambdaE}
\end{equation}
and then both phases remain admissible for every $\delta>-\sum_jE_j$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $S=\sum_jE_j$. If $E_k+\lambda_k\delta>0$ for all $\delta\in(-S,0)$, letting $\delta\to-S$ gives $\lambda_k\le E_k/S$ for $k=1,2$. Summing,
$1=\sum_k\lambda_k\le\sum_kE_k/S=1$, so every inequality is an equality. Conversely, $E_k+\lambda^E_k\delta=E_k(1+\delta/S)>0$ for every $\delta>-S$.
For $\delta\le-S$ no admissible partition exists, since $\sum_k(E_k+\Lambda_k)=S+\delta\le0$.
\end{proof}

The share $\lambda^E_k$ changes the energy of both phases above their bounds by the same relative amount: for SG and NASG phases at
fixed volumes, $p_k+\pi_k$ is multiplied by $1+\delta/\sum_jE_j$.
With the pressure work the admissible margin becomes $E_k+\lambda_k(\delta-s(p+\pi_k))$; the theorem holds exactly for $s=0$, and the
scheme uses $E_k$ of the transported state, which differs from the margin after the compression work by $O(s)$. When both
phases are at their bounds ($\sum_jE_j=0$) the error is distributed by mass. Altogether
\begin{equation}
\Lambda_k(\delta)=\begin{cases}\min(\delta,Q)\,\dfrac{Q_k}{Q}+\big(\delta-\min(\delta,Q)\big)\lambda^E_k,&\delta\ge0,\\[4pt]
\delta\,\lambda^E_k,&\delta<0,\end{cases}\qquad Q=\sum_kQ_k ,
\label{eq:Lambda}
\end{equation}
a continuous, piecewise affine function of $\delta$ with break points $\delta=0$ and $\delta=Q$. On the active piece
$\Lambda_k=\lambda_k\delta+\xi_k$, with $\xi_k=Q_k-Q\lambda^E_k$ on the third piece and $\xi_k=0$ otherwise. With a common pressure
$\delta=D_0+ps$, $D_0=(\rho e)^C-\sum_k\varepsilon^{\rm tr}_k$. The NASG $p$ closure selects the active piece at every trial pressure of its
root solve, so that $\sum_k\varepsilon_k=(\rho e)^C$ holds exactly at every $p$; the other closures that use $\Lambda_k$ (finite $\mu$
and $\theta$, Mie--Gr\"uneisen phases) select it at the current Newton iterate of the cell pressure. The residual of the
pressure system is therefore continuous and piecewise smooth in $P$.

\subsubsection{Relaxation closures}
\label{sec:relaxation}
The relaxation terms are not split from the transport: they enter the closure \eqref{eq:gibbs}, which for each set of
rates reduces to one scalar equation in the cell pressure, $\Phi(p)=0$. We follow the ordering of time scales of Pelanti
\cite{Pelanti2022}: $\mu=\infty$ if $\theta>0$ or $\nu>0$, and $\theta=\infty$ if $\nu>0$. The closure returns the phase states and the
mixture pressure $\bar p^{\rm cl}=\sum_k\alpha_kp_k$.

\paragraph{Instantaneous mechanical relaxation: $p$ closure ($\mu=\infty$, $\theta=\nu=0$)}
With $p_1=p_2=p_I=p$ and $\omega_ks+\chi_kR=\alpha_k-\alpha^{\rm tr}_k$, \eqref{eq:gibbs} becomes the discrete Gibbs relation
\begin{equation}
\varepsilon_k=\varepsilon^{\rm tr}_k-p\,(\alpha_k-\alpha^{\rm tr}_k)+\Lambda_k(\delta),\qquad \delta=D_0+ps ,
\label{eq:pclosure}
\end{equation}
independent of $\omega_k$ and $p_I$. By \eqref{eq:eps_nasg}, on the active piece of \eqref{eq:Lambda},
\begin{equation}
\alpha_k(p)=\frac{(\gamma_k-1)\big[A_k+B_kp\big]+(p+\gamma_k\pi_k)m_kb_k}{\gamma_k(p+\pi_k)},\qquad
A_k=\varepsilon^{\rm tr}_k-m_kq_k+\lambda_kD_0+\xi_k,\quad B_k=\alpha^{\rm tr}_k+\lambda_ks ,
\label{eq:alpha_p}
\end{equation}
and $\Phi(p)=\sum_k\alpha_k(p)-1$. Here
\[
\alpha_k'(p)=-\frac{(\gamma_k-1)N_k}{\gamma_k(p+\pi_k)^2},\qquad N_k=A_k-(B_k-m_kb_k)\pi_k .
\]
If the transported state of phase $k$ is admissible, $\varepsilon^{\rm tr}_k-m_kq_k-(\alpha^{\rm tr}_k-m_kb_k)\pi_k=E_k>0$, and $N_k=E_k+\lambda_k(D_0-s\pi_k)+\xi_k$. As
$p\downarrow\max_k(-\pi_k)$, $\Phi\to+\infty$; as $p\to\infty$, $\Phi\to\sum_k\big((\gamma_k-1)B_k+m_kb_k\big)/\gamma_k-1<\sum_kB_k-1=0$, since $m_kb_k<B_k$ and $\sum_kB_k=1-s+s=1$. A
root therefore exists; it is unique when $N_k>0$ for both phases, in which case $\Phi$ is decreasing. In general the root is
bracketed on $(\max_k(-\pi_k),\infty)$ and found by a safeguarded Newton iteration, and admissibility is checked at the
solution. For a Mie--Gr\"uneisen phase \eqref{eq:pclosure} with \eqref{eq:mg} gives $\alpha_k(p)$ as the root of
\[
f_k(\alpha)=m_k\varepsilon_r(m_k/\alpha)+\alpha\,\frac{p-p_r(m_k/\alpha)}{\Gamma_k}+p\,\alpha-A_k-B_kp=0,\qquad
f_k'(\alpha)=\frac{\rho_kc_k^2}{\Gamma_k}>0 ,
\]
($A_k=\varepsilon^{\rm tr}_k+\lambda_kD_0+\xi_k$), unique when $c_k^2>0$ and found by a bracketed Newton iteration in $\ln\alpha$; the outer equation is solved
by a bracketed Newton iteration, without a global monotonicity result.

\paragraph{Finite mechanical relaxation ($\mu<\infty$, $\theta=\nu=0$)}
Let $a_k=\alpha^{\rm tr}_k+\omega_ks$, so that $\alpha_1=a_1+R$, $\alpha_2=a_2-R$, and $p_I=w_1p_1+w_2p_2$ with $w_1=Z_2/(Z_1+Z_2)$, the acoustic impedances
$Z_k=\rho_kc_k$ being frozen on $X^*$. For given $R$, \eqref{eq:gibbs} with \eqref{eq:eps_nasg} is the $2\times2$ linear system
\begin{equation}
\Big(\frac{\alpha_k-m_kb_k}{\gamma_k-1}+\omega_ks\Big)p_k+\chi_kR\,(w_1p_1+w_2p_2)-\lambda_ks\,(\omega_1p_1+\omega_2p_2)
=\varepsilon^{\rm tr}_k-m_kq_k-\frac{\gamma_k\pi_k(\alpha_k-m_kb_k)}{\gamma_k-1}+\lambda_kD_0+\xi_k
\label{eq:twopressure}
\end{equation}
for $(p_1,p_2)$, $k=1,2$, with $\delta=D_0+s\sum_k\omega_kp_k$. $R$ solves the scalar equation
\[
\mathcal F(R)=R-\tau\mu\big(p_1(R)-p_2(R)\big)=0 .
\]
Increasing $R$ increases $\alpha_1$, decreases $p_1$ and increases $p_2$, so $\mathcal F$ is increasing on its admissible interval and
the root is unique. Relaxation reduces $|p_1-p_2|$, so the root lies between $0$ and $R_1=\tau\mu(p_1-p_2)|_{R=0}$; if $R_1$ is not
admissible, $\mathcal F$ tends to infinity with the correct sign at the singularity of \eqref{eq:twopressure}, and bisection towards
$0$ gives an admissible bracket. For $\mu=0$, $R=0$: each phase does the Gibbs work at its own pressure,
$\varepsilon_k-\varepsilon^{\rm tr}_k=-p_k(\alpha_k-\alpha^{\rm tr}_k)+\Lambda_k$, and the volumes change by $\alpha_k=\alpha^{\rm tr}_k/(1-s)$.

\paragraph{Instantaneous thermal relaxation: $pT$ closure ($\mu=\theta=\infty$, $\nu=0$)}
The masses are $m_k=m^{\rm tr}_k$. The mixture energy $\sum_km_ke_k(p,T)=(\rho e)^C$ gives the temperature explicitly, and
\begin{equation}
T(p)=\frac{(\rho e)^C-\sum_km_kq_k}{\sum_km_kc_{vk}\,\frac{p+\gamma_k\pi_k}{p+\pi_k}},\qquad
\Phi(p)=\sum_km_kv_k\big(p,T(p)\big)-1 ,
\label{eq:pTclosure}
\end{equation}
independent of $\alpha^{\rm tr}_k$, $s$ and $\Lambda_k$: the volume fractions are dependent variables, as in the four-equation $pT$ model.
The admissible root is unique \cite{PelantiShyue2014}; the closure returns $\alpha_k=m_kv_k$, $\varepsilon_k=m_ke_k$ and $\bar p^{\rm cl}=p$.

\paragraph{Finite thermal relaxation ($\mu=\infty$, $\theta<\infty$, $\nu=0$)}
The heat $\Delta Q=\tau\theta(T_2-T_1)$ is added to \eqref{eq:pclosure}, $\varepsilon_1\mathrel{+}=\Delta Q$, $\varepsilon_2\mathrel{-}=\Delta Q$. By \eqref{eq:alpha_p}, $\alpha_k$ is linear in
$\Delta Q$, $\alpha_k=\alpha^0_k(p)+\chi_k(\gamma_k-1)\Delta Q/\big(\gamma_k(p+\pi_k)\big)$, with $\alpha^0_k$ the volumes of the $p$ closure, and the
temperatures $T_k=(\alpha_k/m_k-b_k)(p+\pi_k)/\big((\gamma_k-1)c_{vk}\big)$ change by
\[
T_k=T^0_k(p)+\chi_k\frac{\Delta Q}{C_{pk}},\qquad C_{pk}=\gamma_kc_{vk}m_k ,
\]
the heat at constant pressure being the enthalpy increase. Hence, in closed form,
\begin{equation}
\Delta Q(p)=\frac{\tau\theta\,\big(T^0_2(p)-T^0_1(p)\big)}{1+\tau\theta\,(C_{p1}^{-1}+C_{p2}^{-1})},
\label{eq:dQ}
\end{equation}
and $\Phi(p)=\sum_k\alpha_k(p)-1$; $\theta=0$ gives the $p$ closure.

\paragraph{Finite chemical relaxation ($\mu=\theta=\infty$, $\nu<\infty$)}
The density $\rho=\sum_km^{\rm tr}_k$ is fixed. For given $p$ and vapour mass fraction $Y=Y_2$,
\begin{equation}
T(p,Y)=\frac{(\rho e)^C/\rho-(1-Y)q_1-Yq_2}{(1-Y)c_{v1}\phi_1+Yc_{v2}\phi_2},\quad \phi_k=\frac{p+\gamma_k\pi_k}{p+\pi_k},\qquad
\mathcal R(Y)=\rho Y-m^{\rm tr}_2+\tau\nu\,(g_2-g_1)\big(p,T(p,Y)\big)=0 .
\label{eq:nuclosure}
\end{equation}
At fixed energy $T$ decreases with $Y$ ($e_2(p,T)>e_1(p,T)$) and $\partial_T(g_2-g_1)=-(s_2-s_1)<0$, so
$\mathcal R'(Y)=\rho+\tau\nu(s_2-s_1)|\partial_YT|>0$ and $Y$ is unique on its admissible interval
$[Y_{\min},\min(1-Y_{\min},(e-q_1)/(q_2-q_1))]$, the upper bound keeping $T>0$. If $\mathcal R(Y)$ does not change sign on the interval, $Y$ takes
the end value: complete condensation or evaporation. Then $\Phi(p)=\rho\sum_kY_kv_k(p,T)-1$. A state-dependent rate (Sec.~\ref{sec:res_bartak})
is evaluated in each cell from its $pT$-equilibrium state at the beginning of the step and frozen over the stages.

\paragraph{Instantaneous chemical relaxation: $pTg$ closure ($\mu=\theta=\nu=\infty$)}
$T=T_{\rm sat}(p)$, $Y$ follows from the energy and $\Phi(p)=\rho\sum_kY_kv_k-1$; $Y$ is clamped to $[Y_{\min},1-Y_{\min}]$, $Y_{\min}=10^{-10}$, with $T$
then from the energy, which coincides with the end-value branch of the finite-rate closure, so $\Phi$ is continuous. For
$p\ge p_c$ the cell is single-phase, the phase being chosen by comparing $1/\rho$ with $v_1(p_c,T_{\rm sat}(p_c))$.

\paragraph{Activation and projection}
The interfacial quantities $\rho_I$, $e_I$ do not appear in any closure. Thermal and chemical relaxation are active in interface cells,
$\epsilon_I\le\alpha_1\le1-\epsilon_I$ with $\epsilon_I=10^{-4}$. Mass transfer is activated where the temperature of the $pT$-equilibrium
state exceeds $T_{\rm sat}(p)$, or, when thermal and chemical relaxation act together, where a phase temperature of the
mechanical-equilibrium state exceeds $T_{\rm sat}(p)$ (the levels of Sec.~\ref{sec:results}). Activation is decided once per
time step on $U^n$. An instantaneous relaxation activated inside a stage is an $O(1)$ jump of the state, which the stage
combination of the IMEX scheme would extrapolate; for the instantaneous $pT$ and $pTg$ closures $U^n$ is therefore projected
onto their equilibrium manifold (the same closure with $s=0$, conserving $m_k$ or $\rho$, $\rho\mathbf u$ and $E$), and a cell whose
projection has no admissible solution uses the mechanical closure for that step. The closure type is frozen over the
stages.

\subsubsection{Solution of the pressure system}
\label{sec:pressure_system}
With the face velocity and face pressure of Sec.~\ref{sec:dissipation}, the fully discrete counterpart of
\eqref{eq:global_semi} is the system
\begin{equation}
G_{i,j}(P)\equiv\bar p^{\rm cl}_{i,j}\big(s_{i,j},X^{\rm tr}_{i,j},(\rho e)^C_{i,j};\ \mu,\theta,\nu\big)-P_{i,j}=0
\label{eq:global}
\end{equation}
for the cell pressures $P_{i,j}$. Its residual in cell $(i,j)$ depends only on the pressures of the cell and its four
neighbours, and the nonlinearity, which carries the equations of state and the relaxation, is local to the cell, as in
the mildly nonlinear systems of \cite{DumbserCasulli2016,CasulliZanolli2012}. The Jacobian is a three-point (five-point in 2D) matrix with negative diagonal and non-negative off-diagonal entries.
The system is solved by Newton's method with a finite-difference Jacobian and a backtracking line search that also
requires every local closure to be admissible. The unknown is the pressure perturbation $P-P_{\rm ref}$, with $P_{\rm ref}$ the
minimum of the initial mixture pressure. The residual of cell $(i,j)$ is scaled by $N_{i,j}=|P_{i,j}|+K_{i,j}+P_{E,i,j}+1$, where
$K$ is the Wood bulk modulus $\rho c_p^2$ of $X^*$ and $P_{E}=\sum_k\big(|\varepsilon^*_k|+|m^*_kq_k|\big)/\sum_k\partial\varepsilon_k/\partial p|_{\alpha_k,m_k}$ the
pressure scale of the energy round-off; the natural scale of $P$ is $\sigma_{i,j}=N_{i,j}/D_{i,j}$ with
$D_{i,j}=1+(K_{i,j}+P_{E,i,j})\sum_f(\tau/h_f)\beta_f$, the scale of $\partial G_{i,j}/\partial P_{i,j}$ ($\beta_f$ the pressure coefficient of $u_f$, Sec.~\ref{sec:fully}),
and the finite-difference increments are $10^{-7}\sigma$. The linear systems are solved by the Thomas algorithm for
non-periodic one-dimensional problems and otherwise by BiCGStab with a Jacobi plus constant-coarse-space
preconditioner (relative tolerance $10^{-13}$), which makes the iteration independent of the Mach number. The
iteration stops when $\max|G_{i,j}|/N_{i,j}<10^{-13}$ or when the Newton step satisfies $\max|\delta P_{i,j}|/(|P_{i,j}-P_{\rm ref}|+\sigma_{i,j})<10^{-13}$;
if the line search cannot reduce the residual, the solution is accepted when every $|G_{i,j}|$ lies within $10^{-13}N_{i,j}$ plus the
round-off floor of the closure, at most 60 iterations being allowed.

\subsection{Implicit dissipation}
\label{sec:dissipation}
The implicit terms are discretised by central differences on the faces. The central operator does not damp the
odd--even velocity mode, and the material-speed dissipation of \eqref{eq:rusanov} does not act on pressure jumps, so that,
as in \cite{DumbserCasulli2016,BoscheriPareschi2021}, numerical dissipation is added to the implicit step by means of the Laplace operator
\begin{equation}
\mathbb L(\kappa,q)_{i,j}=\frac1{\Delta x^2}\Big(\kappa_{i+1/2,j}(q_{i+1,j}-q_{i,j})-\kappa_{i-1/2,j}(q_{i,j}-q_{i-1,j})\Big)
+\frac1{\Delta y^2}\Big(\kappa_{i,j+1/2}(q_{i,j+1}-q_{i,j})-\kappa_{i,j-1/2}(q_{i,j}-q_{i,j-1})\Big),
\label{eq:laplace}
\end{equation}
applied to the pressure and to the velocity only, the acoustic characteristic variables: the volumes, partial densities and
phase energies already carry the material-speed dissipation of the explicit step, and a dissipation proportional to the
sound speed on them would smear contacts by a factor $1/M$ more than the material dissipation.

\paragraph{Face velocity and face pressure}
On the face $(i+\tfrac12,j)$, with $\rho^*_{i,j}=\sum_km^*_{k,i,j}$, $u^*_{i,j}=(\rho u)^*_{i,j}/\rho^*_{i,j}$ and $Z_{i,j}=\rho^*_{i,j}c_{f,i,j}$ (frozen
sound speed on $X^*$), the face velocity is the average of the cell velocities plus the pressure acceleration of the two
adjacent half cells, the face pressure is the density-weighted average that conserves momentum, and each carries the
diffusive flux of the other acoustic variable:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
u_f&=\bar u^*_f-\frac{\tau(P_{i+1,j}-P_{i,j})}{\bar\rho_f\Delta x}-\frac{\kappa^P_f}{\Delta x}\big(P_{i+1,j}-P_{i,j}\big),\\
p_f&=\frac{\rho^*_{i+1,j}P_{i,j}+\rho^*_{i,j}P_{i+1,j}}{\rho^*_{i,j}+\rho^*_{i+1,j}}-\frac{\kappa^u_f}{\Delta x}\,D^*_f,
\end{aligned}\qquad
\kappa^P_f=\frac{\varphi_f\psi^P_f\,\Delta x}{Z_{i,j}+Z_{i+1,j}},\qquad \kappa^u_f=\frac{\varphi_f\psi^u_f\,Z_{i,j}Z_{i+1,j}\,\Delta x}{Z_{i,j}+Z_{i+1,j}},
\label{eq:faces}
\end{equation}
with $\bar u^*_f=\tfrac12(u^*_{i,j}+u^*_{i+1,j})$, $\bar\rho_f=\tfrac12(\rho^*_{i,j}+\rho^*_{i+1,j})$ and, in one dimension, $D^*_f=u^*_{i+1}-u^*_i$. Without the
factors $\varphi_f\psi_f$ the two diffusive terms are the upwind terms of the acoustic Riemann solution. In the momentum update the
face pressure contributes $\tau\,\mathbb L(\kappa^u,u)$, a bulk viscosity with coefficient $\tfrac12\rho c\Delta x$ for equal impedances; the
divergence of the face velocity enters the strain, the transport and the energy flux, and in the pressure equation
\eqref{eq:wave} it contributes $\tau K\,\mathbb L(\kappa^P,P)$, with $K\kappa^P=\tfrac12c\Delta x$ for equal impedances. Both coefficients are the acoustic
eigenvalue times $\Delta x$, and both terms are implicit, so that they do not restrict the time step \eqref{eq:cfl}. In two dimensions the
normal velocity difference is replaced by $\Delta x$ times the mean of the two vertex divergences of the face,
\begin{equation}
D^*_{i+1/2,j}=\Delta x\,\tfrac12\big(\mathrm{div}_{i+1/2,j+1/2}+\mathrm{div}_{i+1/2,j-1/2}\big),\quad
\mathrm{div}_{i+1/2,j+1/2}=\frac{u_{i+1,j}+u_{i+1,j+1}-u_{i,j}-u_{i,j+1}}{2\Delta x}+\frac{v_{i,j+1}+v_{i+1,j+1}-v_{i,j}-v_{i+1,j}}{2\Delta y},
\label{eq:vertex_div}
\end{equation}
so that the velocity term acts on the divergence only and leaves divergence-free motion, such as vortices, untouched;
for $y$-independent data it reduces to the one-dimensional difference.

The face sweeps $u_f\tau$ during the step, of which the explicit flux has already swept $\bar u^e_f\tau$, $\bar u^e_f=\tfrac12(u^L+u^R)$; the
incremental flux supplies the difference from the upwind side of $u_f$, with the reconstructed face values of
\eqref{eq:rusanov} as donors,
\begin{equation}
I_f[X]=X^L_f\big[(u_f)^+-(\bar u^e_f)^+\big]+X^R_f\big[(u_f)^--(\bar u^e_f)^-\big],\qquad X\in\{m_k,\varepsilon_k\}.
\label{eq:increment}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Acoustic weight}
Backward Euler already damps acoustic waves: for linear acoustics, one step with \eqref{eq:faces} has, at low wave
numbers, the effective viscosity $\tfrac12c^2\tau+\varphi\,\tfrac12c\Delta x=\tfrac12c\Delta x(\nu+\varphi)$, $\nu=c\tau/\Delta x$. Requiring that each step carries at least
the upwind viscosity $\tfrac12c\Delta x$ on resolved acoustic waves, and no more, gives
\begin{equation}
\varphi_f=\Big(1-\frac{\tau\min(c_{f,i,j},c_{f,i+1,j})}{\Delta x}\Big)^+ :
\label{eq:weight}
\end{equation}
for $\tau\to0$ \eqref{eq:faces} gives the acoustic Godunov face states, and for $c\tau\ge\Delta x$, in particular in the low Mach number
regime, the Laplace terms vanish, so that they add no dissipation that does not vanish with the mesh at fixed Mach
number. The frozen sound speed is used since $\rho c_f\ge\rho c_{\rm eq}$ for every relaxed closure \cite{FlattenLund2011}, so that the dissipation is
never smaller than that of the relaxed waves. Where $X^*$ is not admissible, $c_f=\infty$ and the face carries no Laplace terms.

\paragraph{Second-order factors}
The factors $\psi\in[0,1]$ evaluate the Laplace terms on the reconstructed face states, $\psi D=X^R-X^L$ for a face jump $D$.
The pressure is a function of the volume-transported quantities and the velocity, whose reconstruction is scaled by
$\phi_s=\min(\hat\phi_{i,j},\hat\phi_{i+1,j})$, with $\hat\phi$ the minimum of the shared factor \eqref{eq:shared_limiter} and of the smoothness ratios
(minmod over central slope) of the velocity components; with the minmod slope of $P$ this gives
\begin{equation}
\psi^P_f=1-\phi_s\big(1-\psi^{\rm mm}_f[P]\big),\qquad \psi^u_f=\psi^{\rm mm}_f[u],\qquad
\psi^{\rm mm}_f[X]=1-\frac{\sigma^{\rm mm}_{i,j}[X]+\sigma^{\rm mm}_{i+1,j}[X]}{2D},
\label{eq:psi}
\end{equation}
the velocity-jump term acting on the separately reconstructed velocity. The slopes of $P$ are those of the pressure field
of the previous Newton initial guess (the pressure of the previous stage), and those of $u$ are taken on $u^*$, so that
the factors are frozen during the solution of the stage; with unlimited central slopes $\sigma$ are the central slopes,
clipped to $\psi\in[0,1]$. Both terms are first order at a local
extremum of any quantity whose flux they modify, in particular at contacts, and second order where the solution is
smooth (the LED criterion of Jameson \cite{Jameson1995}).

\subsection{Well-balanced property}
\label{sec:wb}
\begin{proposition}[Contacts and temperature consistency]\label{prop:wb}
(i) A state with uniform $u$ and $p$ and arbitrary $\alpha_k$, $\rho_k$ is preserved exactly by the scheme with mechanical
relaxation; with thermal or chemical relaxation active, this holds if in addition $T_1=T_2$.
(ii) If each phase has uniform $p$ and $T_k$, transport does not change $\rho_k$, $e_k$, $T_k$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
(i) By \eqref{eq:eps_nasg} $\varepsilon_k=c_k(p)\alpha_k+(q_k-b_kc_k(p))m_k$ in every cell; the shared limiter keeps this relation on the faces,
and since with uniform velocity $\delta_x[\bar u]=0$, the path-conservative update \eqref{eq:alpha_explicit} transports $\alpha_k$ with the same
linear Rusanov operator \eqref{eq:rusanov} as $m_k$ and $\varepsilon_k$, it holds for $X^*$. With $P\equiv p$ all terms of \eqref{eq:faces} beyond the averages
vanish, $u_f=u$, $p_f=p$, the increments vanish, $s=\delta=0$, and $\alpha_k=\alpha^*_k$ solves the closure at $p$, so $P\equiv p$ solves
\eqref{eq:global} (with $T_1=T_2$ the projection onto the $pT$ manifold leaves the state unchanged). (ii) $m_k$ and $\varepsilon_k$ are proportional to $\alpha_k$ and all three are transported by the same
linear operator.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Asymptotic preserving property}
\label{sec:ap}
\paragraph{Relaxation limits}
\begin{theorem}[Relaxation limits]\label{thm:limits}
Assume that the local closures have non-singular Jacobians at their solutions and that the Jacobian of
\eqref{eq:global} is non-singular. (i) As $\mu$, $\theta$ or $\nu\to\infty$ the stage solution converges to that of the corresponding
equilibrium closure with an error $O(1/(\tau\mu))$, $O(1/(\tau\theta))$, $O(1/(\tau\nu))$ (for $\nu$, where $Y$ is not clamped).
(ii) For a vanishing rate the closure reduces exactly to the scheme with that relaxation switched off: $\mu=0$ gives
the frozen two-pressure scheme, $\theta=0$ the $p$ closure and $\nu=0$ the $pT$ closure.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
(i) For $\mu$, with $\kappa=1/(\tau\mu)$ the closure reads $p_1-p_2=\kappa R$; at $\kappa=0$ it is the $p$ closure, and the
implicit function theorem gives an $O(\kappa)$ difference for each cell; the non-singular global Jacobian carries it to the
discrete solution. The $\theta$ and $\nu$ limits follow in the same way from $T_1-T_2=O(1/(\tau\theta))$ and
$|g_2-g_1|=O(1/(\tau\nu))$. The tableau of Sec.~\ref{sec:time} is stiffly accurate and L-stable, so every stage lies on
the equilibrium manifold. (ii) $\mu=0$, $\theta=0$, $\nu=0$ give $R=0$, $\Delta Q=0$, $Y=Y^{\rm tr}$.
\end{proof}
The limit schemes are consistent discretisations of the relaxed models. For the $p$ closure, linearising
\eqref{eq:pclosure} with $\delta=0$, each phase is isentropic at the common pressure, $\mathrm dp=-K_k\,\mathrm d\alpha_k/\alpha_k$, and $\sum_k\mathrm d\alpha_k=s$ gives
\[
\mathrm d\alpha_k=s\,\frac{\alpha_k/K_k}{\sum_j\alpha_j/K_j},\qquad K_k=\rho_kc_k^2 .
\]
With $s=\Delta t\,\partial_xu$ and the transport part of $\alpha_k$ this is
\[
\partial_t\alpha_1+u\,\partial_x\alpha_1=\alpha_1\Big(\frac{\rho c_p^2}{\rho_1c_1^2}-1\Big)\partial_xu=\frac{\alpha_1\alpha_2(\rho_2c_2^2-\rho_1c_1^2)}{\alpha_1\rho_2c_2^2+\alpha_2\rho_1c_1^2}\,\partial_xu ,
\]
the volume-fraction equation of the Kapila model \cite{Kapila2001,Pelanti2022}, $c_p$ being the Wood sound speed. In the same way the $pT$
and $pTg$ closures are the four-equation and homogeneous equilibrium models, whose volume fractions are dependent variables.

\paragraph{Low-Mach limit of the mixture}
Let $u_0$, $c_0$ and $\rho_0$ be a reference velocity, sound speed and density and $M=u_0/c_0$. With impermeable
boundaries and well-prepared data, pressure variations are of the size of the inertial terms, $\rho_0u_0^2=M^2\rho_0c_0^2$,
so that $\bar p=\bar p_{(0)}+M^2\bar p_{(2)}+\dots$ with $\bar p_{(2)}=O(\rho_0c_0^2)$. In the momentum equation the gradient of
$\bar p_{(0)}$ would exceed the inertia by $M^{-2}$, hence $\partial_x\bar p_{(0)}=0$; integrating the pressure equation of the
mixture, $\partial_t\bar p+u\,\partial_x\bar p+\rho c_p^2\,\partial_xu=0$, over the domain then gives $\bar p_{(0)}={\rm const}$ and
$\partial_xu_{(0)}=0$: the incompressible limit of the mixture.
\begin{proposition}[Formal low-Mach limit]\label{prop:lowmach}
For fixed $\Delta t$, $\Delta x$ and well-prepared data $P=P_0+M^2P_2$, formally as $M\to0$: the face velocities are
discretely divergence-free to $O(M^2)$, $P_2$ solves the discrete Poisson equation
$\delta\cdot\big(\bar u^*_f-\tau\,\delta P_2/(\bar\rho_f\Delta x)\big)=O(M^2)$, the Laplace terms of \eqref{eq:faces} vanish ($\varphi=0$ once
$M<a_{ii}C$), and the limit scheme is explicit convection plus a projection, with a time step independent of $M$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Scale pressures by $\rho_0c_0^2$, so that the pressure coefficient of the face velocity, $\tau/(\bar\rho_f\Delta x)$ in dimensional form, carries a
factor $M^{-2}$ relative to the inertia. Linearising the closure about $X^*$, the pressure equation \eqref{eq:global} reads
\[
P_i-P^*_i=-K^W_i\,s_i+\dots,\qquad s_i=\frac\tau{\Delta x}\,\delta[u_f]_i,\qquad u_f=\bar u^*_f-\frac{\tau}{M^2\bar\rho_f}\frac{\delta P}{\Delta x}+\dots,
\]
with $P^*$ the mixture pressure of $X^*$ and $K^W=\rho c_p^2=O(1)$ the Wood bulk modulus. Insert $P=P_0+M^2P_2$ with $P_0$ constant. The term
$K^W_is_i$ must be $O(M^2)$ because $P_i-P^*_i=O(M^2)$ for well-prepared data, so $\delta[u_f]_i=O(M^2)$: the face velocities are discretely
divergence-free. The pressure gradient in $u_f$ is $\delta P/(M^2\Delta x)=\delta P_2/\Delta x$, and the order-one balance gives the compact Poisson
equation of the statement, i.e.\ a projection of the explicit face velocities. In the momentum update $p_f$ is a weighted average of
$P$, $P_0$ cancels in $\delta p_f$, and the cell velocity is corrected by $\tau\,\delta\overline{P_2}/\Delta x$. The acoustic Courant number is
$c\tau/\Delta x=a_{ii}C\,c/\max|u|=O(1/M)$, so $\varphi=(1-c\tau/\Delta x)^+=0$ once $M<a_{ii}C$. The explicit Rusanov dissipation uses the material
speed and the closures return states on the equilibrium manifold, both independently of $M$. The limit scheme is therefore
explicit convection followed by a projection of the face velocities, with a time step set by \eqref{eq:cfl}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Second-order time discretisation}
\label{sec:time}
The scheme of Secs.~\ref{sec:advective}--\ref{sec:dissipation} is first order in time. Second order is obtained with
the semi-implicit IMEX Runge--Kutta schemes of Boscarino, Filbet and Russo \cite{BoscarinoFilbetRusso2016}, for which the
time linearisation introduced by the splitting, explicit velocity in the advective fluxes and implicit velocity
increment, gives the semi-implicit structure directly. The semi-discrete system is written in autonomous form as
\begin{equation}
\partial_tU=\mathcal H\big(U_E(t),U_I(t)\big),
\label{eq:autonomous}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal H$ is the spatial discretisation of the split system, $U_E$ the state on which the advective sub-system
is evaluated and $U_I$ the state of the pressure--relaxation sub-system. An IMEX Runge--Kutta scheme
\cite{Ascher1997,PareschiRusso2005} is defined by a pair of Butcher tableaux $(\tilde A,\tilde{\mathbf b},\tilde{\mathbf c})$ and
$(A,\mathbf b,\mathbf c)$, $\tilde A$ strictly lower triangular and $A$ lower triangular. We use the stiffly accurate, L-stable
LSDIRK2(2,2,2) scheme \cite{BoscarinoFilbetRusso2016,Avgerinos2019},
\begin{equation}
\begin{array}{c|cc}0&0&0\\ \hat c&\hat c&0\\\hline &1-\beta&\beta\end{array}\qquad
\begin{array}{c|cc}\beta&\beta&0\\ 1&1-\beta&\beta\\\hline &1-\beta&\beta\end{array},\qquad \beta=1-\tfrac1{\sqrt2},\quad \hat c=\tfrac1{2\beta}.
\label{eq:lsdirk}
\end{equation}
With $U^n_E=U^n_I=U^n$, the stages $i=1,2$ are
\begin{equation}
U_{E,i}=U^n+\Delta t\sum_{j<i}\tilde a_{ij}K_j,\qquad \tilde U_{I,i}=U^n+\Delta t\sum_{j<i}a_{ij}K_j,\qquad
K_i=\mathcal H\big(U_{E,i},\ \tilde U_{I,i}+\Delta t\,a_{ii}K_i\big).
\label{eq:stages}
\end{equation}
Each stage is one application of the first-order scheme: the explicit fluxes \eqref{eq:explicit} are evaluated on
$U_{E,i}$, the update starts from $\tilde U_{I,i}$ instead of $U^n$, and the implicit step is $\tau=a_{ii}\Delta t$, giving the stage
value $U_i$ and $K_i=(U_i-\tilde U_{I,i})/\tau$. Since $\mathbf b$ is the last row of $A$, $U^{n+1}=U_2$. The first step uses the
acoustic Courant number $0.4$, as in \cite{BattistiBoscheri2025}, and no step may exceed $1.2$ times the previous one;
the steps limited by this rule before the time step first reaches \eqref{eq:cfl} use one backward-Euler stage. If in some stage
the explicit predictor has a non-positive density or volume in a cell, a local closure has no root with both phases
admissible (the cone of Sec.~\ref{sec:advective} for stiffened and Noble--Abel stiffened gases, $c_k^2>0$ for Mie--Gr\"uneisen
phases), or the Newton iteration for \eqref{eq:global} does not converge, the step is repeated with half the time step,
with the same scheme. With a prescribed fixed time step the start-up rule is not applied.

\subsection{Fully discrete scheme in two dimensions}
\label{sec:fully}
One stage of the scheme, with the explicit state $Q^e=U_{E,i}$, the update base $Q^s=\tilde U_{I,i}$ and the implicit step
$\tau=a_{ii}\Delta t$ ($Q^e=Q^s=U^n$, $\tau=\Delta t$ for a first-order step), consists of the following steps; $r_x=\tau/\Delta x$,
$r_y=\tau/\Delta y$, and $u_n$, $u_t$ denote the normal and tangential velocity on a face. When thermal or chemical relaxation is
used, before the first stage of a step the relaxation closure of every cell is activated and $U^n$ is projected as in
Sec.~\ref{sec:relaxation}.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Explicit advective sub-system.} The face values are reconstructed on $Q^e$ by \eqref{eq:shared_limiter} with the cap \eqref{eq:phi_cap}, and every
transported quantity is advanced with the Rusanov fluxes \eqref{eq:rusanov},
\[
X^*_{i,j}=X^s_{i,j}-r_x\,\delta_x\big[F^e[X]\big]_{i,j}-r_y\,\delta_y\big[G^e[X]\big]_{i,j},\qquad
X\in\{m_1,m_2,\varepsilon_1,\varepsilon_2,\rho u,\rho v,E\},
\]
with $G^e$ the $y$-flux of \eqref{eq:rusanov} with $v$ in place of $u$, and the volume fraction by the path-conservative update
\eqref{eq:alpha_explicit} from $X^s$, $\alpha^*_2=1-\alpha^*_1$.
\item \emph{Face coefficients.} On every face the quantities of Sec.~\ref{sec:dissipation} are computed from $X^*$, the
limiter factors of the reconstruction of $Q^e$ and the pressures of the previous stage (the Newton initial guess): the averages $\bar u^*_f$, $\bar\rho_f$, the impedances $Z$, the weight \eqref{eq:weight},
the factors \eqref{eq:psi} and the velocity jump \eqref{eq:vertex_div}. They are frozen during the solution of the stage,
so that the face velocity and face pressure \eqref{eq:faces} are affine in the two adjacent cell pressures,
\[
u_f=\bar u^*_f-\beta_f\,(P_R-P_L),\qquad p_f=w_fP_L+(1-w_f)P_R-\varpi_f ,
\]
with $\beta_f=\tau/(\bar\rho_f h)+\kappa^P_f/h$, $w_f=\rho^*_R/(\rho^*_L+\rho^*_R)$ and $\varpi_f=\kappa^u_fD^*_f/h$, $h=\Delta x$ or $\Delta y$.
\item \emph{Incremental fluxes and conservative updates.} For given cell pressures, the incremental fluxes
\eqref{eq:increment} are split into their donor parts $I_f=I^L_f+I^R_f$; the incremental mass fluxes are
$\hat M^{L,R}_f=\sum_kI^{L,R}_f[m_k]$, $\hat M_f=\hat M^L_f+\hat M^R_f$. Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
&X^{\rm tr}_{i,j}=X^*_{i,j}-r_x\,\delta_x\big[I[X]\big]_{i,j}-r_y\,\delta_y\big[I[X]\big]_{i,j},\qquad X\in\{m_k,\varepsilon_k\},\\
&s_{i,j}=r_x\,\delta_x[u_f]_{i,j}+r_y\,\delta_y[v_f]_{i,j},\qquad \alpha^{\rm tr}_{k,i,j}=\alpha^*_{k,i,j}(1-s_{i,j}),\qquad \rho^{n+1}_{i,j}=\textstyle\sum_km^{\rm tr}_{k,i,j},
\end{aligned}
\]
the momentum is advanced with the face pressure and the incremental momentum flux, normal and tangential,
\[
\begin{aligned}
(\rho u)^{\rm new}_{i,j}&=(\rho u)^*_{i,j}-r_x\,\delta_x\big[p_f+u_f\hat M_f\big]_{i,j}-r_y\,\delta_y\big[u^L_t\hat M^L_f+u^R_t\hat M^R_f\big]_{i,j},\\
(\rho v)^{\rm new}_{i,j}&=(\rho v)^*_{i,j}-r_x\,\delta_x\big[v^L_t\hat M^L_f+v^R_t\hat M^R_f\big]_{i,j}-r_y\,\delta_y\big[p_f+v_f\hat M_f\big]_{i,j},
\end{aligned}
\]
with the tangential velocities $u_t$, $v_t$ of the donor cells on $X^*$, and the total energy with the energy flux
\[
F^I_f[E]=\textstyle\sum_kI_f[\varepsilon_k]+\tfrac12u_n^2\hat M_f+\tfrac12\big((u^L_t)^2\hat M^L_f+(u^R_t)^2\hat M^R_f\big)+p_fu_n ,\qquad
E^{\rm new}_{i,j}=E^*_{i,j}-r_x\,\delta_x\big[F^I[E]\big]_{i,j}-r_y\,\delta_y\big[F^I[E]\big]_{i,j},
\]
$u_n=u_f$ on $x$-faces and $u_n=v_f$ on $y$-faces. The conservative mixture internal energy is
$(\rho e)^C_{i,j}=E^{\rm new}_{i,j}-|(\rho\mathbf u)^{\rm new}_{i,j}|^2/(2\rho^{n+1}_{i,j})$. All these quantities depend only on the pressures of
the cell and its four neighbours.
\item \emph{Local closure.} In every cell the defect \eqref{eq:defect} is partitioned by \eqref{eq:Lambda}, with the
physical budget \eqref{eq:Qk} computed on $X^*$ after step 1 and frozen during the stage, and the relaxation closure of
Sec.~\ref{sec:relaxation} returns $\bar p^{\rm cl}_{i,j}$ and the phase states.
\item \emph{Pressure system.} The system \eqref{eq:global} is solved for the cell pressures by the Newton method of
Sec.~\ref{sec:pressure_system}; every residual evaluation repeats steps 3 and 4.
\item \emph{Update.} At the converged pressures, the stage value consists of the momentum and the total energy of
step 3 and the volume fractions, partial densities and phase energies of the closure of step 4.
\end{enumerate}
The operator $\mathcal H$ in \eqref{eq:stages} is the one-stage update given by steps 1--6: step 1 is the explicit part,
evaluated on $U_{E,i}$, and steps 2--6 the implicit part over $\tau=a_{ii}\Delta t$. The construction is the same in one
and two space dimensions.

In summary, the scheme is stabilised by three sources of numerical dissipation. The first is the Rusanov
dissipation of the explicit advective fluxes, which scales with the flow velocity. The second and third are the two
implicit Laplace terms in \eqref{eq:faces}: a pressure Laplacian through the face velocity and a velocity Laplacian
(bulk viscosity) through the face pressure, which act on the acoustic waves with the weight \eqref{eq:weight} and vanish as $c\tau/\Delta x$
grows, i.e.\ in the low Mach number limit. Since all three are either explicit with the material speed or implicit,
none of them requires a time step smaller than \eqref{eq:cfl}.
