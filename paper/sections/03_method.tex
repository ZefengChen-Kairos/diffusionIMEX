\section{Numerical method}
\label{sec:method}

The scheme splits \eqref{eq:model} into a transport part, advanced explicitly with a Rusanov flux whose
dissipation speed is the material speed, and an implicit part that contains the pressure, the work of the
pressure on each phase and all relaxation sources. The implicit part reduces to one scalar equation per
cell for the mixture pressure, whose residual is evaluated through a single local closure; the rates
$\mu,\theta,\nu$ enter only as parameters of that closure. We present the method in one space dimension on
a uniform grid with spacing $\Delta x$; the multi-dimensional extension is given in Sec.~\ref{sec:multid}.
Throughout, $r=\tau/\Delta x$ with $\tau$ the implicit step of a stage, $\delta_i[F_f]=F_{i+1/2}-F_{i-1/2}$.

\subsection{Transport--velocity splitting}
\label{sec:tv_split}
Following the splitting of Toro and V\'azquez-Cend\'on, the flux $Xu$ of every transported quantity at the
new time level is written as
\begin{equation}
Xu^{n+1}=\underbrace{X\,u^{e}}_{\rm explicit}+\underbrace{X\,(u^{n+1}-u^{e})}_{\rm implicit\ increment},
\label{eq:tv}
\end{equation}
where $u^e$ is the velocity of the explicit stage state. Table~\ref{tab:split} lists the two parts of each
equation.
\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\caption{Explicit and implicit parts of \eqref{eq:model}.}
\label{tab:split}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
equation & explicit (Rusanov) & implicit (pressure and closure) \\
\midrule
$\alpha_k$ & $\partial_x(\alpha_ku^e)$ & $\partial_x(\alpha^{\rm face}_k\Delta u)$, strain $-\alpha_k\partial_xu$, relaxation \\
$m_k$ & $\partial_x(m_ku^e)$ & $\partial_x(m^{\rm face}_k\Delta u)$, mass transfer \\
$\varepsilon_k$ & $\partial_x(\varepsilon_ku^e)$ & $\partial_x(\varepsilon^{\rm face}_k\Delta u)$, work $\alpha_kp_k\partial_xu$, relaxation \\
$\rho u$ & $\partial_x(\rho u\,u^e)$ & $\partial_x\bar p$, $\partial_x(u\,\hat M\Delta u)$ \\
$E$ & $\partial_x(E\,u^e)$ & $\partial_x(\bar pu)$, $\partial_x(\hat E\Delta u)$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}
The explicit subsystem has the single eigenvalue $u$: $\alpha_k,m_k,\varepsilon_k$ are linearly advected and
$(\rho,\rho u)$ is a pressureless gas. Its Rusanov dissipation speed is therefore the material speed,
independent of the sound speed. The phase masses also carry the implicit increment, so that $\alpha_k$, $m_k$
and $\varepsilon_k$ are transported by the same operator (R3); this makes the transport temperature-consistent
(Sec.~\ref{sec:properties}).

\subsection{Volume-transport form and path-conservative Rusanov}
\label{sec:volume_transport}
The volume equation $\partial_t\alpha+u\,\partial_x\alpha=0$ is non-conservative. A path-conservative
discretisation with the linear path $\Psi(s)=Q_L+s(Q_R-Q_L)$ gives
\[
\int_0^1u(\Psi)\,\frac{\mathrm d\alpha(\Psi)}{\mathrm ds}\,\mathrm ds=\bar u_f\,(\alpha_R-\alpha_L),\qquad
\bar u_f=\tfrac12(u_L+u_R).
\]
The identity $\bar u\,\Delta\alpha=\Delta(\alpha u)-\bar\alpha\,\Delta u$, $\bar\alpha=\tfrac12(\alpha_L+\alpha_R)$,
splits the path-conservative Rusanov fluctuations
$D^\pm_f=\tfrac12\big[\Delta(\alpha u)-\bar\alpha_f\Delta u\big]\pm\tfrac12a\,\Delta\alpha$ into
(i) the conservative volume flux $F_f[\alpha]=\tfrac12(\alpha_Lu_L+\alpha_Ru_R)-\tfrac12a\,\Delta\alpha$, identical
in form to the Rusanov fluxes of $m_k$ and $\varepsilon_k$ (R3), and (ii) the volume strain
$-\bar\alpha_f\Delta u_f$, the discrete $-\alpha\,\partial_xu$. The scheme keeps (i) as transport (explicit
part plus implicit increment, with the same discretisation) and moves (ii) into the implicit closure, where
it is paired with the pressure work $\alpha_kp_k\partial_xu$ (R4) and together they form the discrete Gibbs
relation of Sec.~\ref{sec:gibbs}. The explicit path-conservative Rusanov scheme with the linear path and
the volume-transport form used here are therefore the same scheme.

\subsection{Explicit operator}
\label{sec:explicit}
On the explicit stage state $Q^e$, for $X\in\{\alpha_1,\alpha_2,m_1,m_2,\varepsilon_1,\varepsilon_2,\rho u,E\}$,
\begin{equation}
F^e_{i+1/2}[X]=\tfrac12\big(X^Lu^L+X^Ru^R\big)-\tfrac12a_f\big(X^R-X^L\big),\qquad a_f\ge\max\big(|u^L_f|,|u^R_f|\big),
\label{eq:rusanov}
\end{equation}
with $X^L=X_i+\tfrac12\sigma_{X,i}$, $X^R=X_{i+1}-\tfrac12\sigma_{X,i+1}$ and $u^{L,R}$ the reconstructed face
velocities. The dissipation speed is taken face by face, $a_f=\max(|u^L_f|,|u^R_f|)$, or as its maximum over
the domain; both satisfy the positivity condition of Theorem~\ref{thm:positivity}, which is imposed on the
reconstructed face states. The explicit update is
$X^*_i=X^s_i-r\,\delta_i\big[F^e[X]\big]$, where $Q^s$ is the update base of the stage (Sec.~\ref{sec:time}).

\paragraph{Reconstruction}
The volume-transported quantities $\mathcal X_V=(\alpha_1,m_1,m_2,\varepsilon_1,\varepsilon_2)$ share one limiter,
\begin{equation}
\sigma_{X,i}=\phi_i\,\tfrac12(X_{i+1}-X_{i-1}),\qquad
\phi_i=\min_{X\in\mathcal X_V}\frac{\mathrm{minmod}(X_i-X_{i-1},X_{i+1}-X_i)}{\tfrac12(X_{i+1}-X_{i-1})}\in[0,1],
\label{eq:shared_limiter}
\end{equation}
so every slope is bounded by its own minmod slope and has its sign (TVD), and all slopes are the same
multiple of the central difference. Hence linear relations between the quantities hold on the faces as in
the cells: at uniform $p$, $\varepsilon_k=c_k(p)\alpha_k+q_km_k$ by \eqref{eq:eps_nasg} holds for the face values,
which is what R2 needs. Quantities that are constant on the stencil to round-off (relative to a round-off
scale $S_X$: $1$ for $\alpha_1$, $|m_k|$ for $m_k$, the mixture internal plus kinetic energy for $\varepsilon_k$)
do not take part in the minimum; any quantity at a local extremum gives $\phi_i=0$. The velocity is
reconstructed separately, component by component with its own minmod slope,
$u^{L,R}=u_i\pm\tfrac12\,\mathrm{minmod}(u_i-u_{i-1},u_{i+1}-u_i)$, and the face momentum and energy are
assembled as $(\rho u)^{L,R}=\rho^{L,R}u^{L,R}$, $E^{L,R}=\sum_k\varepsilon_k^{L,R}+\tfrac12\rho^{L,R}|u^{L,R}|^2$. The face value of
$\alpha_2$ is $1-\alpha_1^{\rm face}$, so $\sum_kF^e[\alpha_k]=\tfrac12(u^L+u^R)\equiv\bar u^e_f$. The reconstruction does
not call the equation of state, so the explicit operator only requires $\rho>0$ on $Q^e$.

Both the central part and the dissipation of \eqref{eq:rusanov} use the reconstructed values, so that for
$X_t+uX_x=0$ with $u>0$ and $a=u$ the flux is the MUSCL upwind flux $u(X_i+\tfrac12\sigma_i)$.

\subsection{Implicit operator: face velocity, face pressure and incremental fluxes}
\label{sec:implicit_faces}

The unknowns of a stage are the cell mixture pressures $P_i$. With $\rho^*_i=\sum_km^*_{k,i}$ and
$u^*_i=(\rho u)^*_i/\rho^*_i$ on the explicit prediction, the implicit part provides the face velocity $u_f$,
the face pressure $p_f$ and the incremental fluxes, all as functions of $P$.

\subsubsection{Face velocity and face pressure}
\paragraph{Momentum-consistent pressure acceleration}
The cell velocity is the primary variable; the face velocity is the average of the two cell velocities
plus a pressure acceleration. Let the two half cells adjacent to the face, of masses
$\rho^*_i\Delta x/2$ and $\rho^*_{i+1}\Delta x/2$, move with the same acceleration $a_f$ under the pressures
$P_i$, $P_{i+1}$ and the face pressure $p_f$. Their momentum balances give
\[
a_f=-\frac{P_{i+1}-P_i}{\bar\rho_f\Delta x},\quad \bar\rho_f=\tfrac12(\rho^*_i+\rho^*_{i+1}),\qquad
p_f=\frac{\rho^*_{i+1}P_i+\rho^*_iP_{i+1}}{\rho^*_i+\rho^*_{i+1}} .
\]
The cell momentum update $-r(p_{i+1/2}-p_{i-1/2})/\rho^*_i$ is then the average of the accelerations of its two
faces, momentum is conserved, and $p_f=P$ for uniform $P$ (R2).

\paragraph{Acoustic complement}
A von Neumann analysis of one implicit stage for linear acoustics shows that the central implicit acoustic
operator does not damp the odd--even velocity mode $u_i=(-1)^i$ for any $\nu=c\tau/\Delta x$: its face average is
zero, it produces no divergence, and the pressure does not see it; the face pressure, a pressure average,
does not see it either. Only the material-speed Rusanov dissipation damps it, by the factor $|1-2C|$ per
step with $C=u\tau/\Delta x$, which is weak when the flow starts from rest; the mode is excited at strong discontinuities
(pressure jumps, contacts with a large impedance ratio). We therefore add to the implicit face quantities
the upwind terms of the acoustic Riemann solution: the velocity-jump term damps the odd--even mode, and
the pressure-diffusion term makes the face velocity monotone across pressure jumps:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
u_f&=\bar u^*_f-\frac{\tau(P_{i+1}-P_i)}{\bar\rho_f\Delta x}-\varphi_f\,\psi^P_f\,\frac{P_{i+1}-P_i}{Z_i+Z_{i+1}},\\
p_f&=\frac{\rho^*_{i+1}P_i+\rho^*_iP_{i+1}}{\rho^*_i+\rho^*_{i+1}}-\varphi_f\,\psi^u_f\,\frac{Z_iZ_{i+1}}{Z_i+Z_{i+1}}\,D^*_f,
\end{aligned}\qquad
\varphi_f=\Big(1-\frac{\tau\min(c_i,c_{i+1})}{\Delta x}\Big)^+ .
\label{eq:faces}
\end{equation}
\paragraph{Weight of the acoustic complement}
% Derivation in the text (B9.5 Thm 6):
Consider one implicit stage (backward Euler, step $\tau$) of linear acoustics about a state at rest
with sound speed $c$, written with the face quantities \eqref{eq:faces} and both complement terms
multiplied by the same weight $\varphi\ge0$. Expanding the amplification matrix for small wave numbers
gives, for both acoustic characteristic variables, the effective viscosity
\begin{equation}
D_{\rm stage}=\tfrac12c^2\tau+\varphi\,\tfrac12c\Delta x=\tfrac12c\Delta x\,(\nu+\varphi)+O(k^2),\qquad \nu=\frac{c\tau}{\Delta x},
\label{eq:dstage}
\end{equation}
the first term coming from backward Euler and the second from the complement.
To see this, insert a Fourier mode $e^{\mathrm ikx}$ into the stage: the face average, jump and divergence
become $\tfrac12(1+e^{\mathrm ik\Delta x})$, $e^{\mathrm ik\Delta x}-1$ and $1-e^{-\mathrm ik\Delta x}$; the pressure terms are
implicit and the velocity terms explicit, as in \eqref{eq:faces}. The two eigenvalues $g_\pm$ of the
resulting $2\times2$ amplification matrix satisfy $\tau^{-1}\log g_\pm=\mp\mathrm ick-D_{\rm stage}k^2+O(k^3)$
with $D_{\rm stage}$ given by \eqref{eq:dstage}; a direct evaluation of the eigenvalues for
$\nu\in\{0.3,0.6,2\}$ and $\varphi\in\{0,(1-\nu)^+\}$ reproduces \eqref{eq:dstage} to seven digits.
The three-point semi-discretisation $\dot w_i=-\frac{c}{2\Delta x}(w_{i+1}-w_{i-1})+\frac{D}{\Delta x^2}\delta^2w_i$
has non-negative coefficients if and only if $D\ge\tfrac12c\Delta x$, the upwind (Godunov) viscosity.
Requiring that every stage carries at least this viscosity on resolved acoustic waves, and no more,
\[
D_{\rm stage}=\max\big(\tfrac12c\Delta x,\ \tfrac12c^2\tau\big),
\]
fixes the weight uniquely: $\varphi=1-\nu$ for $\nu<1$, and $\varphi=0$ for $\nu\ge1$, where backward
Euler alone already supplies $\tfrac12c^2\tau\ge\tfrac12c\Delta x$. Hence $\varphi=(1-\nu)^+$ in
\eqref{eq:faces}. The two terms are the two halves of the Godunov dissipation on the two characteristic
variables and therefore share the weight.
Three consequences follow. For $\tau\to0$, $\varphi=1$ and $u_f-\bar u^*_f=-\Delta P/(Z_i+Z_{i+1})$,
$p_f-\bar p_f=-\frac{Z_iZ_{i+1}}{Z_i+Z_{i+1}}\Delta u$: the acoustic Godunov face states. For $c\tau\ge\Delta x$,
$\varphi=0$ and the scheme is unchanged; in particular the complement vanishes in the low-Mach regime
(Proposition~\ref{prop:lowmach}). With the
complement the amplification factor of the odd--even mode is $|(1-2\varphi\nu)(1-2C)|$; the velocity-jump term
is an explicit viscosity with $\nu_{\rm add}\tau/\Delta x^2=\tfrac12\nu(1-\nu)\le\tfrac18$, hence stable, while the
pressure-diffusion term is implicit and linear in $P$.

\paragraph{Second-order factors}
The complement \eqref{eq:faces} is the acoustic Godunov dissipation evaluated on the cell values
$(P_i,u_i)$, $(P_{i+1},u_{i+1})$, i.e.\ it is first order. To make it second order where the solution is
smooth we evaluate it on the reconstructed face states of the explicit operator (Sec.~\ref{sec:explicit}),
which are the states the same face carries. Write $D=P_{i+1}-P_i$ and let $\sigma_i$ be the slope of $P$ in
cell $i$; the jump of the reconstructed face pressures is
\begin{equation}
P^R-P^L=D-\tfrac12(\sigma_i+\sigma_{i+1})=:\psi_f D .
\label{eq:psi_def}
\end{equation}
The pressure is not reconstructed by the scheme; it is a function of the reconstructed volume-transported
quantities $X\in\{\alpha_k,m_k,\varepsilon_k\}$ and of the velocity. These are reconstructed with the
shared limiter, $X^{L,R}=X_i\pm\tfrac12\phi_i\,\delta^cX_i$ with $\phi_i\in[0,1]$ the largest factor for
which every slope is a minmod slope, $\phi_i=0$ at a local extremum of any of them, and the velocity
separately with its own minmod slope (Sec.~\ref{sec:explicit}). Linearising $P(Q^R)-P(Q^L)$ about the
cell states gives
\[
P(Q^R)-P(Q^L)=D-\tfrac12\phi_s\big(\delta^cP_i+\delta^cP_{i+1}\big)+O(\Delta x^3),\qquad \phi_s=\min(\phi_i,\phi_{i+1}),
\]
so the pressure slope of the scheme's own face states is the central slope scaled by the shared factor
(in $\phi_i$ we also include the smoothness ratio of the velocity, since the face state $Q$ contains $u$).
Two requirements fix $\sigma$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the dissipation must not exceed the scheme's own reconstruction, $\sigma_i=\phi_s\,\cdot$(slope of $P$),
so that the pressure jump is that of the face states carrying the transported quantities;
\item the dissipation must be LED for $P$ itself (Jameson 1995): its coefficient must be non-negative and
equal to the first-order value at a local extremum of $P$, which holds if the slope of $P$ is a minmod
slope, $\sigma_i=\phi_s\,\sigma^{\rm mm}_i[P]$.
\end{enumerate}
Inserting $\sigma_i=\phi_s\sigma^{\rm mm}_i[P]$ into \eqref{eq:psi_def} gives
\begin{equation}
\psi^P_f=1-\phi_s\big(1-\psi^{\rm mm}_f[P]\big),\qquad
\psi^{\rm mm}_f[P]=1-\frac{\sigma^{\rm mm}_i[P]+\sigma^{\rm mm}_{i+1}[P]}{2D}\in[0,1].
\label{eq:psiP}
\end{equation}
The velocity-jump term modifies only the momentum flux and acts on the separately reconstructed velocity;
the same two requirements with the velocity's own minmod slope give
\begin{equation}
\psi^u_f=\psi^{\rm mm}_f[u].
\label{eq:psiu}
\end{equation}
Consequently: (i) $0\le\psi\le1$, so both dissipation coefficients in \eqref{eq:faces} stay non-negative;
(ii) $\psi^P_f=1$ (first order, the full Godunov dissipation) at a local extremum of $P$ or of any
volume-transported quantity, in particular at a contact, where $P$ and $u$ are continuous but the
impedance jumps; $\psi^u_f=1$ at a local extremum of $u$; (iii) where everything is smooth,
$\psi=O(\Delta x)$ and the complement is second order. This is the LED criterion of Jameson (1995) for a
limited dissipation: first order at an extremum of every quantity carried by the flux that the term
modifies, second order otherwise.

\paragraph{Sound speed in the complement}
The impedances $Z=\rho^*c$ use the frozen sound speed, evaluated on $X^*$ and kept fixed within the stage,
so the residual stays linear in $P$ in these terms. The frozen sound speed is subcharacteristic for every
relaxed closure, $\rho c_f\ge\rho c_{\rm eq}$ for the Wood, $pT$ and $pTg$ speeds (Fl\aa tten and Lund 2011;
Pelanti and Shyue 2014), so the complement is never less dissipative than the Godunov dissipation of the
relaxed wave.
If $X^*$ is not admissible in a cell, $c=\infty$ there, i.e.\ $\varphi=0$. At a solid wall $u_f=0$.

\subsubsection{Incremental fluxes and transported states}
During a stage the face sweeps the volume $u_f\tau$, filled with material from the upwind side of $u_f$. The
explicit part has already swept $\bar u^e_f\tau$ from its own upwind side ($\bar u^e_f=\tfrac12(u^L+u^R)^e$; Rusanov
with $a\ge|u|$ is upwinding plus dissipation), so the increment only supplies the difference:
\begin{equation}
I_f[X]=X^L_f\,w^L+X^R_f\,w^R,\qquad w^L=(u_f)^+-(\bar u^e_f)^+,\quad w^R=(u_f)^--(\bar u^e_f)^-,\qquad X\in\{\alpha_k,m_k,\varepsilon_k\},
\label{eq:increment}
\end{equation}
with $(\cdot)^\pm=\max/\min(\cdot,0)$ and $X^{L,R}_f$ the reconstructed face values of the explicit operator
on the same face ($\alpha_2$: $1-\alpha_1$). If $u_f$ and $\bar u^e_f$ have the same sign the increment is
$X^{\rm up}_f\Delta u_f$, $\Delta u_f=u_f-\bar u^e_f$: a negative increment returns the same material that the
explicit part brought. If they have opposite signs, everything swept explicitly is returned and $u_f\tau$
is swept from the other side. Since
$\sum_k\alpha^{\rm face}_k=1$, $\sum_kI_f[\alpha_k]=\Delta u_f$; the same coefficients act on $\alpha_k$, $m_k$, $\varepsilon_k$
(R3); and $I_f$ is continuous and piecewise linear in $P$.

\begin{theorem}[Positivity]\label{thm:positivity}
Consider a stage with $Q^e=Q^s$ (the first stage, or backward Euler), linear reconstruction with the shared
limiter \eqref{eq:shared_limiter}, and the Rusanov flux \eqref{eq:rusanov}.
(i) If $a_f\ge\max(|u^L_f|,|u^R_f|)$ and $a_fr\le\tfrac12$ on every face, the explicit prediction $X^*_i$ is a
non-negative combination of face values. The condition on $a_f$ is also necessary.
(ii) If moreover $r(a_f+|u_f|)\le\tfrac12$, the transported state $X^{\rm tr}_i=X^*_i-r\,\delta_i[I[X]]$ with the
face-value donors \eqref{eq:increment} is a non-negative combination of face values. Consequently the
transported state of every phase, trace phases included, lies in the admissible cone
$\{\alpha>0,\ m>0,\ \alpha-mb>0,\ \varepsilon-mq-(\alpha-mb)\pi>0\}$, independently of the content ratio of the
phases.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
(i) With linear reconstruction $X_i=\tfrac12(X^R_{i-1/2}+X^L_{i+1/2})$, and
\[
X^*_i=X^L_{i+1/2}\big[\tfrac12-\tfrac r2(a+u^L)_{i+1/2}\big]+X^R_{i-1/2}\big[\tfrac12-\tfrac r2(a-u^R)_{i-1/2}\big]
+\tfrac r2(a-u^R)_{i+1/2}X^R_{i+1/2}+\tfrac r2(a+u^L)_{i-1/2}X^L_{i-1/2}.
\]
All four coefficients are non-negative under the two conditions. If $a<u^R_{i+1/2}$ the third coefficient is
negative, and a large $X^R_{i+1/2}$ with small other face values gives $X^*_i<0$.
(ii) The explicit flux plus the increment is
$F_f+I_f=X^L_f\big[\tfrac12(u^L+a)+w^L\big]+X^R_f\big[\tfrac12(u^R-a)+w^R\big]$. From $a\ge\max|u^{L,R}|$,
$\tfrac12(a+u^L)\ge(\bar u^e)^+$ and $\tfrac12(a-u^R)\ge-(\bar u^e)^-$, hence
$\tfrac12(a+u^L)+w^L\ge(u_f)^+\ge0$ and $\tfrac12(a-u^R)-w^R\ge-(u_f)^-\ge0$: the neighbour coefficients are
non-negative, i.e.\ the increment never returns more than the explicit part brought. The own-cell
coefficients are $\tfrac12-r(\cdot)$ with $(\cdot)\le a+|u_f|$. The shared limiter keeps the face values between
neighbouring cell values, and the admissible set is a convex cone defined by linear constraints, so a
non-negative combination stays in it.
\end{proof}
Under the material CFL condition
$a\,r\approx0.06$ for LSDIRK2 with CFL 0.2. In the second LSDIRK2 stage $Q^e\neq Q^s$ and the argument does not
apply; that case is handled a posteriori (Sec.~\ref{sec:time}).

The transported states, the volume strain and the conservative mixture internal energy are
\begin{equation}
X^{\rm tr}_i=X^*_i-r\,\delta_i\big[I_f[X]\big],\quad X\in\{\alpha_k,m_k,\varepsilon_k\},
\label{eq:transported}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
s_i\equiv1-\sum_k\alpha^{\rm tr}_{k,i}=r\,\delta_i[u_f],
\label{eq:strain}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\rho^{n+1}_i=\sum_km^{\rm tr}_{k,i},\qquad (\rho e)^C_i=E^{n+1}_i-\frac{\big((\rho u)^{n+1}_i\big)^2}{2\rho^{n+1}_i},
\label{eq:rhoeC}
\end{equation}
The momentum and total energy are updated conservatively (R1),
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
(\rho u)^{n+1}_i&=(\rho u)^*_i-r\,\delta_i[p_f]-r\,\delta_i[u_f\hat M_f],\\
E^{n+1}_i&=E^*_i-r\,\delta_i\Big[\textstyle\sum_kI_f[\varepsilon_k]+\tfrac12u_f^2\hat M_f+p_fu_f\Big],
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:mom_energy}
\end{equation}
with the incremental mass flux $\hat M_f=\sum_kI_f[m_k]$, which carries the new face velocity $u_f$ (a uniform
acceleration keeps $u$ uniform). The quantities $X^{\rm tr}_i$, $s_i$ and $(\rho e)^C_i$ depend only on
$(P_{i-1},P_i,P_{i+1})$. The kinetic energy removed by the acoustic complement reaches $(\rho e)^C$ through $E$
and is distributed to the phases by the energy-defect partition of Sec.~\ref{sec:defect}.

\subsection{Unified implicit closure}
\label{sec:closure}
% Inputs from Sec. 3.5: transported states X^tr_i (eq:transported), volume strain s_i (eq:strain)
% and the conservative mixture internal energy (rho e)^C_i (eq:rhoeC); all depend only on
% (P_{i-1}, P_i, P_{i+1}).
The closure is a local map from the transported state of a cell, its volume strain and its conservative
mixture internal energy to the end-of-stage phase states and the mixture pressure. It contains the
pressure work of the acoustic subsystem and all three relaxation processes; the rates $\mu,\theta,\nu$
enter only as parameters.

\subsubsection{Discrete Gibbs relation}
\label{sec:gibbs}
Apply backward Euler over the stage to the implicit part of \eqref{eq:model}: the volume strain
$-\alpha_k\partial_xu$, the pressure work $-\alpha_kp_k\partial_xu$ and the relaxation sources. The strain of
the cell, $s$, is shared between the phases with the frozen fractions
$\omega_k=\alpha^{\rm tr}_k/\sum_j\alpha^{\rm tr}_j$, which is the $\alpha_k$ of $\alpha_k\partial_xu$; the same
$\omega_k$ multiplies the pressure work (R4). With $\chi_1=+1$, $\chi_2=-1$,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\alpha_k&=\alpha^{\rm tr}_k+\omega_ks+\chi_kR,\qquad R=\tau\big(\mu(p_1-p_2)+\mathcal M/\rho_I\big),\\
m_k&=m^{\rm tr}_k+\chi_k\tau\mathcal M,\\
\varepsilon^{\rm nc}_k&=\varepsilon^{\rm tr}_k-\omega_kp_k\,s-\chi_k\,\tau p_I\mu(p_1-p_2)+\chi_k\tau\big(\mathcal Q+e_I\mathcal M\big).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:gibbs}
\end{equation}
Since $\sum_k\omega_k=1$ and $s=1-\sum_k\alpha^{\rm tr}_k$ by \eqref{eq:strain}, $\sum_k\alpha_k=1$: the volume
constraint holds for every $p$ and needs no separate equation. In the instantaneous mechanical limit
($p_1=p_2=p$, $p_I=p$) the first and third lines combine into
\[
\varepsilon^{\rm nc}_k-\varepsilon^{\rm tr}_k=-p\,(\alpha_k-\alpha^{\rm tr}_k)+\chi_k\tau(\mathcal Q+e_I\mathcal M),
\]
the discrete Gibbs relation $\mathrm de_k+p\,\mathrm dv_k=\text{(heat and mass exchange)}$ with the same
volume change in both terms: the pressure work is exact for the discrete volume change, which is why the
closure is algebraic for the NASG phases (property (E2)).

\subsubsection{Shock relations of the model}
\label{sec:shock_relations}
The phase energy equations of \eqref{eq:model} are non-conservative. Across a shock the conservation laws
fix only the mixture Hugoniot $e-e_0+\bar p\,(v-v_0)=0$, $\bar p=\tfrac12(p_0+p_1)$; how the dissipation is
shared between the phases is not determined by the inviscid model but by the shock structure, and
numerically by the viscous path of the scheme (cf.\ Abgrall and Karni 2010 for path-conservative schemes).
We adopt the shock relations of Saurel et al.\ (2007), used by SPB 2009 and validated against
experimental shock speeds in epoxy--spinel mixtures: each phase follows its own Hugoniot,
\begin{equation}
e_k-e_{k0}+\bar p\,(v_k-v_{k0})=0\qquad(k=1,2).
\label{eq:II5}
\end{equation}
These relations also define the exact solutions of the five-equation model used in Sec.~5.
For the equations of state considered here the fundamental derivative
$\mathcal G_k=\tfrac12\big(1+\partial K_k/\partial p|_s\big)$, $K_k=\rho_kc_k^2$, is positive
($\mathcal G=(\gamma+1)/2$ for NASG; for the Mie--Gr\"uneisen phases we checked $\min\mathcal G=1.66$
(Cochran--Chan, $\rho\in[800,2500]$) and $1.26$ (JWL, $\rho\in[500,2500]$)). Then every admissible shock is
compressive (Bethe--Weyl): the isentropes are convex, the Hugoniot has second-order contact with the
isentrope at $p_0$, entropy increases along the Hugoniot with $p$, and the entropy condition requires
$p_1>p_0$, $v_1<v_0$. We use this as the compression criterion below.

\subsubsection{Mixture-energy consistency and the energy-defect partition}
\label{sec:defect}
The non-conservative mixture energy $\sum_k\varepsilon^{\rm nc}_k$ differs from the conservative one
\eqref{eq:rhoeC} by the defect
\begin{equation}
\delta=(\rho e)^C-\sum_k\varepsilon^{\rm tr}_k+s\sum_k\omega_kp_k ,
\label{eq:defect}
\end{equation}
in which the relaxation terms cancel between the phases. In smooth flow $\delta$ is a discretisation error
of alternating sign; at a shock it is the $O(1)$ dissipation of the scheme, kinetic energy converted into
internal energy. We distribute it,
\begin{equation}
\varepsilon_k=\varepsilon^{\rm nc}_k+\Lambda_k(\delta),\qquad \sum_k\Lambda_k(\delta)=\delta,
\label{eq:partition}
\end{equation}
so that $\sum_k\varepsilon_k=(\rho e)^C$ holds exactly inside the closure: the solution is
mixture-energy consistent (R6) without an end-of-stage projection, and no re-initialisation in the
sense of SPB 2009 Sec.~3.4 is needed. The partition $\Lambda_k$ is the only freedom left, and it
determines the limit shock relations of the scheme. Two principles fix it: the physical dissipation
is shared according to the model's shock relations \eqref{eq:II5}; the discretisation error is shared so
that no phase is harmed.

\paragraph{Profile analysis}
In the shock frame a steady profile has mass flux $j=\rho u$ and total normal stress
$P=p-\sigma_{\rm num}$, where $\sigma_{\rm num}$ is the stress of the numerical viscosity. Momentum and
energy give the Rayleigh line $P(v)=p_0+j^2(v_0-v)$ and $\mathrm de=-P\,\mathrm dv$. For phase $k$ (per unit
phase mass, mass fraction $Y_k$)
\begin{equation}
\mathrm de_k=-p\,\mathrm dv_k+\mathrm dq_k,\qquad Y_k\,\mathrm dq_k=\lambda_k\,\mathrm dq,\qquad
\mathrm dq=(p-P)\,\mathrm dv\ge0,
\label{eq:profile}
\end{equation}
with $\lambda_k=\partial\Lambda_k/\partial\delta$ the local share. Summing over $k$ gives back
$\mathrm de=-P\,\mathrm dv$: whatever the share, the mixture ends on its Hugoniot, and the share only
decides the end-state phase volumes. Integrating \eqref{eq:profile} and comparing with \eqref{eq:II5}, the
relations \eqref{eq:II5} hold if and only if
\begin{equation}
Y_kq_k=\int_{\rm profile}(p-\bar p)\,\mathrm dv_k\qquad(k=1,2):
\label{eq:qk}
\end{equation}
the heat received by phase $k$ equals the difference between its compression work along the profile
and its Hugoniot work.

\begin{theorem}[Shock share]\label{thm:shock_share}
(i) For weak shocks, $\Delta p=p_1-p_0\to0$, the dissipation received by phase $k$ per unit mixture volume
under \eqref{eq:II5} is
\begin{equation}
Y_k\rho\,q_k=\frac{\alpha_k\mathcal G_k}{6K_k^2}\Delta p^3+O(\Delta p^4),
\label{eq:weak_share}
\end{equation}
so the share compatible with \eqref{eq:II5} is unique,
$w_k=\dfrac{\alpha_k\mathcal G_k/K_k^2}{\sum_j\alpha_j\mathcal G_j/K_j^2}$.
(ii) No share $\lambda_k=\Lambda_k(U)$ that is a continuous function of the local state $U$ alone makes the
end state of the profile \eqref{eq:profile} satisfy \eqref{eq:II5} for all shock strengths.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
(i) Along a weak profile each phase is isentropic to the order needed,
$v_k(p)=v_{k0}\big[1-x/K_k+\mathcal G_kx^2/K_k^2+O(x^3)\big]$ with $x=p-p_0$. Inserting this into
\eqref{eq:qk} with $\bar p=p_0+\tfrac12\Delta p$ and integrating term by term gives \eqref{eq:weak_share}, which
is the entropy production $T_k\Delta s_k$ of a single-phase shock of strength $\Delta p$ and does not depend
on the path.
(ii) Let $U_0$ be any state and consider a weak shock with pre-shock state $U_0$. All states on its
profile lie in an $O(\Delta p)$ neighbourhood of $U_0$; by continuity the end-state share is
$\Lambda_k(U_0)+O(\Delta p)$, and by (i) consistency requires $\Lambda_k(U_0)=w_k(U_0)$. Since $U_0$ is
arbitrary, the only candidate local rule is $\Lambda=w$. Integrating \eqref{eq:profile} with $\lambda=w$ for
water (stiffened gas, $\gamma=4.4$, $\pi=6\times10^8$~Pa) with $\alpha_{\rm air}=10^{-3}$, $p_0=10^5$~Pa, and
the mass flux $j$ of the \eqref{eq:II5} shock to $p_1=10^{10}$~Pa (relative tolerance $10^{-10}$) gives an
end pressure $1.0602\times10^{10}$~Pa, an end water fraction $0.98793$ instead of $0.99975$, and an air
temperature 49 times the \eqref{eq:II5} value. The deviation grows monotonically with the shock strength
(water with 50\% air: relative end-pressure deviation $4\times10^{-9}$, $4\times10^{-5}$, $1.8\times10^{-3}$,
$2\times10^{-2}$ for $p_1=10^6,10^8,10^9,10^{10}$~Pa), so it is not an integration error. Hence $w$, and by
the uniqueness argument every continuous local rule, fails for strong shocks.
\end{proof}

By Theorem~\ref{thm:shock_share}(ii) the share must use information beyond the local state, namely the
post-shock (or the pre-shock state and the shock speed). For strong shocks the constant share that makes
the profile end on \eqref{eq:II5} is well approximated by the Hugoniot--isentrope energy gap at the
end pressure (Table~\ref{tab:shares}),
\begin{equation}
Q_k=m_k\,(v^{\rm pre}_k-b_k)\,f_k(p_{\rm lo}\to p_{\rm hi}),\qquad
f_k=\frac{(p_{\rm hi}+\gamma_k\pi_k)(\tilde v_H-\tilde v_S)}{\gamma_k-1},
\label{eq:Qk}
\end{equation}
\[
\tilde v_H=\frac{(\gamma_k-1)x_{\rm hi}+(\gamma_k+1)x_{\rm lo}}{(\gamma_k+1)x_{\rm hi}+(\gamma_k-1)x_{\rm lo}},\qquad
\tilde v_S=\Big(\frac{x_{\rm lo}}{x_{\rm hi}}\Big)^{1/\gamma_k},\qquad x=p+\pi_k ,
\]
whose share $Q_k/\sum_jQ_j$ reduces to $w_k$ for weak shocks. For Mie--Gr\"uneisen phases the Hugoniot
and the isentrope $(p-p_r)v^{\Gamma+1}={\rm const}$ are solved by bisection.

\paragraph{Local shock strength}
$Q_k$ requires the pre- and post-shock pressures. We take the extrema $p_{\rm lo},p_{\rm hi}$ of the mixture
pressure of the predicted state $X^*$ on the scheme's own compact stencil (the $\pm1$ neighbours; the
five-point cross in 2D), the pre-shock phase specific volume $v^{\rm pre}_k=\alpha_k/m_k$ from the
lowest-pressure cell of the stencil, and the phase mass $m_k$ of the cell itself (it is the cell's own
material that is compressed). The budget is evaluated only in compressing cells (central divergence of
$u^*$ negative, by the compression criterion of Sec.~\ref{sec:shock_relations}) and is frozen within the
stage. When a shock passes a cell, the defects of the successive steps add up to the physical
dissipation $Q=\sum_kQ_k$, so $\delta\le Q$ at each step, and the part of $\delta$ above $Q$ is necessarily
error; in smooth compression $Q\propto\Delta p^3$ is negligible. The stencil is the smallest non-local one, so no length parameter is introduced.

\paragraph{Discretisation error}
The rest of $\delta$ is shared according to the energy of each phase above its admissibility bound,
\begin{equation}
\lambda^E_k=\frac{E_k}{\sum_jE_j},\qquad
E_k=\varepsilon^{\rm tr}_k-m^{\rm tr}_kq_k-(\alpha^{\rm tr}_k-m^{\rm tr}_kb_k)\pi_k=\frac{(p_k+\pi_k)(\alpha_k-m_kb_k)}{\gamma_k-1}\Big|_{\rm tr}
\label{eq:lambdaE}
\end{equation}
(for Mie--Gr\"uneisen phases, the thermal energy above the cold curve, $\varepsilon_k-m_k\varepsilon_r$).
\begin{proposition}[Error split]\label{prop:error_split}
Let both phases be admissible before the split, $E_k>0$, and let the volumes be fixed. Among the splits
$\Lambda_k=\lambda_k\delta$ with shares $\lambda_k$ independent of $\delta$ and $\sum_k\lambda_k=1$, the only one
that keeps both phases admissible for every $\delta>-\sum_jE_j$ is $\lambda_k=\lambda^E_k$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
After the split the energy above the bound is $E_k+\lambda_k\delta$. Letting $\delta\to+\infty$ requires
$\lambda_k\ge0$; letting $\delta\to-\sum_jE_j$ requires $E_k-\lambda_k\sum_jE_j\ge0$, i.e.\
$\lambda_k\le E_k/\sum_jE_j$. Summing over $k$, both sides equal one, so every inequality is an equality.
Conversely, $E_k+\lambda^E_k\delta=E_k(1+\delta/\sum_jE_j)>0$.
\end{proof}
Only the negative side, $\delta\to-\sum_jE_j$, is needed in the proof. Equivalently, $\lambda^E$ is the unique
minimiser of $\sum_k\Lambda_k^2/E_k$ subject to $\sum_k\Lambda_k=\delta$ (the Lagrange condition gives
$\Lambda_k\propto E_k$): it perturbs every phase by the same relative amount, the factor
$1+\delta/\sum_jE_j$ on $p_k+\pi_k$. With a volume strain $s\neq0$ the admissible
quantity also contains the compression work, which is shared by $\omega_k$ in \eqref{eq:gibbs} and is not
part of the defect.

\paragraph{Partition}
Combining the two parts,
\begin{equation}
\Lambda_k(\delta)=\begin{cases}\min(\delta,Q)\,\lambda^{\rm II.5}_k+\big(\delta-\min(\delta,Q)\big)\lambda^E_k,&\delta\ge0,\\[2pt]
\delta\,\lambda^E_k,&\delta<0,\end{cases}\qquad \lambda^{\rm II.5}_k=\frac{Q_k}{Q},\quad Q=\sum_kQ_k ,
\label{eq:Lambda}
\end{equation}
a continuous, piecewise affine function of $\delta$ with break points $\delta=0$ and $\delta=Q$. On the
active piece $\Lambda_k=\lambda_k\delta+\xi_k$ ($\xi_k=Q_k-Q\lambda^E_k$ on the third piece, zero otherwise).
Because $\delta=D_0+ps$ with $D_0=(\rho e)^C-\sum_k\varepsilon^{\rm tr}_k$ depends on the trial pressure, the
piece is selected by $\delta(p)$, so the residual of Sec.~\ref{sec:solver} is continuous and piecewise
smooth.

\subsubsection{Closures for each set of rates}
\label{sec:closures}
We assume the ordering of time scales of Pelanti (2022): $\mu=\infty$ whenever $\theta>0$ or $\nu>0$, and
$\theta=\infty$ whenever $\nu>0$. Each closure reduces to one scalar equation in the cell pressure.

\paragraph{$p$ closure ($\mu=\infty$, $\theta=\nu=0$)}
With $p_1=p_2=p$, \eqref{eq:gibbs} and \eqref{eq:partition} give
\begin{equation}
\varepsilon_k=\varepsilon^{\rm tr}_k-p\,(\alpha_k-\alpha^{\rm tr}_k)+\lambda_k\delta+\xi_k,\qquad \delta=D_0+ps,
\label{eq:pclosure}
\end{equation}
independent of $\omega_k$ and $p_I$. With \eqref{eq:eps_nasg} this is explicit in $\alpha_k$,
\begin{equation}
\alpha_k(p)=\frac{(\gamma_k-1)\big[A_k+B_kp\big]+(p+\gamma_k\pi_k)m_kb_k}{\gamma_k(p+\pi_k)},\qquad
A_k=\varepsilon^{\rm tr}_k-m_kq_k+\lambda_kD_0+\xi_k,\quad B_k=\alpha^{\rm tr}_k+\lambda_ks,
\label{eq:alpha_p}
\end{equation}
and the local equation is $\Phi(p)=\sum_k\alpha_k(p)-1=0$. Since
$\alpha_k'(p)=-(\gamma_k-1)N_k/\big(\gamma_k(p+\pi_k)^2\big)$ with $N_k=A_k-(B_k-m_kb_k)\pi_k$, and $N_k>0$
is the admissibility of phase $k$, $\Phi$ is decreasing; $\Phi\to+\infty$ as $p\downarrow\max_k(-\pi_k)$ and
$\Phi\to\sum_k\big((\gamma_k-1)B_k+m_kb_k\big)/\gamma_k-1<\sum_kB_k-1=0$ as $p\to\infty$. Hence the root exists
and is unique; it is found by a bracketed Newton iteration. For a Mie--Gr\"uneisen phase $\alpha_k(p)$ is the
root of $f_k(\alpha)=m_k\varepsilon_r(m_k/\alpha)+\alpha\,(p-p_r(m_k/\alpha))/\Gamma_k+p\alpha-A_k-B_kp$, with
$f_k'(\alpha)=\rho_kc_k^2/\Gamma_k>0$, so it is unique whenever $c_k^2>0$.
For Mie--Gr\"uneisen phases we have no global monotonicity proof for the outer equation $\Phi(p)=0$; it is solved
by a bracketed Newton iteration whose bracket is expanded geometrically from the initial guess, a pressure for
which some $\alpha_k(p)$ has no solution ($c_k^2\le0$) counting as $\Phi=+\infty$.

\paragraph{Finite mechanical relaxation ($\mu<\infty$, $\theta=\nu=0$)}
With $a_k=\alpha^{\rm tr}_k+\omega_ks$, $\alpha_1=a_1+R$, $\alpha_2=a_2-R$ and $p_I=w_1p_1+w_2p_2$
($w_1=Z_2/(Z_1+Z_2)$, impedances frozen at $X^*$), \eqref{eq:gibbs}--\eqref{eq:partition} are, for given $R$,
a $2\times2$ linear system for $(p_1,p_2)$,
\begin{equation}
\Big(\frac{\alpha_k-m_kb_k}{\gamma_k-1}+\omega_ks\Big)p_k+\chi_kR\,(w_1p_1+w_2p_2)-\lambda_ks\,(\omega_1p_1+\omega_2p_2)
=\varepsilon^{\rm tr}_k-m_kq_k-\frac{\gamma_k\pi_k(\alpha_k-m_kb_k)}{\gamma_k-1}+\lambda_kD_0+\xi_k ,
\label{eq:twop}
\end{equation}
followed by the scalar equation $\mathcal F(R)=R-\tau\mu\big(p_1(R)-p_2(R)\big)=0$. Increasing $R$ expands
phase 1 and compresses phase 2, so $\mathcal F$ is increasing on the admissible interval and the root is
unique; it lies between $0$ and $\tau\mu(p_1-p_2)|_{R=0}$. The output pressure is
$\bar p^{\rm cl}=\alpha_1p_1+\alpha_2p_2$. For $\mu=0$, $R=0$ and each phase does Gibbs work at its own
pressure: the frozen scheme.

\paragraph{$pT$ closure ($\mu=\theta=\infty$, $\nu=0$)}
The masses are $m_k=m^{\rm tr}_k$ and the mixture energy gives the temperature explicitly (property (E3)),
\begin{equation}
T(p)=\frac{(\rho e)^C-\sum_km_kq_k}{\sum_km_kc_{vk}\frac{p+\gamma_k\pi_k}{p+\pi_k}},\qquad
\Phi(p)=\sum_km_kv_k\big(p,T(p)\big)-1=0 ,
\label{eq:pT}
\end{equation}
independent of $\alpha^{\rm tr}$, $s$ and $\lambda$: the volume fractions are dependent variables, as in the
four-equation model. The admissible root is unique (Pelanti and Shyue 2014, App.~C).

\paragraph{Finite thermal relaxation ($\mu=\infty$, $\theta<\infty$, $\nu=0$)}
The heat exchange $\Delta Q=\tau\theta(T_2-T_1)$ is added to \eqref{eq:pclosure} with opposite signs. By
\eqref{eq:alpha_p}, $\alpha_k$ is linear in $\Delta Q$, so $T_k=T^0_k(p)+\chi_k\Delta Q/C_{pk}$ with
$C_{pk}=\gamma_kc_{vk}m_k$ (isobaric heat equals enthalpy change), and
\begin{equation}
\Delta Q(p)=\frac{\tau\theta\,\big(T^0_2(p)-T^0_1(p)\big)}{1+\tau\theta\,(C_{p1}^{-1}+C_{p2}^{-1})}
\label{eq:dQ}
\end{equation}
in closed form; the local equation is again $\Phi(p)=\sum_k\alpha_k-1=0$, and $\theta=0$ recovers the $p$
closure.

\paragraph{Finite mass transfer ($\mu=\theta=\infty$, $\nu<\infty$)}
With $\rho=\sum_km^{\rm tr}_k$ fixed, for given $p$ and $Y=Y_2$,
\begin{equation}
T(p,Y)=\frac{(\rho e)^C/\rho-(1-Y)q_1-Yq_2}{(1-Y)c_{v1}\phi_1+Yc_{v2}\phi_2},\quad \phi_k=\frac{p+\gamma_k\pi_k}{p+\pi_k},\qquad
\mathcal R(Y)=\rho Y-m^{\rm tr}_2+\tau\nu\,(g_2-g_1)\big(p,T(p,Y)\big)=0 .
\label{eq:nu}
\end{equation}
At fixed energy $T$ decreases with $Y$ and $\partial_T(g_2-g_1)=-(s_2-s_1)<0$, so
$\mathcal R'(Y)=\rho+\tau\nu(s_2-s_1)|\partial_YT|>0$ and $Y$ is unique on the admissible interval
$[Y_{\min},\min(1-Y_{\min},(e-q_1)/(q_2-q_1))]$; if the root leaves it, $Y$ takes the bound (complete
condensation or evaporation). The outer equation is $\Phi(p)=\rho\sum_kY_kv_k(p,T)-1=0$. Only mixture
quantities enter, so $\rho_I$ and $e_I$ never appear.

\paragraph{$pTg$ closure ($\mu=\theta=\nu=\infty$)}
$T=T_{\rm sat}(p)$, $Y$ follows from the energy, and $\Phi(p)=\rho\sum_kY_kv_k-1$; a clamped $Y$ gives the same
branch as the finite-rate bounds, so $\Phi$ is continuous. Above the end point $p_c$ of the saturation curve
the mixture is single-phase.

\paragraph{Activation and projection}
Thermal and chemical relaxation act only in interface cells, $\epsilon_I\le\alpha_1\le1-\epsilon_I$, and
mass transfer only where $T>T_{\rm sat}(p)$ (Pelanti and Shyue 2014; Pelanti 2022). Activation is decided
once per step on $U^n$, and $U^n$ is projected onto the equilibrium manifold of the activated closure (the
same closure with $s=0$); the closure type is then frozen over the stages. An instantaneous relaxation is
a jump of the state; with the projection at the start of the step the stage increments $U_i-U^n$ remain
$O(\Delta t)$, so the stage combinations of the IMEX scheme do not extrapolate the jump, and the data are
well-prepared, as required by the AP property of stiffly accurate IMEX schemes (Theorem~\ref{thm:limits}). Finite-rate closures are not projected.

\subsubsection{Closure output}
The closure returns $\alpha_k,m_k,\varepsilon_k,p_k$ (and $T_k$) and the mixture pressure
$\bar p^{\rm cl}=\sum_k\alpha_kp_k$. The global equation of the stage is
\begin{equation}
G_i(P)\equiv\bar p^{\rm cl}_i\big(s_i,X^{\rm tr}_i,(\rho e)^C_i;\ \mu,\theta,\nu\big)-P_i=0 :
\label{eq:global}
\end{equation}
the pressure of a cell equals the thermodynamic response of its material to the volume strain of the stage,
a nonlinear and relaxing generalisation of $p^{n+1}=p^*-\tau\rho c^2\partial_xu^{n+1}$.

\subsection{Asymptotic limits}
\label{sec:limits}
\begin{theorem}[Relaxation limits]\label{thm:limits}
Assume that the local closures of Sec.~\ref{sec:closures} have non-singular Jacobians at their solutions
(guaranteed by the monotonicity shown there) and that the global Jacobian of \eqref{eq:global} is
non-singular (Sec.~\ref{sec:solver}).
(i) As $\mu\to\infty$, $\theta\to\infty$ or $\nu\to\infty$, the discrete solution of every stage converges to
that of the corresponding equilibrium closure ($p$, $pT$, $pTg$) with errors $O(1/(\tau\mu))$,
$O(1/(\tau\theta))$, $O(1/(\tau\nu))$, uniformly in $\Delta x$; the limit scheme is a consistent discretisation of
the equilibrium model.
(ii) For $\mu=0$, $\theta=0$, $\nu=0$ the closure reduces exactly to the frozen (non-relaxing) scheme.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
(i) Mechanical relaxation: with $\kappa=1/(\tau\mu)$ the finite-$\mu$ closure reads $p_1-p_2=\kappa R$. For
$\kappa=0$, $p_1=p_2=p$, $p_I=p$, and $\omega_ks+\chi_kR=\alpha_k-\alpha^{\rm tr}_k$, so \eqref{eq:gibbs} and
\eqref{eq:partition} become exactly the $p$ closure \eqref{eq:pclosure}. By the implicit function theorem the
local solution is smooth in $\kappa$, so it differs from the $\kappa=0$ solution by $O(\kappa)$ with a constant
that is local to the cell; non-singularity of the global Jacobian carries this to the global solution.
Thermal relaxation: \eqref{eq:dQ} gives $T_1-T_2=O(1/(\tau\theta))$ and the limit equations are \eqref{eq:pT},
whose admissible root is unique. Chemical relaxation: \eqref{eq:nu} gives
$|g_2-g_1|=|\rho Y-m^{\rm tr}_2|/(\tau\nu)=O(1/(\tau\nu))$ (unless $Y$ is clamped), and the limit is the $pTg$
closure. Consistency of the limit scheme, e.g.\ for $\mu$: linearising the $p$ closure, each phase is
isentropic and isobaric, $\mathrm d\alpha_k=s\,\frac{\alpha_k/(\rho_kc_k^2)}{\sum_j\alpha_j/(\rho_jc_j^2)}$; with
$s=\Delta t\,\partial_xu$ this is
\[
\partial_t\alpha_1+u\,\partial_x\alpha_1=\frac{\alpha_1\alpha_2(\rho_2c_2^2-\rho_1c_1^2)}{\alpha_1\rho_2c_2^2+\alpha_2\rho_1c_1^2}\,\partial_xu,
\]
the volume equation of the Kapila model. In time the implicit tableau is stiffly accurate and L-stable
(Sec.~\ref{sec:time}), every stage lies on the equilibrium manifold and $U^{n+1}$ is the last stage, so the
limits $\mu\to\infty$ and $\Delta t\to0$ commute.
(ii) $\mu=0$ gives $R=0$, $\theta=0$ gives $\Delta Q=0$, $\nu=0$ gives $Y=Y^{\rm tr}$. Each phase then does Gibbs
work at its own pressure, $\varepsilon_k-\varepsilon^{\rm tr}_k=-p_k(\alpha_k-\alpha^{\rm tr}_k)+\Lambda_k(\delta)$, with
$\alpha_k=\alpha^{\rm tr}_k/(1-s)$: the six-equation TV--IMEX scheme without relaxation. The reduction is exact,
since a vanishing rate and a switched-off relaxation evaluate the same formulas.
\end{proof}
\begin{proposition}[Formal low-Mach limit]\label{prop:lowmach}
Non-dimensionalise pressure by $\rho_rc_r^2$, keep $\Delta t$ and $\Delta x$ fixed, and take
well-prepared data $P=P_0+M^2P_2$ with $P_0$ constant and $\mathbf u=O(1)$. Then, formally as $M\to0$,
every implicit stage satisfies
\begin{enumerate}
\item $(\delta\cdot u_f)_i=O(M^2)$: the face velocities are discretely divergence-free;
\item $\delta\cdot\big(\bar u^*_f-\tau\,\delta P_2/(\bar\rho_f\Delta x)\big)=O(M^2)$: $P_2$ solves the discrete
Poisson equation of a face-velocity projection;
\item the cell velocities are corrected by the density-weighted face average of $P_2$ ($P_0$ cancels),
an approximate projection;
\item the acoustic complement vanishes ($\varphi_f=0$ once $M<a_{ii}\,\mathrm{CFL}$), the explicit part
carries only the material-speed Rusanov dissipation, and the closure returns equilibrium states, all
independently of $M$.
\end{enumerate}
The limit scheme is therefore a consistent discretisation of the incompressible mixture Euler equations
(explicit convection plus a face-velocity projection), with a time step independent of $M$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Formal derivation]
In the non-dimensional variables the pressure gradient in the face velocity \eqref{eq:faces} carries the
factor $1/M^2$: $u_f=\bar u^*_f-\frac{\tau}{M^2\bar\rho_f}\frac{\delta P}{\Delta x}+\dots$, while the linearised
closure is $P_i-P^*_i=-\tau K_i(\delta\cdot u_f)_i+\dots$ with $K=\rho c_W^2=O(1)$. Inserting $P=P_0+M^2P_2$ and
collecting powers of $M$, the order $M^{-2}$ gives $(\delta\cdot u_f)_i=O(M^2)$ (item 1) and the order $1$ gives
item 2. In the momentum update $(\rho u)^{n+1}=(\rho u)^*-\tau\,\delta p_f/\Delta x$ the face pressure is a
density-weighted average of $P$, in which $P_0$ cancels, leaving $\tau\,\delta(\overline{P_2})/\Delta x$ (item 3).
For item 4, $c\tau/\Delta x=a_{ii}\mathrm{CFL}\,c/a=O(1/M)$, so $\varphi=0$ once $M<a_{ii}\mathrm{CFL}$; the explicit
part contains only the material-speed dissipation (Sec.~\ref{sec:explicit}); the closure returns equilibrium
states by Theorem~\ref{thm:limits}.
\end{proof}
The limit alone does not exclude $O(1/M)$ dissipation at intermediate Mach numbers or in the start-up
ramp (Dellacherie 2010; Battisti and Boscheri, Sec.~3.6). Term by term: the explicit dissipation is
$a\Delta x$ with the material speed; the pressure-diffusion term changes $u_f$ by
$\varphi\psi\,\Delta P/(Z_i+Z_{i+1})\sim\varphi\psi\tfrac M2\tfrac{\Delta x}LU$, i.e.\ $O(M)$ relative to $U$; the
velocity-jump term acts on the vertex divergence (Sec.~\ref{sec:multid}), so on a smooth divergence-free
field its contribution to $p_f$ relative to $\rho U^2$ is $\varphi\psi[O(M\Delta x/L)+O((\Delta x/L)^3/M)]$, and since
$\sup_M\varphi/M=1/(4a_{ii}\mathrm{CFL})$ this is small uniformly in $M$. The Taylor--Green vortex of Sec.~5 confirms the proposition at fixed $\Delta t$:
the face divergence is proportional to $M^2$ down to $M=8.45\times10^{-7}$, the velocity and pressure errors are
independent of $M$ for $M\le10^{-3}$, $\|\rho-1\|_2/M^2\to0.220$, with no rejected step and one Newton
iteration per stage. To reach this the solver must itself be independent of $M$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:solver}).

\subsection{Global nonlinear system and its solution}
\label{sec:solver}
The residual $G_i(P)$ of \eqref{eq:global} depends on $(P_{i-1},P_i,P_{i+1})$ (five points in 2D), so its
Jacobian is tridiagonal (pentadiagonal). It is monotone: $\partial s_i/\partial P_i=r^2(1/\bar\rho_{i+1/2}+1/\bar\rho_{i-1/2})
+r\sum_f\varphi_f\psi^P_f/(Z_i+Z_{i\pm1})>0$ (the complement only adds to it), the closure pressure decreases
under expansion, $\partial\bar p^{\rm cl}/\partial s<0$ (property (E5)), and $\partial E_i/\partial P_i\approx-2r^2\bar p/\bar\rho<0$.
Hence $\partial G_i/\partial P_i<-1$ while the off-diagonal entries are $O(r^2\rho c^2/\bar\rho)$: the Jacobian is a
diagonally dominant M-matrix of the form $I-\tau^2\partial_x(c^2\partial_x)$.

\paragraph{Newton iteration}
We solve $G(P)=0$ by Newton's method with a finite-difference Jacobian (each cell's three or five pressures
perturbed, one local closure per perturbation) and a backtracking line search (step halved, at most 40
times) that accepts a step when every local closure is admissible and the Armijo condition
$\|G\|_{\rm new}<(1-10^{-4}\lambda)\|G\|$ holds. Three ingredients make the iteration independent of the Mach
number.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The unknown is the pressure perturbation $P-P_{\rm ref}$, $P_{\rm ref}$ the minimum of the initial mixture
pressure. Face pressure, face velocity and momentum contain only pressure differences or weighted
averages with weights summing to one; the absolute pressure is restored only in the energy flux and in the
closure. In exact arithmetic the scheme is unchanged; in floating point the round-off of the stored
pressure, amplified by $\nu^2$ through the face velocities and the closure, is $\nu^2\epsilon_m\rho U^2$, independent of
the background pressure (Klein 1995; Sesterhenn et al.\ 1999).
\item Finite-difference steps and the step criterion use the natural pressure scale $s_i=N_i/D_i$, with
$N_i=|P_i|+K_i+P_{E,i}$ the residual scale and $D_i=1+(K_i+P_{E,i})\sum_f(\tau/h_f)k_f\approx1+2d\nu^2$ the scale
of $\partial G_i/\partial P_i$ ($k_f$ the pressure coefficient of the face velocity, $d$ the dimension). The step is
$10^{-7}s_i$, so the induced volume change and the relative round-off of the derivative are both
independent of $M$.
\item The linear systems are solved by the Thomas algorithm (1D, non-periodic) or by BiCGStab with the
two-level preconditioner $M^{-1}r=D^{-1}r+\mathbf 1\,(\mathbf 1^{\rm T}r)/(\mathbf 1^{\rm T}J\mathbf 1)$, Jacobi plus a
constant coarse space (Nicolaides 1987). A constant shift of $P$ changes $G$ only by $O(1)$ while the
diagonal is $\approx1+2d\nu^2$, so after Jacobi preconditioning the constant mode has the eigenvalue
$\approx1/(2d\nu^2)$; the coarse correction moves it to $[1,2]$ and the preconditioned spectrum becomes
independent of $M$.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Convergence criterion}
The closure determines the pressure only to $\epsilon_m(p+\pi)$, and the round-off $\epsilon_m|E|$ of the
conservative total energy enters each phase pressure through $\Lambda_k(\delta)$; the attainable accuracy of
$G_i$ is therefore $\epsilon_m\max(K_i,P_{E,i})$, $K=\rho c_W^2$ (Wood bulk modulus, so that $G/K$ is the residual
of the volume constraint) and $P_{E,i}=\sum_k(|\varepsilon_k|+|m_kq_k|)/\sum_k\partial\varepsilon_k/\partial p$. We stop when
$\max_i|G_i|/N_i<10^{-13}$, or when the full Newton step satisfies
$\max_i|\delta P_i|/(|P_i-P_{\rm ref}|+s_i)<10^{-13}$, or when the residual is below the round-off floor of the
closure,
\begin{equation}
|G_i|<10^{-13}N_i+\sigma_i,\qquad
\sigma_i=\epsilon_m\Big(|(\rho e)^C|+\sum_k|\varepsilon^{\rm tr}_k|+\sum_k|m_kq_k|\Big)\sum_k\lambda_k\frac{\gamma_k-1}{\gamma_k(p+\pi_k)}\Big/|\Phi'(p)| :
\label{eq:floor}
\end{equation}
$D_0=(\rho e)^C-\sum_k\varepsilon^{\rm tr}_k$ is a difference of large numbers carrying $\epsilon_m(\cdots)$, it enters
phase $k$ with $\lambda_k$ and reaches the pressure through $\partial\alpha_k/\partial A_k=(\gamma_k-1)/(\gamma_k(p+\pi_k))$
and $\Phi'(p)$; below $\sigma_i$ Newton cannot improve the residual.

\paragraph{Admissibility}
A stage is inadmissible if $Q^e$ has $\rho\le0$, if a closure input is inadmissible ($\alpha^{\rm tr}_k\le m^{\rm tr}_kb_k$,
$m^{\rm tr}_k\le0$ or $N_k\le0$), or if Newton does not converge. This is the implicit counterpart of the
positivity CFL condition of an explicit scheme; the treatment is described next.

\subsection{Time integration}
\label{sec:time}
We use the semi-implicit IMEX Runge--Kutta framework of Boscarino, Filbet and Russo with the
LSDIRK2(2,2,2) tableau
\[
\tilde A=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\ \hat c&0\end{pmatrix},\quad A=\begin{pmatrix}\gamma&0\\ 1-\gamma&\gamma\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\gamma=1-\tfrac1{\sqrt2},\ \hat c=\tfrac1{2\gamma},
\]
which is stiffly accurate ($b$ equals the last row of $A$, $\tilde b=b$), second order and L-stable. Stage $i$
computes $Q^e=U^n+\Delta t\sum_{j<i}\tilde a_{ij}K_j$ and $Q^s=U^n+\Delta t\sum_{j<i}a_{ij}K_j$, the explicit update of
Sec.~\ref{sec:explicit} on $Q^e$ with base $Q^s$, and the implicit solve of Secs.~\ref{sec:implicit_faces}--\ref{sec:solver}
with $\tau=a_{ii}\Delta t$, giving $U_i$ and $K_i=(U_i-Q^s)/\tau$; $U^{n+1}$ is the last stage. The relaxation sources
are part of the implicit tableau through the closure.

\paragraph{Time step}
The step is limited by the material speed only,
$\Delta t=C\,\big[\max_i|u_i|/\Delta x+\max_i|v_i|/\Delta y\big]^{-1}$, so acoustic waves are not resolved in time.
Where an acoustic structure is itself the object of the computation, the acoustic Courant number
$a_{ii}C\,c/|u|$ controls the dispersion error of LSDIRK2; we use $C=0.2$ by default and $C=0.05$ for the two
tests in which $u\ll c$ and the waves are acoustic (Sec.~5).

\paragraph{Start-up and a-posteriori fallback}
Starting from rest or from discontinuous data, the first step uses the acoustic time step with Courant
number $0.4$ and every following step may grow by at most a factor $1.2$ until the material CFL step is
reached; steps limited by this ramp use one backward-Euler stage. In the second LSDIRK2 stage $Q^e\ne Q^s$
($Q^e-U^n=(U_1-U^n)/(2\gamma^2)$, a factor 5.8 of the first increment) and the positivity argument of
Theorem~\ref{thm:positivity} does not apply. An inadmissible step is therefore first recomputed with the
same $\Delta t$ by one backward-Euler stage (an a-posteriori order reduction in the spirit of MOOD, Clain,
Diot and Loub\`ere 2011), for which Theorem~\ref{thm:positivity} holds; only if that fails is $\Delta t$ halved.
All fallbacks and rejections are counted and reported in Sec.~5.

\paragraph{Stiff finite rates}
For a relaxation $y'=\lambda y$, $z=\lambda\Delta t<0$, the stability function of LSDIRK2,
$R(z)=\frac{1+(1-2\gamma)z}{(1-\gamma z)^2}$, is negative for $z<-2.41$ (minimum $-0.207$ at $z\approx-8.2$): the
stage overshoots the equilibrium, as any second-order method must for some $z$. For rates in the stiff
but finite range the instantaneous closure (its limit by Theorem~\ref{thm:limits}) or backward Euler is
appropriate; in the tests of Sec.~5 $|z|\le10^{-3}$.

\subsection{Multi-dimensional extension}
\label{sec:multid}
The explicit fluxes are computed on the faces of each direction; the reconstruction is the same as in 1D
(shared limiter for the volume-transported quantities, each velocity component with its own minmod). The
face velocity \eqref{eq:faces} corrects the normal component only; the incremental mass flux carries the new
normal face velocity and the donor's tangential velocity. The volume strain is
$s_{ij}=r_x\delta_x[u_f]+r_y\delta_y[v_f]$, and the positivity bound of Theorem~\ref{thm:positivity}(ii) holds per
direction with $\tfrac14$ in place of $\tfrac12$. The shock budget of Sec.~\ref{sec:defect} uses the five-point
cross.

\paragraph{Velocity-jump term in divergence form}
The acoustic subsystem sees the velocity only through its divergence, so the vortical part of the velocity
is in the kernel of the acoustic operator and acoustic dissipation should not act on it. On the $x$-face
$(i+\tfrac12,j)$ we therefore replace the normal jump by
\begin{equation}
D^*_{i+1/2,j}=\Delta x\,\tfrac12\big(\mathrm{div}_{i+1/2,j+1/2}+\mathrm{div}_{i+1/2,j-1/2}\big),\quad
\mathrm{div}_{i+1/2,j+1/2}=\frac{u_{i+1,j}+u_{i+1,j+1}-u_{i,j}-u_{i,j+1}}{2\Delta x}+\frac{v_{i,j+1}+v_{i+1,j+1}-v_{i,j}-v_{i+1,j}}{2\Delta y},
\label{eq:vertex_div}
\end{equation}
the average of the divergences of the bilinear interpolant at the two face vertices (symmetrically on the
$y$-faces). Its properties: (i) for $y$-independent data $D^*=u_{i+1}-u_i$, bit for bit, so the 1D scheme is
unchanged; (ii) with uniform $Z$ on a periodic square grid, summation by parts gives
$\sum_{ij}\big[u_{ij}(q_{i+1/2,j}-q_{i-1/2,j})+v_{ij}(q_{i,j+1/2}-q_{i,j-1/2})\big]=-\varphi\psi\tilde Zh^2\sum_{\rm vertices}\mathrm{div}_v^2\le0$
for $q_f=\varphi\psi\tilde Z_fD^*_f$: the term is a bulk viscosity of von Neumann--Richtmyer type that dissipates
kinetic energy only through the vertex divergence; (iii) its low-Mach size is the one used in
Proposition~\ref{prop:lowmach}; (iv) the odd--even mode $u_{ij}=(-1)^i$ is still damped ($D^*=\pm2$). The
construction follows the vorticity- and stationarity-preserving schemes of Morton and Roe (2001) and
Barsukow (2019) and the low-Mach analyses of Guillard and Viozat (1999) and Dellacherie (2010).

\subsection{Summary of properties}
\label{sec:properties}
\paragraph{Well-balanced contacts (R2)}
Let $u\equiv u_0$, $p_k\equiv p$, with arbitrary $\alpha_k$, $\rho_k$ (and phase temperatures). By (E1) the cell
values satisfy $\varepsilon_k=c_k(p)\alpha_k+q_km_k$ and $\rho u=u_0\rho$; the shared-limiter reconstruction keeps these
linear relations on the faces, and \eqref{eq:rusanov} is linear in $X$, so $X^*$ satisfies them too. With
$P\equiv p$, $\bar u^*_f=u_0=\bar u^e_f$, all terms of \eqref{eq:faces} beyond the averages vanish, $u_f=u_0$,
$p_f=p$, $\Delta u_f=0$, the increments vanish, $s=0$, $X^{\rm tr}=X^*$, $E^{n+1}=E^*$ and $\delta=0$. In the $p$
closure $\alpha_k=\alpha^*_k$ solves \eqref{eq:pclosure} at $p$, and the root is unique; similarly for finite $\mu$
($R=0$) and for the thermal closures when the phase temperatures are in equilibrium. Hence $P\equiv p$ is the
solution and $(u,p)$ stay uniform. The IMEX stage states are linear combinations of such states, so the
argument holds in every stage.

\paragraph{Temperature consistency (R3)}
If every phase has uniform $(p,T_k)$, then $m_k=\rho_k\alpha_k$ and $\varepsilon_k=\rho_ke_k\alpha_k$ are proportional to
$\alpha_k$, and one linear operator applied to all three keeps the ratios, so $\rho_k,e_k,T_k$ are unchanged.

\paragraph{Conservation and mixture-energy consistency (R1, R6)}
$m_k$ (or $\rho$ with mass transfer), $\rho u$ and $E$ are in flux form and conserved to round-off;
$\sum_k\varepsilon_k=(\rho e)^C=E-\tfrac12\rho u^2$ and $\bar p=\sum_k\alpha_kp_k$ is given by the phase equations of state, so
the solution is mixture-energy consistent in the sense of Pelanti and Shyue (2014, Def.~3.1).

\paragraph{Positivity and limits}
Theorems~\ref{thm:positivity} and \ref{thm:limits} and Proposition~\ref{prop:lowmach}.

\paragraph{Parameters}
The material Courant number, the local or global Rusanov speed, the constants of the start-up ramp and of
the a-posteriori fallback, and the activation threshold $\epsilon_I$ of thermal and chemical relaxation are set
per test (Sec.~5).
