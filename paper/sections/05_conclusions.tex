\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

We have presented a second-order semi-implicit IMEX finite volume scheme for the single-velocity six-equation
two-phase model with total energy, for stiffened-gas and Noble--Abel stiffened-gas phases with mechanical, thermal and
chemical relaxation and for Mie--Gr\"uneisen phases with instantaneous mechanical relaxation, in one and two
dimensions.

The acoustic waves at low Mach numbers and the relaxation processes up to infinite rates are treated in a single
implicit step. The pressure work and the three relaxation processes, under the time-scale ordering of Pelanti
\cite{Pelanti2022}, enter one local closure per cell, which reduces to one scalar equation, in the cell pressure or,
for finite mechanical relaxation, in the volume increment; one unified formula covers every rate. Each stage then
requires a Newton solve of a nonlinear system for the cell pressures with a sparse three-point Jacobian, five-point in
2D, and the time step is restricted by the flow velocity only, whatever the Mach number and the relaxation rates. We
have proved that, at fixed mesh and under non-degeneracy assumptions, the scheme converges, for data on the
equilibrium manifold, to the corresponding equilibrium scheme as each relaxation rate tends to infinity and reduces to
the scheme without that relaxation as the rate vanishes; formally, it is asymptotic preserving in the low Mach number
limit.

At shocks, the difference between the conservative and the non-conservative mixture internal energy is distributed
between the phases according to the shock relations of the model: the physical part with the constant share for which
the shock profile of the relaxed model ends on the Hugoniot of each phase, computed by shooting, which for weak shocks
reduces to the unique consistent local share, and the discretisation error with the unique linear share that keeps
both phases admissible whenever possible. No calibrated heat exchange is needed.

The flux is split following Toro and V\'azquez-Cend\'on: all quantities, including the volume fraction, are transported
explicitly with the velocity of the explicit state by the path-conservative Rusanov scheme with material-speed
dissipation, while the pressure, its work on each phase and the increments of the mass and internal energy fluxes to the
new velocity are treated implicitly. The central implicit operator is
supplemented by Laplace dissipation of the pressure and the velocity with acoustic coefficients, weighted by the
deficit of the backward-Euler viscosity with respect to the upwind one, so that it vanishes in the low Mach number
regime; it is first order at local extrema and second order in smooth regions. The stiffly accurate, L-stable LSDIRK2
scheme gives second order in time. We have also proved that the scheme preserves moving contacts between stiffened-gas
or Noble--Abel stiffened-gas phases, at uniform temperature when thermal or chemical relaxation is active.

The numerical results confirm these properties: second-order convergence of the velocity and the volume fraction on a
smooth two-phase vortex at Mach numbers from $2.4$ to $0.024$, and of the pressure for well-prepared data, face
velocities that are discretely divergence-free to $O(M^2)$ down to $M\approx10^{-6}$ on the Taylor--Green vortex with
a time step independent of the Mach number, and agreement with exact solutions and reference results for interfaces,
single-phase and two-phase shock tubes, cavitation, evaporation and depressurisation problems, and two-dimensional
shock--bubble, Richtmyer--Meshkov, vapour-bubble compression and underwater-explosion problems, at instantaneous and
finite relaxation rates.

Future work will address an extension to higher order in space and time, unstructured meshes, and a velocity
disequilibrium model, in which the drag relaxation would enter the same local closure.
