\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

We have presented a second-order semi-implicit IMEX finite volume scheme for the single-velocity six-equation
two-phase model with total energy and mechanical, thermal and chemical relaxation, for stiffened-gas,
Noble--Abel stiffened-gas and Mie--Gr\"uneisen equations of state, in one and two space dimensions. The flux is split
following Toro and V\'azquez-Cend\'on: all phase quantities are transported explicitly with the old velocity and a
Rusanov flux whose dissipation speed is the material speed, the volume fraction being discretised by the
path-conservative Rusanov scheme with the linear path, while the pressure, its work on each phase, an incremental flux
of the transported quantities and the three relaxation processes are treated implicitly. The time step is therefore
restricted by the flow velocity only, whatever the Mach number and the relaxation rates.

The implicit pressure work and the relaxation terms enter a single local closure, which for every set of rates, finite
or infinite, reduces to one scalar equation for the cell pressure; the stage reduces to a mildly nonlinear system for
the cell pressures with a three-point (five-point in 2D) Jacobian, solved by Newton's method. The difference between
the conservative and the non-conservative mixture internal energy is distributed between the phases according to the
shock relations of the model: the physical part with the constant share for which the shock profile of the relaxed
model ends on the Hugoniot of each phase, computed by shooting, which for weak shocks reduces to the unique consistent
local share, and the discretisation error with the unique share that keeps both phases admissible for every defect. No
calibrated heat exchange is needed. The acoustic dissipation that the central implicit operator lacks is supplied at the
faces from the acoustic Riemann solution, weighted by the deficit of the implicit viscosity with respect to the upwind
one and limited to second order in smooth regions. Second order in time is obtained with the stiffly accurate,
L-stable LSDIRK2 scheme.

We have proved that the scheme preserves moving contacts and the phase temperatures under transport, that with a
limiter that keeps the reconstructed face states admissible the transported phase states of a backward-Euler stage remain
admissible under a face condition independent of the phase contents, and
that, under non-degeneracy assumptions, it converges to the corresponding equilibrium scheme as each relaxation rate tends
to infinity and reduces to the scheme without that relaxation as the rate vanishes; formally, the scheme is asymptotic
preserving in the low Mach number limit of the mixture. The numerical results confirm these properties: second-order
convergence on a smooth two-phase vortex, a Newton iteration count and a time step independent of the Mach number, face
velocities that are discretely divergence-free to $O(M^2)$ down to $M\approx10^{-6}$ on the Taylor--Green vortex, and
agreement with exact solutions and reference results for interfaces, single-phase and two-phase shock tubes, cavitation,
evaporation and depressurisation problems, and two-dimensional shock--bubble, Richtmyer--Meshkov, bubble-collapse and
underwater-explosion problems, at instantaneous and finite relaxation rates.

Future work will address an extension to higher order in space and time, unstructured meshes, and a velocity
disequilibrium model, in which the drag relaxation would enter the same local closure.
