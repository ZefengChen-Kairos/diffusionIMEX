\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The single-velocity six-equation two-phase model with relaxation \cite{SPB2009,PelantiShyue2014,Pelanti2022} is widely
used for compressible liquid--gas and liquid--vapour flows: cavitation in hydraulic machinery and fuel injectors,
flashing and evaporation fronts and the rapid depressurisation of pressurised water in nuclear reactor safety,
underwater explosions, and the shock loading of condensed materials and mixtures
\cite{SaurelPetitpasAbgrall2008,Bartak1990,PelantiShyue2019,Chiapolino2017,DeLorenzo2018,DeLorenzo2019,Schmidmayer2020,SaurelPantano2018}.
Two features make these flows difficult. First, the Mach number varies by orders of magnitude within one flow: a
liquid moving at a few metres per second is in the incompressible regime, while the gas, a vapour pocket or a
detonation product in the same domain may be transonic or carry strong shocks. Second, the phases exchange
momentum, heat and mass on time scales that range from much slower to much faster than the flow itself, so that
the same computation contains frozen, finite-rate and equilibrium regions, and phase transition through
metastable states.

Diffuse-interface models describe such flows with one set of equations in the whole domain, pure fluids, interfaces
and mixtures alike \cite{SaurelAbgrall1999a,AbgrallKarni2001,SaurelPantano2018}. They derive from the seven-equation
model of Baer and Nunziato \cite{BaerNunziato1986}, in which each phase has its own velocity and pressure. Relaxing the
velocities gives the single-velocity six-equation model with two pressures; relaxing also the pressures gives the
five-equation model of Kapila et al.\ \cite{Kapila2001,MurroneGuillard2005,AllaireClercKokh2002}, and further thermal and
chemical relaxation give the four-equation $pT$ model and the homogeneous equilibrium model
\cite{FlattenLund2011,Lund2012,Pelanti2022}. Saurel, Petitpas and Berry \cite{SPB2009} proposed to compute
interfaces and mixtures with the six-equation model and stiff pressure relaxation: its frozen sound speed is monotone in
the volume fraction, its volume-fraction equation contains only the pressure work, and the mixture total energy,
redundant for smooth flows, is added as a seventh equation (``6+1'') to recover the conservative mixture energy at
shocks. Pelanti and
Shyue \cite{PelantiShyue2014} made the approach mixture-energy-consistent and added heat and mass transfer
\cite{SaurelPetitpasAbgrall2008,Zein2010}, and Pelanti \cite{Pelanti2022} extended the relaxation procedures to arbitrary
rates and to the Noble--Abel stiffened-gas \cite{LeMetayerSaurel2016} and Mie--Gr\"uneisen equations of state.

A known difficulty of the six-equation model is the behaviour at shocks in mixtures: the model is non-conservative,
its shock relations \cite{SaurelGavrilyukRenaud2003} are not those of the scheme's numerical shock layer, and the energy
deposited in the layer is shared between the phases in a way that depends on the scheme. Saurel et al.\ \cite{SPB2009}
showed that the computed post-shock state then deviates from the five-equation solution for strong shocks, and
corrected it with an artificial heat exchange in the shock layer whose coefficient must be calibrated for each mixture
\cite{Petitpas2007}. A second difficulty is the stiffness of the model with mechanical, thermal and chemical relaxation,
of two kinds: the acoustic waves, fast compared with the flow at low Mach numbers, and the relaxation processes, whose
rates range from zero to infinity and may differ by orders of magnitude between the three processes. The numerical
methods above \cite{SPB2009,PelantiShyue2014,Pelanti2022,DeLorenzo2018}, and the seven-equation solver of \cite{SirianniRe2026}, are explicit Godunov-type
schemes with HLLC, Roe or wave-propagation solvers, which integrate the relaxation in a step of its own after a
hyperbolic step restricted by the acoustic CFL condition. Since a small amount of liquid raises the frozen sound speed
of a mixture to nearly the liquid value, a flow at a few metres per second is then advanced with the time step of a
wave moving at more than a kilometre per second, and the upwind dissipation, which scales with the sound speed,
destroys the low-Mach asymptotics of the pressure \cite{GuillardViozat1999,GuillardMurrone2004,Dellacherie2010}.
Preconditioning restores the correct scaling of the dissipation \cite{Turkel1987,Pelanti2017} and, combined with
implicit time integration, removes the time step restriction \cite{LeMartelot2013}, but at the price of large nonlinear
systems coupling all variables, whose solution is costly and whose convergence is difficult to control. Both
difficulties are avoided by treating implicitly only the terms responsible for the stiffness and keeping the nonlinear
transport explicit, which is the idea of semi-implicit methods.

Pressure-based semi-implicit methods go back to Harlow and Welch \cite{HarlowWelch1965} and Harlow and Amsden
\cite{HarlowAmsden1971}, with fundamental contributions by Casulli and Greenspan \cite{CasulliGreenspan1984}, Klein
\cite{Klein1995}, Munz et al.\ \cite{Munz2003} and Park and Munz \cite{ParkMunz2005}. Conservative, shock-capturing
versions are obtained by splitting the flux into a convective part with eigenvalues given by the flow velocity and a
pressure part carrying the acoustic waves \cite{ZhaBilgen1993,LiouSteffen1993,ToroVazquez2012}. Dumbser and Casulli
\cite{DumbserCasulli2016} combined the Toro--V\'azquez-Cend\'on splitting with an implicit pressure sub-system for
general equations of state; the resulting system is only mildly nonlinear, the nonlinearity being confined to the
diagonal through the equation of state, and is solved by the nested Newton method of Casulli and Zanolli
\cite{CasulliZanolli2012}. Higher-order and all-Mach extensions followed in finite volume and discontinuous Galerkin
frameworks \cite{TavelliDumbser2017,Boscheri2020,BoscheriPareschi2021,BoscheriTavelli2022,Boscarino2022,Orlando2022}, and
in ideal magnetohydrodynamics the Alfv\'en and magnetosonic waves have been removed successively from the explicit step
\cite{Dumbser2019,Fambri2021,BoscheriThomann2024,Dematte2024}, with a pressure-based formulation for a general equation
of state and pressure positivity enforced inside the implicit solve in \cite{Chen2026}. Time integration of second and
higher order is provided by implicit--explicit (IMEX) Runge--Kutta schemes \cite{Ascher1997,PareschiRusso2005}, whose
stiffly accurate, L-stable members preserve relaxation limits
\cite{PareschiRusso2005,BoscarinoRusso2009,BoscarinoPareschiRusso2017}. A scheme that reduces, as the Mach number tends to zero, to a
consistent discretisation of the incompressible limit with a time step independent of the Mach number is asymptotic
preserving \cite{Jin1999,KlainermanMajda1981}; such all-speed schemes have been analysed for the Euler equations in
\cite{DegondTang2011,HaackJinLiu2012,Cordier2012,Noelle2014,Dimarco2018,Boscheri2020}. These developments
concern mainly single-phase flows.

For diffuse-interface two-phase models, all-Mach and IMEX methods are still few: pressure-based and implicit--explicit
schemes have been developed for weakly compressible flows under a Baer--Nunziato type model \cite{ReAbgrall2022}, for
low-Mach flows with mass transfer \cite{Demou2022}, for a two-phase model with phasic pressures \cite{MalusaAlaia2024}
and for a two-fluid single-temperature model \cite{Lukacova2024}, and splittings of convection and acoustics have been
derived for the Baer--Nunziato \cite{TokarevaToro2017} and Kapila \cite{tenEikelder2017} models. Battisti and Boscheri \cite{BattistiBoscheri2025} designed a linearly implicit, asymptotic
preserving and well-balanced scheme for the seven-equation model, with explicit Rusanov fluxes for the convective terms,
central implicit discretisation of the pressure terms and the stiff relaxation sources solved in a separate step.
Closest to the present work, Schropff, Petitpas and Daniel \cite{Schropff2026} derived a Zha--Bilgen type splitting of
the single-velocity two-pressure model and a semi-implicit scheme in which the pressure sub-system is solved by Picard
iterations and the phase internal energies are corrected to conserve total energy; their scheme is restricted to one
space dimension and instantaneous pressure relaxation, and the dissipation of its explicit step is a multiple of the
material speed with a coefficient chosen for each test. In these schemes the two stiffnesses are thus handled
separately: the relaxation is either restricted to instantaneous pressure equilibrium or solved in a step of its own
after the pressure update.

In this work we treat both stiffnesses in a single implicit step. The pressure work and the three relaxation processes,
with finite or infinite rates under the time-scale ordering of Pelanti
\cite{Pelanti2022}, enter one local closure per cell, which reduces to a scalar equation
for the cell pressure; the implicit step is then a nonlinear system for the cell pressures, mildly nonlinear in the
sense of \cite{DumbserCasulli2016,CasulliZanolli2012}, with a three-point (five-point in 2D) Jacobian, solved by a Newton
method whose iteration count is observed to be independent of the Mach number. The resulting scheme is asymptotic
preserving with respect to both stiffnesses: it converges to the corresponding equilibrium scheme as each relaxation
rate tends to infinity, reduces to the scheme without that relaxation as the rate vanishes, and is formally asymptotic
preserving in the low Mach number limit, with a time step set by a CFL condition on the material speed only, whatever
the Mach number and the relaxation rates.

The second ingredient is the treatment of shocks. The difference between the conservative and the non-conservative
mixture internal energy, which at a shock is the dissipation of the scheme, is distributed between the phases
according to the shock relations of the model \cite{SaurelGavrilyukRenaud2003}, instead of by a calibrated correction.
We show that for weak shocks the share consistent with these relations is unique, compute for every shock strength the
constant share for which the shock profile of the relaxed model ends on the Hugoniot of each phase, and distribute the
remaining discretisation error in the unique linear proportion that keeps both phases admissible.

The discretisation follows a splitting in the manner of Zha and Bilgen \cite{ZhaBilgen1993}: all quantities are
transported with the old velocity by the path-conservative Rusanov scheme \cite{Pares2006,Castro2006}, whose dissipation
speed is the material speed, the volume fraction being advected entirely in this explicit step with a fluctuation that
keeps the volume, mass and energy of a trace phase consistent, and the implicit step carries the pressure, its work on
each phase and an incremental flux of the conservative transported quantities. The central implicit operator is
supplemented by implicit Laplace dissipation of the pressure and the velocity with acoustic coefficients, the upwind
terms of the acoustic Riemann solution, weighted by the deficit of the implicit viscosity with respect to the upwind
viscosity, so that it vanishes in the low Mach number regime, and limited so that the scheme is second order in
smooth regions and local-extremum-diminishing. Second order in time is obtained with the stiffly accurate, L-stable
LSDIRK2 scheme \cite{BoscarinoFilbetRusso2016}. The scheme applies
to stiffened-gas, Noble--Abel stiffened-gas and Mie--Gr\"uneisen equations of state, in one and two space dimensions.

Besides the relaxation limits and the low Mach number limit, we prove that the scheme preserves moving contacts (with
equal phase temperatures when thermal relaxation is active) and the temperature of each phase under transport. The
scheme is validated on twenty-two problems in one and two space dimensions (counting the single- and two-phase
Taylor--Green vortices separately): the convergence and low-Mach tests of \cite{BattistiBoscheri2025}, single-phase
Riemann problems at low and high Mach numbers and a low-Mach interface advection from \cite{Schropff2026}, and the
interface, shock, cavitation, evaporation and depressurisation problems of \cite{SPB2009,PelantiShyue2014,Pelanti2022}.
