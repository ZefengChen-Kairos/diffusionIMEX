\section{Mathematical model}
\label{sec:governing}

We consider the single-velocity six-equation two-phase model of Saurel, Petitpas and Berry (SPB 2009),
augmented with the heat- and mass-transfer terms of Pelanti and Shyue (2014) and Pelanti (2022). For phases
$k=1,2$ let $\alpha_k$ be the volume fraction ($\alpha_1+\alpha_2=1$), $\rho_k$ the density, $m_k=\alpha_k\rho_k$ the partial
density, $e_k$ the specific internal energy and $\varepsilon_k=\alpha_k\rho_ke_k$ the phase internal energy per unit mixture
volume; $p_k$, $T_k$, $c_k$, $g_k$ are the phase pressure, temperature, sound speed and Gibbs free energy. The
mixture density is $\rho=m_1+m_2$, the velocity $u$, the mixture pressure $\bar p=\alpha_1p_1+\alpha_2p_2$ and the total
energy $E=\varepsilon_1+\varepsilon_2+\tfrac12\rho u^2$. In one space dimension the model reads
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
&\partial_t\alpha_1+u\,\partial_x\alpha_1=\mathcal P+\mathcal M/\rho_I,\\
&\partial_t m_1+\partial_x(m_1u)=\mathcal M,\qquad \partial_t m_2+\partial_x(m_2u)=-\mathcal M,\\
&\partial_t(\rho u)+\partial_x(\rho u^2+\bar p)=0,\\
&\partial_t\varepsilon_1+\partial_x(\varepsilon_1u)+\alpha_1p_1\,\partial_xu=-p_I\mathcal P+\mathcal Q+e_I\mathcal M,\\
&\partial_t\varepsilon_2+\partial_x(\varepsilon_2u)+\alpha_2p_2\,\partial_xu=+p_I\mathcal P-\mathcal Q-e_I\mathcal M,\\
&\partial_t E+\partial_x\big((E+\bar p)u\big)=0,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:model}
\end{equation}
with the mechanical, thermal and chemical relaxation terms
\begin{equation}
\mathcal P=\mu(p_1-p_2),\qquad \mathcal Q=\theta(T_2-T_1),\qquad \mathcal M=\nu(g_2-g_1),\qquad
p_I=\frac{Z_2p_1+Z_1p_2}{Z_1+Z_2},\quad Z_k=\rho_kc_k .
\label{eq:sources}
\end{equation}
The three terms drive the phases towards mechanical, thermal and chemical equilibrium. Pressure relaxation
$\mathcal P$ changes the volume fraction, with the associated interfacial work, until $p_1=p_2$; temperature
relaxation $\mathcal Q$ is the interphase heat exchange, which drives $T_1=T_2$; and Gibbs-free-energy relaxation
$\mathcal M$ is the mass transfer of phase change (evaporation or condensation, $\mathcal M>0$ from phase 2 to
phase 1), which drives $g_1=g_2$. The rates $\mu,\theta,\nu\ge0$ are inverse relaxation times and may be stiff. The total-energy
equation is redundant for smooth solutions, but it is the conservative one and fixes the mixture internal
energy at shocks (the ``+1'' equation of SPB 2009). The interfacial density $\rho_I$ and energy $e_I$ cancel in
every closure used here and need not be specified. The system can be written in the non-conservative form
\begin{equation}
\partial_t\mathbf Q+\partial_x\mathbf F(\mathbf Q)+\mathbf B(\mathbf Q)\,\partial_x\mathbf Q=\mathbf S(\mathbf Q),\qquad
\mathbf Q=(\alpha_1,m_1,m_2,\rho u,\varepsilon_1,\varepsilon_2,E)^{\top},
\label{eq:general}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf B$ collects the products $u\,\partial_x\alpha_1$ and $\alpha_kp_k\,\partial_xu$ and $\mathbf S$ the relaxation terms.

\paragraph{Equations of state}
Each phase follows the Noble--Abel stiffened-gas (NASG) law with parameters $(\gamma,\pi,q,q',c_v,b)$,
\begin{equation}
p(\rho,e)=\frac{(\gamma-1)\rho(e-q)}{1-\rho b}-\gamma\pi,\qquad
v(p,T)=\frac{(\gamma-1)c_vT}{p+\pi}+b,\qquad e(p,T)=c_vT\,\frac{p+\gamma\pi}{p+\pi}+q,
\label{eq:nasg}
\end{equation}
with $g=(\gamma c_v-q')T-c_vT\ln\big(T^\gamma/(p+\pi)^{\gamma-1}\big)+bp+q$ and $c^2=\gamma(p+\pi)/\big(\rho(1-\rho b)\big)$; $b=0$
gives the stiffened gas. The form used by the scheme is the internal energy of phase $k$ in a cell, where it
occupies the volume $\alpha_k$ with mass $m_k$, at pressure $p$,
\begin{equation}
\varepsilon_k(\alpha_k,m_k,p)=\frac{(p+\gamma_k\pi_k)(\alpha_k-m_kb_k)}{\gamma_k-1}+m_kq_k ,
\label{eq:eps_nasg}
\end{equation}
which is affine in $(\alpha_k,m_k)$ at fixed $p$ and explicitly invertible for $\alpha_k$. The admissible set of a phase,
$\{\alpha>0,\ m>0,\ \alpha-mb>0,\ \varepsilon-mq-(\alpha-mb)\pi>0\}$, is a convex cone. The saturation temperature
$T_{\rm sat}(p)$ solves $g_1(p,T)=g_2(p,T)$. Detonation products and nitromethane are described by
Mie--Gr\"uneisen laws $p=\Gamma\rho(e-\varepsilon_r(\rho))+p_r(\rho)$ (JWL, Cochran--Chan), whose reference curve is an
isentrope, $\varepsilon_r'=p_r/\rho^2$; in phase-volume form
$\varepsilon_k=m_k\varepsilon_r(\rho_k)+\alpha_k(p-p_r(\rho_k))/\Gamma_k$, and admissibility is $c^2>0$.

\paragraph{Relaxed models and wave speeds}
For $\mu\to\infty$ the model reduces to the five-equation pressure-equilibrium model of Kapila et al.; adding
$\theta\to\infty$ gives the four-equation $pT$ model and adding $\nu\to\infty$ the homogeneous equilibrium model ($pTg$)
(Pelanti 2022). The sound speeds satisfy the subcharacteristic ordering
\begin{equation}
c_{pTg}\le c_{pT}\le c_p\le c_f,\qquad c_f^2=\sum_kY_kc_k^2,\qquad \frac1{\rho c_p^2}=\sum_k\frac{\alpha_k}{\rho_kc_k^2},
\label{eq:subchar}
\end{equation}
with $Y_k=m_k/\rho$. The eigenvalues of \eqref{eq:model} are $u$ and $u\pm c_f$, so a fully explicit scheme is restricted
by
\begin{equation}
\Delta t\le\mathrm{CFL}\,\min_\Omega\frac{\Delta x}{|u|+c_f},
\label{eq:cfl_explicit}
\end{equation}
which becomes prohibitive when the flow is slow compared with the sound speed, while the relaxation terms
add their own stiffness.

\subsection{Non-dimensional form}
\label{sec:nondim}
To identify the stiff terms we introduce reference values common to both phases (the mixture is
characterised by one Mach number, as in Re and Abgrall and in Battisti and Boscheri): length $L_0$,
velocity $u_0$, time $\tau_0=L_0/u_0$, density $\rho_0$ and pressure $p_0$, with internal and total energies
scaled by $p_0$. With $\epsilon=\gamma_0M^2$, $M=u_0/c_0$, $c_0^2=\gamma_0p_0/\rho_0$, and dropping the tildes,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
&\partial_t\alpha_1+u\,\partial_x\alpha_1=\tilde\mu(p_1-p_2)+\dots,\qquad
\partial_t m_k+\partial_x(m_ku)=\pm\tilde\nu\,(g_2-g_1),\\
&\partial_t(\rho u)+\partial_x(\rho u^2)+\frac1\epsilon\,\partial_x\bar p=0,\\
&\partial_t\varepsilon_k+\partial_x(\varepsilon_ku)+\alpha_kp_k\,\partial_xu=\mp p_I\tilde\mu(p_1-p_2)\pm\tilde\theta(T_2-T_1)+\dots,\\
&\partial_t E+\partial_x\big((E+\bar p)u\big)=0,\qquad E=\textstyle\sum_k\varepsilon_k+\tfrac12\epsilon\,\rho u^2,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:nondim}
\end{equation}
with the dimensionless rates $\tilde\mu=\mu p_0\tau_0$, $\tilde\theta=\theta T_0\tau_0/p_0$ and $\tilde\nu=\nu p_0\tau_0/\rho_0^2$. Two
sources of stiffness appear. The pressure gradient in the momentum equation carries $1/\epsilon$; it is coupled to
the pressure work $\alpha_kp_k\,\partial_xu$ in the phase energy equations and to the enthalpy part of the energy flux,
$(E+\bar p)u=\tfrac12\epsilon\rho u^2u+(\rho e+\bar p)u$, which together determine the pressure response to compression.
The relaxation terms carry $\tilde\mu,\tilde\theta,\tilde\nu$, which may be arbitrarily large. Both groups are treated
implicitly by the scheme of Sec.~\ref{sec:method}, the second inside the same local closure as the first.
