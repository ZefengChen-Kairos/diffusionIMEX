\section{Governing equations}
\label{sec:governing}

We consider the single-velocity six-equation two-phase model of Saurel, Petitpas and Berry (SPB 2009),
augmented with the heat- and mass-transfer terms of Pelanti and Shyue (2014) and Pelanti (2022). Each phase
has its own pressure, temperature and Gibbs free energy; three rates control their relaxation towards
mechanical, thermal and chemical equilibrium.

\subsection{Six-equation model with mechanical, thermal and chemical relaxation}
\label{sec:model}
For phases $k=1,2$ let $\alpha_k$ be the volume fraction ($\alpha_1+\alpha_2=1$), $\rho_k$ the density, $m_k=\alpha_k\rho_k$
the partial density, $e_k$ the specific internal energy and $\varepsilon_k=\alpha_k\rho_ke_k$ the phase internal
energy per unit mixture volume; $p_k$, $T_k$, $c_k$, $g_k$ are the phase pressure, temperature, sound speed
and Gibbs free energy. The mixture density is $\rho=m_1+m_2$, the velocity $u$, the mixture pressure
$\bar p=\alpha_1p_1+\alpha_2p_2$ and the total energy $E=\varepsilon_1+\varepsilon_2+\tfrac12\rho u^2$. In one space dimension
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
&\partial_t\alpha_1+u\,\partial_x\alpha_1=\mathcal P+\mathcal M/\rho_I,\\
&\partial_t m_1+\partial_x(m_1u)=\mathcal M,\qquad \partial_t m_2+\partial_x(m_2u)=-\mathcal M,\\
&\partial_t(\rho u)+\partial_x(\rho u^2+\bar p)=0,\\
&\partial_t\varepsilon_1+\partial_x(\varepsilon_1u)+\alpha_1p_1\,\partial_xu=-p_I\mathcal P+\mathcal Q+e_I\mathcal M,\\
&\partial_t\varepsilon_2+\partial_x(\varepsilon_2u)+\alpha_2p_2\,\partial_xu=+p_I\mathcal P-\mathcal Q-e_I\mathcal M,\\
&\partial_t E+\partial_x\big((E+\bar p)u\big)=0,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:model}
\end{equation}
with the relaxation terms
\begin{equation}
\mathcal P=\mu(p_1-p_2),\qquad \mathcal Q=\theta(T_2-T_1),\qquad \mathcal M=\nu(g_2-g_1),\qquad
p_I=\frac{Z_2p_1+Z_1p_2}{Z_1+Z_2},\quad Z_k=\rho_kc_k .
\label{eq:sources}
\end{equation}
The rates $\mu$ [Pa$^{-1}$s$^{-1}$], $\theta$ [Pa\,s$^{-1}$K$^{-1}$] and $\nu$ [Pa\,kg$^2$s$^{-1}$J$^{-2}$] are non-negative;
$\mathcal M>0$ transfers mass from phase 2 to phase 1. The total-energy equation is a consequence of the others
for smooth solutions, but it is the conservative one: at shocks the non-conservative phase energy equations
are not reliable, and the conserved total energy fixes the mixture internal energy (the ``+1'' equation of
SPB 2009). The interfacial density $\rho_I$ and energy $e_I$ cancel in every closure used here (Pelanti and
Shyue 2014, Sec.~2.3; see Sec.~\ref{sec:closure}) and need not be specified. With the impedance-weighted
interfacial pressure $p_I$ the entropy produced by mechanical relaxation is non-negative.

\subsection{Relaxation hierarchy and limit models}
\label{sec:hierarchy}
Instantaneous mechanical relaxation, $\mu\to\infty$, gives the five-equation pressure-equilibrium model of
Kapila et al.; adding $\theta\to\infty$ gives the four-equation pressure--temperature ($pT$) equilibrium model,
and adding $\nu\to\infty$ the homogeneous equilibrium model ($pTg$, equal Gibbs free energies) (Pelanti 2022,
Sec.~3). The sound speeds of the hierarchy satisfy the subcharacteristic ordering
\begin{equation}
c_{pTg}\le c_{pT}\le c_p\le c_f,\qquad c_f^2=\sum_kY_kc_k^2,\qquad \frac1{\rho c_p^2}=\sum_k\frac{\alpha_k}{\rho_kc_k^2},
\label{eq:subchar}
\end{equation}
with $Y_k=m_k/\rho$, $c_f$ the frozen and $c_p$ the Wood sound speed; $c_{pT}$ and $c_{pTg}$ are given in
Pelanti (2022, eqs.~19, 25). The equilibrium models provide the exact solutions against which the scheme is
tested in Sec.~5, and their equations are the limits that the scheme must reproduce
(Theorem~\ref{thm:limits}).

\subsection{Equations of state}
\label{sec:eos}

\subsubsection{Noble--Abel stiffened gas}
Each phase has parameters $(\gamma,\pi,q,q',c_v,b)$; $b=0$ is the stiffened gas. With $v=1/\rho$,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
&p(\rho,e)=\frac{(\gamma-1)\rho(e-q)}{1-\rho b}-\gamma\pi,\qquad
v(p,T)=\frac{(\gamma-1)c_vT}{p+\pi}+b,\qquad e(p,T)=c_vT\,\frac{p+\gamma\pi}{p+\pi}+q,\\
&s=c_v\ln\frac{T^\gamma}{(p+\pi)^{\gamma-1}}+q',\qquad
g=(\gamma c_v-q')T-c_vT\ln\frac{T^\gamma}{(p+\pi)^{\gamma-1}}+bp+q,\qquad
c^2=\frac{\gamma(p+\pi)}{\rho(1-\rho b)} .
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:nasg}
\end{equation}
The form used throughout the scheme is the internal energy of phase $k$ in a cell, where it occupies the
volume $\alpha_k$ with mass $m_k$, at pressure $p$:
\begin{equation}
\varepsilon_k(\alpha_k,m_k,p)=\frac{(p+\gamma_k\pi_k)(\alpha_k-m_kb_k)}{\gamma_k-1}+m_kq_k .
\label{eq:eps_nasg}
\end{equation}
For given $\alpha_k$, $m_k$ and mixture internal energy $\rho e=\sum_k\varepsilon_k$ it is linear in $p$, which gives the
mixture pressure of the pressure-equilibrium model,
\begin{equation}
p=\frac{\rho e-\sum_km_kq_k-\sum_k\gamma_k\pi_k\frac{\alpha_k-m_kb_k}{\gamma_k-1}}{\sum_k\frac{\alpha_k-m_kb_k}{\gamma_k-1}} .
\label{eq:pmix_nasg}
\end{equation}
The saturation curve $T_{\rm sat}(p)$ solves $g_1(p,T)=g_2(p,T)$; for stiffened gases it is a genuine phase
equilibrium only where $v_2(p,T_{\rm sat})>v_1(p,T_{\rm sat})$, up to an end point $p_c$ where $v_1=v_2$.

\subsubsection{Mie--Gr\"uneisen phases}
The nitromethane test of SPB 2009 (Cochran--Chan) and the detonation products of Pelanti (2022) (JWL) use
\begin{equation}
p(\rho,e)=\Gamma\rho\big(e-\varepsilon_r(\rho)\big)+p_r(\rho),\qquad \Gamma={\rm const},
\label{eq:mg}
\end{equation}
with $p_r=A_1x^{E_1}-A_2x^{E_2}$, $x=\rho/\rho_{\rm ref}$, for Cochran--Chan and
$p_r=a\,e^{-r_1\rho_0/\rho}+b\,e^{-r_2\rho_0/\rho}$ for JWL. In both the reference curve is an isentrope,
$\varepsilon_r'(\rho)=p_r(\rho)/\rho^2$, which is the only property used below. In phase-volume form, with $\rho_k=m_k/\alpha_k$,
\begin{equation}
\varepsilon_k(\alpha_k,m_k,p)=m_k\varepsilon_r(\rho_k)+\alpha_k\frac{p-p_r(\rho_k)}{\Gamma_k},\qquad
c^2=\frac{(\Gamma+1)(p-p_r(\rho))}{\rho}+p_r'(\rho),
\label{eq:eps_mg}
\end{equation}
admissibility is $c^2>0$, and the mixture pressure is again linear in $p$ (Pelanti 2022, eq.~28). The
Mie--Gr\"uneisen phases are used with mechanical relaxation only.

\subsubsection{Properties of the equations of state used by the scheme}
\label{sec:eos_properties}
The scheme relies on five properties of \eqref{eq:eps_nasg}:
\begin{itemize}
\item[(E1)] at fixed $p$, $\varepsilon_k$ is affine in $(\alpha_k,m_k)$: any linear transport operator applied to
$(\alpha_k,m_k,\varepsilon_k)$ preserves a uniform pressure, so contacts are not perturbed (Sec.~\ref{sec:properties});
\item[(E2)] \eqref{eq:eps_nasg} can be solved explicitly for $\alpha_k$: the discrete Gibbs closure is algebraic
(Sec.~\ref{sec:closure});
\item[(E3)] $v(p,T)$ and $e(p,T)$ are explicit and $e$ is linear in $T$: the temperature is explicit in the $pT$,
$pTg$ and finite-rate closures;
\item[(E4)] $g_1=g_2$ defines $T_{\rm sat}(p)$: the $pTg$ closure and the activation of mass transfer;
\item[(E5)] convexity, $c_k^2>0$, $\partial v/\partial p|_T<0$, $c_p>0$: the residuals of all local closures are
monotone in $p$ and their roots unique.
\end{itemize}
For Mie--Gr\"uneisen phases (E1) holds when each phase has the same density on both sides of an interface
(the setting of SPB 2009 Sec.~4.2), (E2) is replaced by a one-dimensional monotone root (Sec.~\ref{sec:closure}),
(E3)--(E4) are not used, and (E5) follows from $c^2>0$. The admissible set of an NASG phase is
$\{\alpha>0,\ m>0,\ \alpha-mb>0,\ \varepsilon-mq-(\alpha-mb)\pi>0\}$, a convex cone defined by linear constraints.
