\section{System of governing equations}
\label{sec:governing}
We consider the single-velocity six-equation two-phase model of Saurel, Petitpas and Berry \cite{SPB2009}, with the
mixture total energy as additional equation and with the heat- and mass-transfer terms of Pelanti and Shyue
\cite{PelantiShyue2014} and Pelanti \cite{Pelanti2022}. In vector form it reads
\begin{equation}
\left\{
\begin{aligned}
&\frac{\partial\alpha_1}{\partial t}+\mathbf u\cdot\nabla\alpha_1=\mathcal P+\frac{\mathcal M}{\rho_I},\\
&\frac{\partial m_1}{\partial t}+\nabla\cdot(m_1\mathbf u)=\mathcal M,\qquad \frac{\partial m_2}{\partial t}+\nabla\cdot(m_2\mathbf u)=-\mathcal M,\\
&\frac{\partial(\rho\mathbf u)}{\partial t}+\nabla\cdot\big(\rho\mathbf u\otimes\mathbf u+\bar p\,\mathbf I\big)=\mathbf 0,\\
&\frac{\partial\varepsilon_1}{\partial t}+\nabla\cdot(\varepsilon_1\mathbf u)+\alpha_1p_1\,\nabla\cdot\mathbf u=-p_I\mathcal P+\mathcal Q+e_I\mathcal M,\\
&\frac{\partial\varepsilon_2}{\partial t}+\nabla\cdot(\varepsilon_2\mathbf u)+\alpha_2p_2\,\nabla\cdot\mathbf u=p_I\mathcal P-\mathcal Q-e_I\mathcal M,\\
&\frac{\partial E}{\partial t}+\nabla\cdot\big((E+\bar p)\mathbf u\big)=0 .
\end{aligned}\right.
\label{eq:model}
\end{equation}
The first equation evolves the volume fraction of phase 1, the next two enforce the conservation of the mass of each
phase, the fourth governs the mixture momentum (one equation per spatial direction), the next two account for the
internal energy of each phase, with its pressure work, and the last for the conservation of the mixture total energy.
Here, for phases $k=1,2$, $\alpha_k$ is the volume fraction ($\alpha_1+\alpha_2=1$), $\rho_k$ the density, $m_k=\alpha_k\rho_k$ the partial
density, $e_k$ the specific internal energy and $\varepsilon_k=\alpha_k\rho_ke_k$ the phase internal energy per unit mixture volume;
$p_k$, $T_k$, $c_k$ and $g_k$ are the phase pressure, temperature, sound speed and Gibbs free energy. The mixture density is
$\rho=m_1+m_2$, $\mathbf u=[u,v]^{\mathsf T}$ is the velocity field, $\bar p=\alpha_1p_1+\alpha_2p_2$ the mixture pressure, and $E$ the total energy
per unit volume, the sum of the phase internal energies and the kinetic energy,
\begin{equation}
E=\varepsilon_1+\varepsilon_2+k,\qquad k=\tfrac12\rho|\mathbf u|^2 .
\label{eq:energy}
\end{equation}
The right-hand sides are the mechanical, thermal and chemical relaxation terms
\begin{equation}
\mathcal P=\mu(p_1-p_2),\qquad \mathcal Q=\theta(T_2-T_1),\qquad \mathcal M=\nu(g_2-g_1),\qquad
p_I=\frac{Z_2p_1+Z_1p_2}{Z_1+Z_2},\quad Z_k=\rho_kc_k .
\label{eq:sources}
\end{equation}
The three terms drive the phases towards equilibrium:
\begin{itemize}
\item mechanical relaxation $\mathcal P$: the volume fraction changes, with the associated interfacial work, until $p_1=p_2$;
\item thermal relaxation $\mathcal Q$: interphase heat exchange, which drives $T_1=T_2$;
\item chemical relaxation $\mathcal M$: mass transfer by phase change (evaporation or condensation, $\mathcal M>0$
from phase 2 to phase 1), which drives $g_1=g_2$.
\end{itemize}
The rate parameters $\mu,\theta,\nu\ge0$ set the speed of the three processes and may be stiff. The total-energy equation is
redundant for smooth solutions, but it is the conservative one and fixes the mixture internal energy at shocks (the
``+1'' equation of \cite{SPB2009}). The interfacial density $\rho_I$ and the specific internal energy $e_I$ of the
transferred mass are taken, for definiteness, as the mixture values, $\rho_I=\rho$ and $e_I=(\varepsilon_1+\varepsilon_2)/\rho$. With the
ordering of time scales used in this work (mechanical and thermal equilibrium whenever mass transfer acts, $\mu=\theta=\infty$
if $\nu>0$), mass transfer takes place at $p_1=p_2=p$ and $T_1=T_2=T$. There, by the Gibbs relation
$m_kT_k\,\mathrm Ds_k=\mathrm D\varepsilon_k+p_k\,\mathrm D\alpha_k-h_k\,\mathrm Dm_k$ of each phase, the contributions of $\rho_I$ and $e_I$ to the entropy
production of the two phases cancel, and the mixture entropy production of the transfer is
$\mathcal M(g_2-g_1)/T=\nu(g_2-g_1)^2/T\ge0$ for any $\rho_I$, $e_I$; for the same reason $\rho_I$ and $e_I$ do not enter the closures of
Sec.~\ref{sec:relaxation}.

An equation of state $p_k=p_k(\rho_k,e_k)$ is required for each phase to close the system. In this work we consider two
families: the Noble--Abel stiffened-gas law, for which the internal energy is affine in the pressure (Sec.~\ref{sec:nasg}),
and Mie--Gr\"uneisen laws with nonlinear reference curves (Sec.~\ref{sec:mg}).

Letting $\mathbf Q=(\alpha_1,m_1,m_2,\rho\mathbf u,\varepsilon_1,\varepsilon_2,E)^{\mathsf T}$ denote the vector of unknowns, system \eqref{eq:model} can be
written in compact form as
\begin{equation}
\partial_t\mathbf Q+\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathcal F}(\mathbf Q)+\mathbf B(\mathbf Q)\cdot\nabla\mathbf Q=\mathbf S(\mathbf Q),
\label{eq:compact}
\end{equation}
where $\boldsymbol{\mathcal F}$ collects the flux vectors, $\mathbf B$ the non-conservative products and $\mathbf S$ the relaxation terms. For
one-dimensional flows aligned with the $x$-direction, \eqref{eq:model} reads
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\begin{bmatrix}\alpha_1\\ m_1\\ m_2\\ \rho u\\ \varepsilon_1\\ \varepsilon_2\\ E\end{bmatrix}
+\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\begin{bmatrix}0\\ m_1u\\ m_2u\\ \rho u^2+\bar p\\ \varepsilon_1u\\ \varepsilon_2u\\ (E+\bar p)u\end{bmatrix}
+\begin{bmatrix}u\,\partial_x\alpha_1\\0\\0\\0\\ \alpha_1p_1\,\partial_xu\\ \alpha_2p_2\,\partial_xu\\0\end{bmatrix}
=\begin{bmatrix}\mathcal P+\mathcal M/\rho_I\\ \mathcal M\\ -\mathcal M\\ 0\\ -p_I\mathcal P+\mathcal Q+e_I\mathcal M\\ p_I\mathcal P-\mathcal Q-e_I\mathcal M\\ 0\end{bmatrix},
\label{eq:general}
\end{equation}
i.e.\ $\partial_t\mathbf Q+\partial_x\mathbf F(\mathbf Q)+\mathbf B(\mathbf Q)\,\partial_x\mathbf Q=\mathbf S(\mathbf Q)$. Without relaxation terms the system has seven eigenvalues,
\begin{equation}
\lambda_{1,7}=u\mp c_f,\qquad \lambda_{2,\dots,6}=u,
\label{eq:eigs}
\end{equation}
with the phase sound speeds and the frozen sound speed of the mixture
\begin{equation}
c_k^2=\frac{\partial p_k}{\partial\rho_k}\Big|_{e_k}+\frac{p_k}{\rho_k^2}\,\frac{\partial p_k}{\partial e_k}\Big|_{\rho_k},\qquad
c_f^2=\sum_kY_kc_k^2,\qquad Y_k=\frac{m_k}{\rho} .
\label{eq:cf}
\end{equation}
A fully explicit scheme is therefore restricted by
\begin{equation}
\Delta t\le\mathrm{CFL}\,\min_\Omega\frac{\Delta x}{|u|+c_f},
\label{eq:cfl_explicit}
\end{equation}
which becomes prohibitive when the flow is slow compared with the sound speed, while the relaxation terms add their own
stiffness.

\paragraph{Relaxed models}
For $\mu\to\infty$ the model reduces to the five-equation pressure-equilibrium model of Kapila et al.\ \cite{Kapila2001};
adding $\theta\to\infty$ gives the four-equation $pT$ model and adding $\nu\to\infty$ the homogeneous equilibrium model ($pTg$)
\cite{Pelanti2022}. Their sound speeds satisfy the subcharacteristic ordering \cite{FlattenLund2011}
\begin{equation}
c_{pTg}\le c_{pT}\le c_p\le c_f,\qquad \frac1{\rho c_p^2}=\sum_k\frac{\alpha_k}{\rho_kc_k^2} .
\label{eq:subchar}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Noble--Abel stiffened-gas equation of state}
\label{sec:nasg}
The Noble--Abel stiffened-gas (NASG) law \cite{LeMetayerSaurel2016} has the parameters $(\gamma,\pi,q,q',c_v,b)$,
\begin{equation}
p(\rho,e)=\frac{(\gamma-1)\rho(e-q)}{1-\rho b}-\gamma\pi,\qquad
v(p,T)=\frac{(\gamma-1)c_vT}{p+\pi}+b,\qquad e(p,T)=c_vT\,\frac{p+\gamma\pi}{p+\pi}+q,
\label{eq:nasg}
\end{equation}
with the Gibbs free energy $g=(\gamma c_v-q')T-c_vT\ln\big(T^\gamma/(p+\pi)^{\gamma-1}\big)+bp+q$ and the sound speed
$c^2=\gamma(p+\pi)/\big(\rho(1-\rho b)\big)$; $b=0$ gives the stiffened gas and $b=\pi=q=0$ the ideal gas. The form used by the
scheme is the internal energy of phase $k$ in a cell, where it occupies the volume $\alpha_k$ with mass $m_k$, at pressure $p$,
\begin{equation}
\varepsilon_k(\alpha_k,m_k,p)=\frac{(p+\gamma_k\pi_k)(\alpha_k-m_kb_k)}{\gamma_k-1}+m_kq_k ,
\label{eq:eps_nasg}
\end{equation}
which is affine in $(\alpha_k,m_k)$ at fixed $p$, affine in $p$ at fixed $(\alpha_k,m_k)$, and explicitly invertible for $\alpha_k$. The
admissible set of a phase, $\{\alpha>0,\ m>0,\ \alpha-mb>0,\ \varepsilon-mq-(\alpha-mb)\pi>0\}$, is a convex cone. The saturation
temperature $T_{\rm sat}(p)$ solves $g_1(p,T)=g_2(p,T)$ on the branch with $v_2>v_1$; this branch ends at a pressure $p_c$,
above which no two-phase equilibrium exists.

\subsection{Mie--Gr\"uneisen equations of state}
\label{sec:mg}
Detonation products and liquid nitromethane are described by Mie--Gr\"uneisen laws
\begin{equation}
p=\Gamma\rho\big(e-\varepsilon_r(\rho)\big)+p_r(\rho),\qquad c^2=\frac{(\Gamma+1)(p-p_r)}{\rho}+p_r'(\rho),
\label{eq:mg}
\end{equation}
with constant $\Gamma$, whose reference curve is an isentrope, $\varepsilon_r'=p_r/\rho^2$. We use the Cochran--Chan law
\cite{CochranChan1979} and the JWL law \cite{LeeHornigKury1968},
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
&\text{Cochran--Chan:}\quad p_r(\rho)=A_1x^{E_1}-A_2x^{E_2},\qquad
\varepsilon_r(\rho)=\frac1\rho\Big(\frac{A_1x^{E_1}}{E_1-1}-\frac{A_2x^{E_2}}{E_2-1}\Big),\qquad x=\frac\rho{\rho_0},\\
&\text{JWL:}\quad p_r(\rho)=Ae^{-R_1\rho_0/\rho}+Be^{-R_2\rho_0/\rho},\qquad
\varepsilon_r(\rho)=\frac{A}{R_1\rho_0}e^{-R_1\rho_0/\rho}+\frac{B}{R_2\rho_0}e^{-R_2\rho_0/\rho}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:mg_ref}
\end{equation}
In phase-volume form $\varepsilon_k=m_k\varepsilon_r(m_k/\alpha_k)+\alpha_k\big(p-p_r(m_k/\alpha_k)\big)/\Gamma_k$, which is nonlinear in $\alpha_k$; admissibility
is $c^2>0$. Mie--Gr\"uneisen phases are used with instantaneous mechanical relaxation only.

\subsection{Split form of the model}
\label{sec:split}
To handle the different time scales of the model we split the fluxes in the manner of Zha and Bilgen
\cite{ZhaBilgen1993}, which allows a different time discretisation for each sub-system according to its stiffness: every
quantity is transported by the flow velocity in one sub-system, and the pressure, its work and the relaxation terms form
the other. The terms of \eqref{eq:general} are separated into (i) the advective part, the transport of every component of
$\mathbf Q$ by the flow velocity, including the non-conservative advection $u\,\partial_x\alpha_1$ of the volume fraction; and (ii) the
pressure--relaxation part, the pressure flux $\mathbf F^p$, the pressure work of each phase $\mathbf B^p\partial_xu$ and the relaxation terms.
Explicitly,
\begin{equation}
\mathbf F^c(\mathbf Q)=\begin{bmatrix}0\\ m_1u\\ m_2u\\ \rho u^2\\ \varepsilon_1u\\ \varepsilon_2u\\ Eu\end{bmatrix},\quad
\mathbf B^c(\mathbf Q)\,\partial_x\mathbf Q=\begin{bmatrix}u\,\partial_x\alpha_1\\0\\0\\0\\0\\0\\0\end{bmatrix},\quad
\mathbf F^p(\mathbf Q)=\begin{bmatrix}0\\0\\0\\ \bar p\\0\\0\\ \bar pu\end{bmatrix},\quad
\mathbf B^p(\mathbf Q)\,\partial_xu=\begin{bmatrix}0\\0\\0\\0\\ \alpha_1p_1\\ \alpha_2p_2\\0\end{bmatrix}\partial_xu ,
\label{eq:split_fluxes}
\end{equation}
and the one-dimensional system in split form reads
\begin{equation}
\partial_t\mathbf Q+\partial_x\mathbf F^c(\mathbf Q)+\mathbf B^c(\mathbf Q)\,\partial_x\mathbf Q+\partial_x\mathbf F^p(\mathbf Q)+\mathbf B^p(\mathbf Q)\,\partial_xu=\mathbf S(\mathbf Q).
\label{eq:split}
\end{equation}
\begin{enumerate}
\item The advective sub-system is
\begin{equation}
\partial_t\mathbf Q+\partial_x\mathbf F^c(\mathbf Q)+\mathbf B^c(\mathbf Q)\,\partial_x\mathbf Q=\mathbf 0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\partial_t\mathbf Q+\mathbf A^c(\mathbf Q)\,\partial_x\mathbf Q=\mathbf 0,
\label{eq:advective}
\end{equation}
with $\mathbf A^c=\partial\mathbf F^c/\partial\mathbf Q+\mathbf B^c$, which in the variables $\mathbf Q=(\alpha_1,m_1,m_2,\rho u,\varepsilon_1,\varepsilon_2,E)^{\mathsf T}$ reads
\begin{equation}
\mathbf A^c(\mathbf Q)=\begin{bmatrix}
u&0&0&0&0&0&0\\
0&u(1-Y_1)&-uY_1&Y_1&0&0&0\\
0&-uY_2&u(1-Y_2)&Y_2&0&0&0\\
0&-u^2&-u^2&2u&0&0&0\\
0&-u\varepsilon_1/\rho&-u\varepsilon_1/\rho&\varepsilon_1/\rho&u&0&0\\
0&-u\varepsilon_2/\rho&-u\varepsilon_2/\rho&\varepsilon_2/\rho&0&u&0\\
0&-uE/\rho&-uE/\rho&E/\rho&0&0&u
\end{bmatrix}.
\label{eq:Ac}
\end{equation}
It admits the eigenvalues
\begin{equation}
\lambda^c_{1,\dots,7}=u .
\label{eq:eig_adv}
\end{equation}
The $\alpha_1$ row is a non-conservative product; the other rows are conservative, and $\mathbf A^c-u\,\mathbf I$ is nilpotent of
rank one, so one eigenvector is missing and the sub-system is weakly hyperbolic, as the pressureless gas dynamics it
contains. The characteristic speed is the flow velocity alone. The volume fraction is transported only by this
sub-system; in the pressure--relaxation sub-system it changes only through the relaxation terms.
\item The pressure--relaxation sub-system is
\begin{equation}
\partial_t\mathbf Q+\partial_x\mathbf F^p(\mathbf Q)+\mathbf B^p(\mathbf Q)\,\partial_xu=\mathbf S(\mathbf Q),
\label{eq:pressure_sub}
\end{equation}
with the relaxation terms $\mathbf S$ of \eqref{eq:general}. Without sources the volume fractions and the partial densities are
constant, so each phase keeps its density and is heated by its pressure work,
$\partial_tp_k=-\Gamma_kp_k\,\partial_xu$ with the Gr\"uneisen coefficient $\Gamma_k=\rho_k^{-1}\partial p_k/\partial e_k|_{\rho_k}$, and its Jacobian admits the eigenvalues
\begin{equation}
\lambda^p_{1,\dots,5}=0,\qquad \lambda^p_{6,7}=\pm a,\qquad a^2=\sum_kY_k\,\frac{\Gamma_kp_k}{\rho_k} .
\label{eq:eig_p}
\end{equation}
The remaining part of the sound speed, $\sum_kY_k\,\partial p_k/\partial\rho_k|_{e_k}$, comes from the compression of the phases by the mass
fluxes of the advective sub-system; the two parts add up to the frozen sound speed, $a^2+\sum_kY_k\,\partial p_k/\partial\rho_k|_{e_k}=c_f^2$. The
speed $a$ is real for non-negative phase pressures, and small in a liquid at moderate pressure; the sub-system is
therefore never solved on its own, but together with the incremental advective fluxes described below, so that the
implicit step carries the whole frozen sound speed $c_f$.
\end{enumerate}
In the low Mach number regime the acoustic coupling of the two sub-systems is fast compared with the transport, which
introduces severe stiffness associated with the pressure terms, in addition to the stiffness of the relaxation rates.
The advective sub-system is therefore treated explicitly, with a numerical dissipation proportional to the flow velocity,
and the pressure--relaxation sub-system implicitly. So that the implicit step contains the whole acoustic coupling,
the conservative advective fluxes are evaluated with the velocity of the new time level, written as the velocity of the
explicit state $u^e$ plus an increment, $Xu^{n+1}=Xu^e+X(u^{n+1}-u^e)$ for $X\in\{m_1,m_2,\rho u,\varepsilon_1,\varepsilon_2,E\}$: the first part is
the explicit advective flux, and the incremental flux $X(u^{n+1}-u^e)$, which depends on the unknown velocity, is solved together
with the pressure--relaxation sub-system (Sec.~\ref{sec:implicit}). The volume fraction has no flux and no increment: its advection
is completed in the explicit step. Since only the advective eigenvalues enter the explicit stability condition, the time
step depends on the flow velocity alone, independently of the sound speed and of the relaxation rates.
