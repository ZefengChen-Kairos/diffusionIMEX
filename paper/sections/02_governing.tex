\section{System of governing equations}
\label{sec:governing}

We consider the single-velocity six-equation two-phase model of Saurel, Petitpas and Berry \cite{SPB2009},
augmented with the heat- and mass-transfer terms of Pelanti and Shyue \cite{PelantiShyue2014} and Pelanti
\cite{Pelanti2022}. For phases $k=1,2$ let $\alpha_k$ be the volume fraction ($\alpha_1+\alpha_2=1$), $\rho_k$ the density,
$m_k=\alpha_k\rho_k$ the partial density, $e_k$ the specific internal energy and $\varepsilon_k=\alpha_k\rho_ke_k$ the phase internal energy
per unit mixture volume; $p_k$, $T_k$, $c_k$, $g_k$ are the phase pressure, temperature, sound speed and Gibbs free
energy. The mixture density is $\rho=m_1+m_2$, the velocity $\mathbf u=(u,v)$, the mixture pressure $\bar p=\alpha_1p_1+\alpha_2p_2$ and
the total energy $E=\varepsilon_1+\varepsilon_2+\tfrac12\rho|\mathbf u|^2$. The model reads
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
&\partial_t\alpha_1+\mathbf u\cdot\nabla\alpha_1=\mathcal P+\mathcal M/\rho_I,\\
&\partial_t m_1+\nabla\cdot(m_1\mathbf u)=\mathcal M,\qquad \partial_t m_2+\nabla\cdot(m_2\mathbf u)=-\mathcal M,\\
&\partial_t(\rho\mathbf u)+\nabla\cdot(\rho\mathbf u\otimes\mathbf u+\bar p\,\mathbf I)=\mathbf 0,\\
&\partial_t\varepsilon_1+\nabla\cdot(\varepsilon_1\mathbf u)+\alpha_1p_1\,\nabla\cdot\mathbf u=-p_I\mathcal P+\mathcal Q+e_I\mathcal M,\\
&\partial_t\varepsilon_2+\nabla\cdot(\varepsilon_2\mathbf u)+\alpha_2p_2\,\nabla\cdot\mathbf u=+p_I\mathcal P-\mathcal Q-e_I\mathcal M,\\
&\partial_t E+\nabla\cdot\big((E+\bar p)\mathbf u\big)=0 .
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:model}
\end{equation}
The first equation evolves the volume fraction, the next two the mass of each phase, the fourth the mixture momentum,
the next two the internal energy of each phase, with the pressure work of the phase, and the last the mixture total
energy. The right-hand sides are the mechanical, thermal and chemical relaxation terms
\begin{equation}
\mathcal P=\mu(p_1-p_2),\qquad \mathcal Q=\theta(T_2-T_1),\qquad \mathcal M=\nu(g_2-g_1),\qquad
p_I=\frac{Z_2p_1+Z_1p_2}{Z_1+Z_2},\quad Z_k=\rho_kc_k .
\label{eq:sources}
\end{equation}
The three terms drive the phases towards equilibrium:
\begin{itemize}
\item mechanical relaxation $\mathcal P$: the volume fraction changes, with the associated interfacial work, until $p_1=p_2$;
\item thermal relaxation $\mathcal Q$: interphase heat exchange, which drives $T_1=T_2$;
\item chemical relaxation $\mathcal M$: mass transfer by phase change (evaporation or condensation, $\mathcal M>0$
from phase 2 to phase 1), which drives $g_1=g_2$.
\end{itemize}
The rates $\mu,\theta,\nu\ge0$ are inverse relaxation times and may be stiff. The total-energy equation is redundant for
smooth solutions, but it is the conservative one and fixes the mixture internal energy at shocks (the ``+1'' equation
of \cite{SPB2009}). The interfacial density $\rho_I$ and energy $e_I$ cancel in every closure used here and need not be
specified. Each phase is closed by an equation of state $p_k=p_k(\rho_k,e_k)$; we consider the Noble--Abel
stiffened-gas law, for which the internal energy is affine in the pressure (Sec.~\ref{sec:nasg}), and Mie--Gr\"uneisen
laws with nonlinear reference curves (Sec.~\ref{sec:mg}).

For one-dimensional flows along $x$, \eqref{eq:model} can be written in the non-conservative form
\begin{equation}
\partial_t\mathbf Q+\partial_x\mathbf F(\mathbf Q)+\mathbf B(\mathbf Q)\,\partial_x\mathbf Q=\mathbf S(\mathbf Q),\qquad
\mathbf Q=(\alpha_1,m_1,m_2,\rho u,\varepsilon_1,\varepsilon_2,E)^{\top},
\label{eq:general}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf F$ collects the fluxes, $\mathbf B$ the products $u\,\partial_x\alpha_1$ and $\alpha_kp_k\,\partial_xu$, and $\mathbf S$ the relaxation
terms. Its eigenvalues are $u$ and $u\pm c_f$, with the frozen sound speed
\begin{equation}
c_f^2=\sum_kY_kc_k^2,\qquad Y_k=m_k/\rho ,
\label{eq:cf}
\end{equation}
so that a fully explicit scheme is restricted by
\begin{equation}
\Delta t\le\mathrm{CFL}\,\min_\Omega\frac{\Delta x}{|u|+c_f},
\label{eq:cfl_explicit}
\end{equation}
which becomes prohibitive when the flow is slow compared with the sound speed, while the relaxation terms add their own
stiffness.

\paragraph{Relaxed models}
For $\mu\to\infty$ the model reduces to the five-equation pressure-equilibrium model of Kapila et al.\ \cite{Kapila2001};
adding $\theta\to\infty$ gives the four-equation $pT$ model and adding $\nu\to\infty$ the homogeneous equilibrium model ($pTg$)
\cite{Pelanti2022}. Their sound speeds satisfy the subcharacteristic ordering \cite{FlattenLund2011}
\begin{equation}
c_{pTg}\le c_{pT}\le c_p\le c_f,\qquad \frac1{\rho c_p^2}=\sum_k\frac{\alpha_k}{\rho_kc_k^2} .
\label{eq:subchar}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Noble--Abel stiffened-gas equation of state}
\label{sec:nasg}
The Noble--Abel stiffened-gas (NASG) law \cite{LeMetayerSaurel2016} has the parameters $(\gamma,\pi,q,q',c_v,b)$,
\begin{equation}
p(\rho,e)=\frac{(\gamma-1)\rho(e-q)}{1-\rho b}-\gamma\pi,\qquad
v(p,T)=\frac{(\gamma-1)c_vT}{p+\pi}+b,\qquad e(p,T)=c_vT\,\frac{p+\gamma\pi}{p+\pi}+q,
\label{eq:nasg}
\end{equation}
with the Gibbs free energy $g=(\gamma c_v-q')T-c_vT\ln\big(T^\gamma/(p+\pi)^{\gamma-1}\big)+bp+q$ and the sound speed
$c^2=\gamma(p+\pi)/\big(\rho(1-\rho b)\big)$; $b=0$ gives the stiffened gas and $b=\pi=q=0$ the ideal gas. The form used by the
scheme is the internal energy of phase $k$ in a cell, where it occupies the volume $\alpha_k$ with mass $m_k$, at pressure $p$,
\begin{equation}
\varepsilon_k(\alpha_k,m_k,p)=\frac{(p+\gamma_k\pi_k)(\alpha_k-m_kb_k)}{\gamma_k-1}+m_kq_k ,
\label{eq:eps_nasg}
\end{equation}
which is affine in $(\alpha_k,m_k)$ at fixed $p$, affine in $p$ at fixed $(\alpha_k,m_k)$, and explicitly invertible for $\alpha_k$. The
admissible set of a phase, $\{\alpha>0,\ m>0,\ \alpha-mb>0,\ \varepsilon-mq-(\alpha-mb)\pi>0\}$, is a convex cone. The saturation
temperature $T_{\rm sat}(p)$ solves $g_1(p,T)=g_2(p,T)$.

\subsection{Mie--Gr\"uneisen equations of state}
\label{sec:mg}
Detonation products and liquid nitromethane are described by Mie--Gr\"uneisen laws
\begin{equation}
p=\Gamma\rho\big(e-\varepsilon_r(\rho)\big)+p_r(\rho),\qquad c^2=\frac{(\Gamma+1)(p-p_r)}{\rho}+p_r'(\rho),
\label{eq:mg}
\end{equation}
with constant $\Gamma$, whose reference curve is an isentrope, $\varepsilon_r'=p_r/\rho^2$. We use the Cochran--Chan law
\cite{CochranChan1979} and the JWL law \cite{LeeHornigKury1968},
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
&\text{Cochran--Chan:}\quad p_r(\rho)=A_1x^{E_1}-A_2x^{E_2},\qquad
\varepsilon_r(\rho)=\frac1\rho\Big(\frac{A_1x^{E_1}}{E_1-1}-\frac{A_2x^{E_2}}{E_2-1}\Big),\qquad x=\frac\rho{\rho_0},\\
&\text{JWL:}\quad p_r(\rho)=Ae^{-R_1\rho_0/\rho}+Be^{-R_2\rho_0/\rho},\qquad
\varepsilon_r(\rho)=\frac{A}{R_1\rho_0}e^{-R_1\rho_0/\rho}+\frac{B}{R_2\rho_0}e^{-R_2\rho_0/\rho}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:mg_ref}
\end{equation}
In phase-volume form $\varepsilon_k=m_k\varepsilon_r(m_k/\alpha_k)+\alpha_k\big(p-p_r(m_k/\alpha_k)\big)/\Gamma_k$, which is nonlinear in $\alpha_k$; admissibility
is $c^2>0$.

\subsection{Split form of the model}
\label{sec:split}
To treat the convective and the acoustic time scales differently we split the system in the manner of Toro and
V\'azquez-Cend\'on \cite{ToroVazquez2012}: transport by the flow velocity is separated from the pressure terms. Writing
the volume-fraction equation in volume-transport form, $\partial_t\alpha_k+u\,\partial_x\alpha_k=\partial_t\alpha_k+\partial_x(\alpha_ku)-\alpha_k\partial_xu$,
the system for $\mathbf Q=(\alpha_1,m_1,m_2,\rho u,\varepsilon_1,\varepsilon_2,E)^{\top}$ is the sum of two sub-systems.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The \emph{advective sub-system} transports every quantity with the flow velocity,
\begin{equation}
\partial_tX+\partial_x(Xu)=0,\qquad X\in\{\alpha_k,m_k,\varepsilon_k,\rho u,E\}.
\label{eq:advective}
\end{equation}
Its eigenvalues are all equal to $u$: $\alpha_k$, $m_k$, $\varepsilon_k$ and $E$ are linearly transported, and $(\rho,\rho u)$ form the
pressureless gas system, whose eigenvalue $u$ is double with a single eigenvector. The sub-system is weakly hyperbolic,
and its characteristic speed is the flow velocity alone.
\item The \emph{pressure--relaxation sub-system} contains the pressure, the volume strain, the pressure work and all
relaxation terms,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
&\partial_t\alpha_1-\alpha_1\partial_xu=\mathcal P+\mathcal M/\rho_I,\qquad \partial_tm_1=\mathcal M,\qquad \partial_tm_2=-\mathcal M,\\
&\partial_t(\rho u)+\partial_x\bar p=0,\qquad \partial_t\varepsilon_k+\alpha_kp_k\partial_xu=\mathcal S_k,\qquad \partial_tE+\partial_x(\bar pu)=0,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:pressure_sub}
\end{equation}
with $\mathcal S_k$ the right-hand sides of the phase energy equations in \eqref{eq:model}. It carries the acoustic waves and
the relaxation.
\end{enumerate}
In the low Mach number regime the sound speed is much larger than $|u|$, so the acoustic stiffness of the model resides
in the pressure--relaxation sub-system, together with the stiffness of the relaxation rates. The advective
sub-system is therefore discretised explicitly, with a numerical dissipation proportional to the flow velocity, and
the pressure--relaxation sub-system implicitly. In the time discretisation the flux of every transported quantity is
written as $Xu^{n+1}=Xu^e+X(u^{n+1}-u^e)$, $u^e$ the velocity of the explicit state: the first part is the advective flux,
the second, the incremental flux, is treated implicitly with the pressure--relaxation sub-system (Sec.~\ref{sec:implicit}).
Since only the advective eigenvalues enter the explicit stability condition, the time step depends on the flow velocity
alone, independently of the sound speed and of the relaxation rates.
